Paper's own research question / purpose: "We introduce a Markov-model-based framework for Moving Target Defense (MTD) analysis" that "provides general theorems about how the probability of a successful adversary defeating an MTD strategy is related to the amount of time/cost spent by the adversary" and defines "the concept of **security capacity** which measures the strength or effectiveness of an MTD strategy: the security capacity depends on MTD specific parameters and more general system parameters" (line 11). The stated motive is a complaint about the alternative: "most research only focuses on introducing a new MTD scheme … and then using a simulation based approach in order to gauge how effective the scheme can be" (line 37), and "One of the problems with simulation based MTD evaluation is that these techniques are **case-dependent** meaning that they only stand for their specific introduced example" (line 57).

**The headline finding for this survey: there is no empirical evaluation and no parameter sweep at all.** The paper is a theory paper whose "results" are closed-form expressions; parameter dependence is read *off the algebra*, not off a plot. This is the deliberate methodological opposite of the sweep convention, and the paper argues for it explicitly (line 57, line 65).

## B. Setup — what is declared before any result

- Network configuration(s): none, and this is the framework's selling point. The state space is deliberately *compressed*: "we realize that the first approach of a Markov model where each state represents a complete system configuration is useful if we **compress the Markov model to one where states only represent the projection to necessary system and MTD parameters**" (line 69), with the reason repeated in footnote 2, "We avoid unnecessary state explosion by representing states by system and MTD parameters" (line 123). The complaint about the alternative is directed at Zhuang: "the number of states in the Markov model grows exponentially in the number of machines in the system. This attempt being too complex …" (line 67). The only structural quantities are `N` (number of IP addresses / locations) and `K`, `k` (targets hidden, targets needed) — symbols, never given a value except in the toy example.
- Attacker model / scenarios: two scenarios, named once and used as the mirrored subsection headings throughout — **single-target hiding** and **multiple-target hiding** (line 139). The optimality class of the adversary is declared for each, in the same sentence: "Our analysis of single-target hiding assumes an **optimal adaptive adversary** while our analysis of multiple-target hiding assumes an **almost optimal adaptive adversary** (being almost optimal is shown in section 5)" (line 139) — i.e. the paper forward-references where it will discharge the "almost".
  The attacker's strategy is *derived*, not assumed: the round-robin probing argument at line 150 ("if he tries each IP address in sequential round robin order, then he knows his next guess will never be of an IP address he already tried since the defender's last IP address randomization"), and the no-waiting argument at line 189 ("To reduce this unnecessary probability of losing a location while waiting, the attacker should not wait in the first place. This means an optimal adaptive adversarial strategy is to issue moves at every time step as much as the system allows access").
  **Every simplification is made in the attacker's favour and flagged as such** — this is the paper's signature move:
  - "we give the adversary more detailed information (for free): we assume that as soon as the defender re-allocates a target which location before re-allocation was known to the attacker, the attacker is told that this location does not any more correspond to one of the _K_ targets" (line 180);
  - "we give the adversary the advantage to initially start in a state drawn from the stationary distribution _π_ rather than starting in the 0 state which represents the worst starting state for the adversary" (line 205);
  - "we slightly modify the composition game by giving the adversary **two extra advantages**" (line 287), each then itemised;
  - and where an advantage is unrealistic, the paper says so rather than banking it: "This is an advantage for the adversary which **does not happen in practice**. In practice the defender does not know the states of the various games and it will continue playing its MTD strategy as always" (line 312).
  The scope of the metric is fixed and defended in a footnote: "we measure the effectiveness of an MTD strategy by analyzing **when the attacker will be in a winning state for the first time**" (line 97), with footnote 1 naming the two questions this leaves out ("how long an attacker will be in a winning state once it arrives at a winning state" and "when the attacker will again be in a winning state once he exits") and stating why they are deferred (line 99).
- Defence conditions: one concrete mechanism (IP hopping / IP address randomization, line 133–135) instantiated in two scenarios, plus the abstract composition **Layered MTD / MTD\*** (line 271). The defender is restricted, and the restriction declared: "In our analysis we only consider a defender who plays a **memoryless** strategy: at every time step, the defender plays (issues a move) with probability _λ_ regardless any learned information about the attacker's strategy" (line 176). Move arbitration is declared as a rule with a system justification: "**[Defender moves]** _If the defender issues a move, it will take priority over any other player's request_" (line 174), and the resulting rate relation is derived rather than assumed — "if the defender plays memoryless with probability _λ_, then either the defender moves with probability _λ_, or the attacker moves with probability **_µ_ = min{_r_, 1 − _λ_}**, or with probability 1 − _λ_ − _µ_ no one moves" (line 189). No-defence baseline: none — a defenceless system is not a point in this parameter space (λ = 0 is never evaluated).
- Parameter table: **no**. There is no table anywhere in the paper. Parameters are introduced inline at their point of first use, each with a one-clause gloss:
  - `λ`, `µ` — "parameters _λ_ and _µ_ satisfying 0 ≤ _λ_ + _µ_ ≤ 1 which represent the **rate of play** of the defender and attacker, respectively" (Definition 3, line 113);
  - `λ` glossed against the literature's term in related work — "the 'diversity rate', defined as the time interval between each system adaptation (in our terminology the rate _λ_ at which the defender moves in his MTD strategy)" (line 63);
  - `N` — "the total number of possible IP addresses" (line 152) / "the total number of 'locations'" (line 170);
  - `K`, `k` — "_K_ targets (i.e., attack vectors or vulnerabilities) may hide; only if the combination of a smaller subset of _k_ locations of these _K_ target attack vectors are known by the attacker, the attacker is capable of launching a successful attack" (line 170);
  - `r` — "a system which limits the rate _r_ at which a party is allowed to access the system (in more detail: whenever a party requests to access the system, the request is put in a FIFO queue and the system will serve requests according to a **memoryless Poisson process** corresponding to _r_)" (line 189);
  - `T` — the time horizon in logical time steps (Definition 2, line 103);
  - `α` — "expected adversarial cost per logical time slot" (Theorem 2, line 227); `C` — a cost budget;
  - `t_j` — "the time _t_j per logical transition at level _j_ in a universal time unit (e.g. seconds) such that _t_j ≥ 1 for all _j_" (line 353);
  - `c` = security capacity, `s`, `t` with the split rule "The capacity _c_ = _s_ + _t_ is split towards 'a probability 2^−s of successful attack' and 'a considered time frame of 2^t logical time steps'" (line 221).
  Distributions: memoryless/geometric throughout (defender move each step w.p. λ; system access a Poisson process at rate r, line 189; the level-exit process in the composition proof is "a Poisson process with parameter _λ_j+1", line 335). Every transition-matrix entry of the single-target chain is given individually with its probabilistic reading (lines 154–162); the multiple-target entries are derived in prose at line 191.
- Replications: not applicable to the theory. For the one simulation (Figure 4), the number of runs behind "the average (simulated) time to win" (line 343) is **not stated**; nor is the RNG, the tool, or the stopping rule. The sample run itself is dated only by its outcome: "after 439 time steps the attacker wins" (line 303).
- Statistics: none. No CIs, no variance, no significance tests. The only aggregate is an unqualified "average" over an unstated number of runs (lines 343, 349).
- Anything else declared: an algorithmic sketch of the game simulator in pseudocode (Algorithm 1, lines 85–95) with each variable glossed at line 83 — declared as *how M can be simulated*, not as a tool that was run. A pointer to an off-the-shelf method for the hard step: "there exists a promising algorithm [16] which answers efficiently (with no dependence on the size of the Markov chain) … whether a chosen state in a Markov chain has stationary probability less than ∆" (line 203). No hardware, no software, no versions.

- Order of results: general theorem first (Theorem 1, line 201), then the definition it licenses (security capacity, line 223), then the cost generalisation (Theorem 2, line 227), then the two worked instantiations in the same order as section 4 (5.1 single, 5.2 multiple), then the composition theorem, then its toy illustration. The order follows the framework's logical dependency, not any empirical narrative.
- Every figure:

  - **Figure 2** "Markov chain of Multiple-Target Hiding" (caption line 187; OCR at line 185) — same kind, states 0, 1, …, i, i+1, …, Win, edges µK/N, µ(K−i)/(N−i), λ(i+1)/K, 1−λ.
  - **Figure 3** "An example of a composed game made of 3 similar levels" (caption line 310; OCR at line 308) — three stacked 3-state chains M0, M1, M2 with **numeric** probabilities 0.8 / 0.2 / 0.25 / 0.625 / 0.125 / 0.5 / 0.5. The paper's first and only numbers on a diagram.
  - **Figure 4** "Simulation of a sample run of the modified composed game example" (caption line 331; OCR at line 329) — three stacked **time-series step plots**, one per level, x = Time 0–450, y = States 0/1/2, titled `M2−MTD(5,2,0.5,0.5)`, `M1−MTD(5,2,0.5,0.5)`, `M0−MTD(5,2,0.5,0.5)`. This is the only chart in the paper with an x and a y, and its x-axis is *time*, not a parameter.
  - **Figure 5** (appendix, line 476) — a cost-transformation path diagram; OCR is unreadable garbage. Its content is described in prose at line 469.
  - No plot anywhere has a swept parameter on any axis. No error bars, no series-over-parameter-levels.
- Every table: **none**. The paper contains no tables of any kind.
- How the baseline appears: there is no no-defence baseline. The comparative baselines are internal and structural: (i) the single-target formula is the baseline against which the multiple-target formula is checked at K = k = 1 (line 259); (ii) the weakened composition game (M0,…,Mf) is the baseline against which the real Layered MTD is compared (line 343); (iii) the theoretical bound is the baseline against which the simulation is compared (footnote 8, line 349).
- How comparisons are stated in prose: **as asymptotic/scaling statements about symbols**, with an interpretation attached, never as percentages:
  - "In practice the security capacity −log _π_(_N_) ≈ log(_λN/µ_) is **rather small** as _N_ is restricted to the number of unused addresses in the network subnet" (line 251) — a *negative* result about the mechanism, stated plainly.
  - "The security capacity −log _π_(_k_) ≥ _k_(log(_λ_(_N_−_k_+1)) − log(_µK_(_K_−_k_+1))) **scales linearly with _k_** showing that multiple-target hiding is a **good MTD design principle**" (line 261) — scaling claim → design recommendation, in one sentence.
  - "the security capacity is the largest if an adversary needs to find multiple targets (and not just one single target) … The analysis shows that the probability of finding each target scales with the **product** of the probabilities of finding each specific target. This **exponential dependency** on the number of hidden targets is also demonstrated by the analysis of Layered MTD strategies (in our example, the number of hidden targets is equal to the number of levels)" (line 369).
  - A negative design recommendation derived from the *shape* of the bound rather than from any measurement: "if the defender is able to detect whether the adversary enters a state _k_j … the upper bound can be improved by discarding the factors _λ_j. However, this **does not change the asymptotic behavior** of the upper bound and therefore **extra investment in such advanced detection will likely not pay off**" (line 345).
  - The one numeric comparison: "In the example of Figure 4 the average (simulated) time to win the (_M_0, _M_1, _M_2)-MTD game is **471.8** time steps while the average time to win the corresponding layered MTD game is **14257** timesteps" (line 343).

## D. Narrative — the claims, in order

1. Framework generality: "Our framework is general in that it formally defines (and can be used to analyze) a **large class** of MTD strategies as games based on Markov chains" (line 41), and Lemma 1, "Each MTD game can be described as a _M_-MTD game for some transition matrix _M_" (line 107).
2. The main bound: "the probability that the attacker wins in the first _T_ time steps is ≤ _T_ · _π_(_k_)" (Theorem 1, line 201), with the tightness of the bound argued in a footnote rather than measured — "the upper bound is close to the actual probability for _T_ · _π_(_k_) ≪ 1 since (1) the assumed stationary distribution has its probability mass at and around the initial 0 state for well designed MTD strategies …" (footnote 5, line 235).
3. The metric definition it licenses: security capacity (line 221, Definition 4 at line 223), plus the honest caveat "the security capacity will not be much larger than −log _π_(_k_)" (footnote 6, line 237).
4. Cost conversion: Theorem 2 (line 227) turning logical time steps into an adversarial money/time budget, and Corollary 1 (line 359) turning them into wall-clock, with an explicit penalty term — "the security capacity of the layered MTD game measured in time units is at least (7) **minus a time conversion factor** log Π _t_j" (line 361).
5. Applicability claim, hedged with the real bottleneck named: "even if the Markov chain of an MTD game remains complex/large … our theory can be used for security analysis of the MTD game. _**The main challenge is to find an appropriate Markov chain in the first place.**_" (line 203, italicised in the original).
6. Instance claim 1 (negative): single-target IP hopping has small security capacity, bounded by the address pool (line 251).
7. Instance claim 2 (positive): multiple-target hiding scales linearly in _k_ (line 261).
8. Self-check by degenerate case: "Notice that _K_ = _k_ = 1, which describes single-target hiding, has the upper bound _µ_/(_λN_) which is close to (6) implying (by extrapolation) that **the suboptimal adversary considered in the multiple-target hiding game is close to optimal**" (line 259) — the "almost optimal" promised at line 139 is discharged here.
9. Composition claim, immediately followed by an admission of looseness backed by the simulation: "Since an (_M_0, …, _M_f)-MTD game assumes a much weaker defender than the corresponding layered MTD game, the layered MTD game may have a security capacity which is **significantly larger** than (7) … Theorem 3 is a first analysis of layered MTD games and **future research is needed** to understand the exact effect of such composition" (line 343).
10. Theory-vs-simulation reconciliation, in a footnote and to a stated tolerance: "The simulation results fit **up to a constant factor** the theoretical prediction as the average time to win an individual game _M_i is 24.3 and is approximately twice 2^3.32 and the average time to win the (_M_0, _M_1, _M_2)-MTD game is 471.8 and is approximately twice 2^7.97" (footnote 8, line 349).

Claims about the ATTACKER from the data: the paper's claims about the attacker are all *a priori* — derived optimality arguments (round robin, line 150; never wait, line 189) — never inferred from output. The APT framing is asserted as a design motive for the composition, not as a finding: "The main idea is that the attacker must slowly penetrate the defense mechanism **as in an Advanced Persistent Threat (APT)**" (line 271). The attacker's realism is handled entirely by the give-the-adversary-the-advantage convention (see B), so that every claim reads as a guarantee against *any* attacker in the modelled class rather than a measurement of one.

## E. Sensitivity / parameter analysis

**No sweep is present.** No parameter is varied over a range anywhere in the paper; nothing is plotted against a parameter; there is no sensitivity section, no appendix sweep, and no figure with a parameter axis. What the paper does instead, in place of a sweep:

- **Symbolic parameter analysis.** The closed-form stationary distributions are the results, and parameter dependence is stated by reading the formula. Three such readings, and they are the paper's entire "results" content:
  - `−log π(N) ≈ log(λN/µ)` for single-target hiding, with the interpretation "rather small as _N_ is restricted to the number of unused addresses in the network subnet" (line 251) — a range constraint on `N` justified by the real deployment, not by a chosen sweep interval.
  - `−log π(k) ≥ k(log(λ(N−k+1)) − log(µK(K−k+1)))` for multiple-target hiding, "**scales linearly with _k_**" (line 261).
  - The composition bound (7), whose parameter reading is "exponential dependency on the number of hidden targets" / number of levels (line 369) and whose `λ_j` factors are argued to be asymptotically irrelevant (line 345).
  All three are *analytic* statements about how the output moves with a parameter — the same knowledge a sweep would produce, obtained by algebra and stated in one sentence each.
- **Degenerate-case cross-check in place of validation.** The one-target special case of the more general formula is compared with the independently derived single-target formula and their closeness is used to certify the suboptimal adversary as near-optimal (line 259). This is the paper's substitute for cross-validating two models.
- **Simulation used only as a looseness probe, at a single point.** The only numeric parameter setting in the paper is the toy composed game, and it appears **only in the figure titles**, in a positional notation never defined in the text: `M2−MTD(5,2,0.5,0.5)`, `M1−MTD(5,2,0.5,0.5)`, `M0−MTD(5,2,0.5,0.5)` (Figure 4 axis text, line 329). Read against the multiple-target transition rules at line 191, the four slots are (N = 5 locations, K = 2 targets, λ = 0.5, µ = 0.5), which reproduces exactly the numbers printed on Figure 3 (line 308): M_{0,1} = µK/N = 0.2, M_{1,2} = µ(K−1)/(N−1) = 0.125, M_{1,0} = λ/K = 0.25, M_{2,1} = λ·2/K = 0.5, self-loops 0.8 / 0.625 / 0.5. Three levels, f = 2. The paper states the derived quantity for this point: "The security capacity of an individual game _M_i is at least −log _π_i(2) = −log 0.1 = 3.32 and (7) gives at least a security capacity of 3 · 3.32 + log 0.25 = **7.97**" (footnote 8, line 349).
  - Range justification for these values: **none whatsoever** — no citation, no rationale; the setting is introduced only as "the **toy example** of Figure 3" (line 303) and "a sequence of (in our example equivalent) games _M_0, _M_1, and _M_2 (with similar time scales)" (line 303). It exists "In order to get a feeling for how a (_M_0, …, _M_f)-MTD game behaves" (line 303).
  - Design: a single point, not a sweep. One sample run is plotted (Fig. 4) and two averages are reported (471.8 and 14257, line 343; 24.3, line 349) over an unstated number of runs.
  - Claim supported: only that the proven bound is loose — the composed-vs-layered gap of 471.8 vs 14257 (line 343) — and that theory and simulation agree "up to a constant factor" (line 349).
- **Time-scale handling in place of a time-step sweep.** Section 6.1 does symbolically what a sweep over transition rates would do numerically: it re-scales each level's transition matrix by its own `t_j` and proves the stationary distribution is invariant — "the transformation to the new transition matrices does not have any effect on the stationary distribution _π_j" (line 355) — so the whole family of time-scale settings is covered by one corollary rather than by a grid.
- **Range constraints are stated as facts about the deployment, not as chosen bounds.** `N` "is restricted to the number of unused addresses in the network subnet" (line 251); `t_j ≥ 1` by choice of universal unit (line 353); `0 ≤ λ + µ ≤ 1` by construction (line 113); `µ = min{r, 1−λ}` derived from the priority rule (line 189).
- Where a sweep would sit if it existed: the conclusion says so explicitly, as future work — "As a first direction for future research **the security capacity versus system performance can be analyzed as a function of system and MTD parameters**. This allows the defender to understand the price in performance paid for his MTD strategy and **how MTD and system parameters (given system constraints) should be set**" (line 367). That is, the trade-off curve this paper does not draw is named as the next paper.
- Distinctive convention: **parameter analysis by closed form + one degenerate-case check + a single-point simulation used solely to measure the slack in the bound.** The paper's whole methodological argument is that a sweep would be worse — simulation results are "case-dependent meaning that they only stand for their specific introduced example" (line 57) — so a formula that can be read for any parameter setting is presented as strictly stronger than a family of plotted curves.

## F. Discussion / limitations

No discussion section, no limitations section, no threats to validity. Limitations are placed as **footnotes and mid-proof asides at the exact point the assumption is made** — a distinctive placement convention:

- Metric scope, footnote 1: "Additional analysis of _how long an attacker will be in a winning state once it arrives at a winning state_ and _when the attacker will again be in a winning state once he exits a winning state_ is required to understand the effect of an MTD strategy in more detail. Since an adversary reaching a winning state can start launching a successful attack, the authors feel that _when the attacker will be in a winning state for the first time_ is the most important question to analyze" (line 99).
- Existence/uniqueness of the stationary distribution, footnote 3: "This implicitly assumes the existence of a stationary distribution; this distribution may not be unique, however, the theorem applies to any such distribution as long as the other condition of the theorem is met" (line 213).
- What the worst-start assumption rules out, footnote 4: "This excludes a scenario where initially the adversary has a leg up – e.g., because the initial state of the system is a 'default' state where the adversary can assume specific 'default' settings before the defender had the opportunity to move 'targets' around" (line 215).
- Tightness, footnotes 5 and 6: "the upper bound is close to the actual probability for _T_ · _π_(_k_) ≪ 1 …" (line 235); "the security capacity will not be much larger than −log _π_(_k_)" (line 237).
- Where the real difficulty was moved to, in italics in the body: "_The main challenge is to find an appropriate Markov chain in the first place._" (line 203).
- Looseness of the composition bound, in the body and quantified against simulation: "the layered MTD game may have a security capacity which is significantly larger than (7) … **Theorem 3 is a first analysis of layered MTD games and future research is needed** to understand the exact effect of such composition" (line 343), and again in the conclusion, "even though the current proven bound shows an exponential dependency on the number of layers, it is **much weaker when compared to simulation results**" (line 369).
- A modelling artefact disowned as unrealistic: "This is an advantage for the adversary which does not happen in practice. In practice the defender does not know the states of the various games …" (line 312), followed by "The (_M_0, …, _M_f)-MTD game is only introduced for proving Theorem 3" (line 314).
- The framework's own open questions are posed as a rhetorical list at the end of section 3, before any result: "A couple questions concerning the limitations of this abstract definitional framework immediately arise: … How can this logical time be translated to real time …? … How much detail is needed in order to model a complex MTD game …? Finally, how can we measure the security offered by an MTD strategy? **The remainder of this paper answers these questions**" (line 129) — limitations stated as a roadmap.

## G. Transferable vs purpose-specific

Transferable to an honours dissertation evaluating existing MTD mechanisms against a new attacker model on a simulator:

- **Declaring the adversary's optimality class in one sentence, per scenario, with a forward reference to where the claim is discharged** (line 139: "optimal adaptive adversary" / "almost optimal adaptive adversary (being almost optimal is shown in section 5)").
- **The give-the-attacker-the-advantage convention**: every simplification made in the attacker's favour, stated as such at the point it is made ("for free", line 180; "the advantage to initially start in …", line 205; "two extra advantages", line 287), so results read as conservative bounds rather than best-case measurements. Directly usable for a simulator attacker model where fidelity is limited.
- **Disowning an advantage that is unrealistic rather than banking it** (line 312) — and saying what it was introduced for (line 314).
- **Deriving attacker behaviour instead of stipulating it**: the round-robin argument (line 150) and the never-wait argument (line 189) each conclude *why* a rational attacker behaves that way, so the modelled behaviour is defensible rather than arbitrary.
- **Mirrored section headings between model construction and analysis** (4.1/5.1, 4.2/5.2), so a reader can pair each scheme's setup with its result.
- **Naming the parameter in the reader's vocabulary as well as your own** (line 63: "the 'diversity rate' … in our terminology the rate _λ_ at which the defender moves").
- **Placing each limitation as a footnote at the assumption's point of use** (lines 99, 213, 215, 235, 237) rather than in a limitations section — nothing is deferred to a place a reader can skip.
- **The degenerate-case check as cheap validation**: instantiate the general result at the special case for which an independent result exists, and report the closeness (line 259).
- **Stating a negative result about a mechanism as flatly as a positive one** (line 251, single-target IP hopping has "rather small" capacity).
- **Reporting the gap between a conservative model and a realistic one numerically** (471.8 vs 14257, line 343) rather than claiming the conservative number.
- **Range constraints justified by the deployment** ("_N_ is restricted to the number of unused addresses in the network subnet", line 251) rather than by an arbitrary chosen interval.
- Naming the parameter-vs-performance trade-off study as the next step rather than gesturing at it (line 367).

Purpose-specific / venue artefacts:

- **The absence of any sweep, table, or empirical evaluation** is the paper's thesis, not an omission — it argues simulation is "case-dependent" (line 57). A simulator dissertation cannot adopt this stance; it can only borrow the *habit* of stating parameter dependence as a scaling claim alongside its curves.
