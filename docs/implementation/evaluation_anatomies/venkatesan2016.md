## B. Setup — what is declared before any result

- Network configuration(s): **two families, real and synthetic, with different sweep conventions for each** (see E).
  - Real: "real ISP network topologies obtained from the Rocketfuel dataset [20]" (md:275). **Table 1** (md:321–333) declares all ten before any result: columns ASN | Name | No. of routers | No. of ext. routers — Telstra 1221 (2998/329), Sprintlink 1239 (8341/1004), Ebone 1755 (605/310), Verio 2914 (7102/2432), Tiscali 3257 (855/444), Level3 3356 (3447/1827), Exodus 3967 (895/520), VSNL 4755 (121/80), Abovenet 6461 (2720/2066), AT&T 7018 (10152/722). Sizes span 121 to 10,152 routers — declared, though only three are plotted (see F).
  - Synthetic: "simulated medium-scaled ISP networks comprising 3,000 nodes. At the router-level, such networks are known to exhibit **scale-free network properties wherein the degree distribution follows a power-law distribution [20]**. In order to accurately capture the connectivity of an ISP network at the router level, the **BRITE network topology generator [13]** was used to generate these networks. **Ten such networks were considered**, with mission-critical nodes varying between 10%–30% of the network size and **500 C&C locations chosen at random** for each network" (md:338). The generator choice is justified by a cited structural property of the real thing, not asserted.
  - C&C placement in the real networks is declared as a deliberate worst case: "As connections to external routers are outside the monitored domain, in our simulations, we **considered a worst-case scenario and assumed that all the external routers are potentially routing traffic to C&C servers**" (md:336) — which is why Table 1's external-router column exists.
- Attacker model / scenarios: one attacker (a stealthy botmaster exfiltrating from mission-critical nodes), declared as **three explicit assumption bullets** in its own section (md:124–130), each with its justification attached to it:
  1. "The attacker can discover the topology of the network, and is aware of what nodes are mission-critical." — justified by incident reporting: "Reports by Kaspersky labs [11] and Mandiant [12] show that threat actors can infiltrate an organization's network and persist in the system for several years, mapping out the organization and exfiltrating sensitive and valuable intellectual property" (md:126).
  2. Segmented exfiltration, justified by a detection-avoidance mechanism: "Exfiltrating large volumes of data generates abnormally large network flows which in turn may trigger alerts. To avoid detection, the attacker partitions the data to be exfiltrated into _m_ segments _d_1, …, _d_m, and transmits these segments over a temporal span _T_ = ⟨_t_1, …, _t_m⟩ … The attacker is said to have _successfully exfiltrated from a mission-critical node_ **if and only if all the _m_ data segments are exfiltrated by time _t_m**" (md:128) — the success criterion is stated as part of the threat model, not the metric.
  3. Kerckhoffs-style: "The attacker is aware of the detector placement strategy employed by the defender" (md:130), later operationalised as "A rational attacker – who is aware of the defender's strategy – will choose a path which minimizes the probability of detection" (md:171), which is what makes the reported metric a *minimum* (lower bound) rather than an average.
  The attacker's probing capability is grounded in cited technique rather than assumed: "the attacker may progressively learn the location of these detectors through probing … a simple probing strategy could be to send malicious packets to a node suspected of hosting a detector … In an ISP network, an attacker can leverage probing strategies described by Shinoda et al. [17], and by Shmatikov and Wang [18]" (md:217).
  **Attacker fidelity is simplified in one place, and the simplification is argued for rather than waved through**: "existing detection mechanisms suffer from false positives and false negatives … However, for the purpose of our analysis, **the accuracy of detectors is not as critical as their placement**. As mentioned earlier, a prudent attacker – who does not want any portion of the exfiltration traffic to be detected … – will opt for creating more bots in order to establish a detector-free path, rather than having the traffic routed through detectors, **irrespective of their false positive and false negative rate**. Based on these considerations … we ignore the accuracy of detectors and assume that any exfiltration attempt going through a detector node is detected. Factoring the probability of missed detections in our model is relatively straightforward, and we plan to do so as part of our future work" (md:108). Similarly, shortest-path routing is assumed but the assumption is bounded: "our approach does **not** rely on the assumption that traffic is routed along the shortest path, but rather on the more general assumption that **we can predict what paths the algorithm/policy will select** … However, for the sake of presentation, and without limiting the generality of our approach, we do assume that the networks being studied implement a shortest path routing algorithm" (md:61).
- Defence conditions: **four strategies, presented as a chain in which each is motivated by the named deficiency of its predecessor** — a distinctive ordering convention:
  1. _Static Iterative Centrality Placement_ (md:227) — the prior work's best [25], serving as the **static baseline**. Its deficiency is stated before the next strategy: "a persistent attacker can gather enough information to precompute the exfiltration surface of the target system and identify a detector-free path" (md:116).
  2. _Uniform Random Placement_ (md:231) — introduced as: "The static nature of the above strategy enables an attacker to pre-compute the location of detectors … Therefore, in order to create uncertainty about the exact location of detectors, in this strategy, the defender chooses _k_ nodes to place detectors uniformly at random." Serves as the **randomisation baseline** and, in §8.2, as the *normalising reference* for entropy.
  3. _Centrality-Weighted Placement_ (CWS) (md:233, 267) — introduced as: "Although the uniform random strategy introduces uncertainty for an attacker, it may consider nodes that do not lie on any simple path between mission-critical nodes and C&C. As a result, the uniform strategy may provide a low minimum detection probability."
  4. _Expanded Centrality-Weighted Placement_ (ECWS) (md:269) — introduced as: "One of the major drawbacks of the centrality-weighted strategy is that coverage of the exfiltration surface is limited. In fact, the strategy considers only the nodes on the set of all shortest paths … Therefore … the coverage of the exfiltration surface is expanded by considering all the nodes in the paths that are **_δ_-times longer than the shortest paths**."
  The defender's resource constraint is declared and given a real-world reading: "we consider a resource-constrained setting where the defender can only deploy _k_ detectors. **In practice, an upper bound on the number of detectors can be determined by considering the number of systems in the network that can perform detection tasks without impacting the performance of other applications/services running on them.** A dual problem that can be defined is that of minimizing the number of detectors needed to satisfy predefined security requirements, but we do not discuss this problem for reasons of space" (md:134) — the dual formulation is named and explicitly deferred.
- Parameter table: **no**. Table 1 is a topology inventory, not a parameter table. Parameters are declared inline: `k` detectors (md:134), `m` data segments and span `T` (md:128), `δ` path-length slack (md:269), mission-critical fraction (md:338), C&C count (md:338), network size (pdf:44). Distributions: the defender's placement is an explicit probability distribution over temporal placements (Definitions 5–6, md:142–146) sampled "without replacement" (md:169); C&C locations and mission-critical nodes "chosen at random" (md:338) with the distribution unstated (presumably uniform — **not stated**).
- Replications: declared per experiment family and non-uniformly.
  - Synthetic detection/entropy: "**Ten such networks were considered**" (md:338), and the aggregation rule is given with its justification: "We observed that variations in the detection probability for different synthetic networks were **less than 1%** and hence, for sake of presentation, we only show mean values" (pdf:44).
  - Running time: "For each network size, we generated **10 ISP-type topologies**, with 10% of network size as mission-critical nodes and 500 C&C locations chosen at random … **The runtime was averaged over the 10 graph settings for each network size**" (pdf:44).
  - Real topologies: replication count **not stated** (the strategies are stochastic, so some replication must have occurred; the number is not given).
  - Horizon `m` (number of data segments / time points): **not stated** for any experiment.
- Statistics: **the only paper in this batch to report significance testing.** "In all our simulations, we set _δ_ = 0.5 for the expanded centrality-weighted strategy and **tested the statistical significance of the presented results using paired _t_-test at 95% confidence interval**" (pdf:44). No error bars are described; dispersion is handled by the "<1% variation, so means only" statement (pdf:44). The `R²` of a fit is reported on the chart itself (Fig. 7, `R² = 0.9968`, pdf:45) and again in prose ("with an _R_² value of 0.99", pdf:45).
- Anything else declared: hardware, in the running-time subsection only — "All experiments were conducted on an **AMD Opteron processor with 4GB memory running Ubuntu 12.04**" (pdf:44). Algorithm 1 is given in full pseudocode with typed Input/Output (md:191–209) and its complexity derived line-by-line (md:185–187). Implementation language and code availability: **not stated**.

## C. Results — how the numbers are shown

- Order of results: metric 1 (detection probability) → metric 2 (attacker's uncertainty) → cost (running time). Within 8.1 and 8.2, **real topologies first, synthetic second**, so each claim is made on measured networks and then on the controlled ones.
- Every figure in the evaluation portion:
  - **Fig. 4** "Minimum detection probability for different networks using centrality-weighted (CWS) and expanded centrality-weighted (ECWS) strategies" (pdf:44) — three panels, one per ISP: (a) Telstra, (b) Sprintlink, (c) Verio. Each is a line chart: **x = "No. of Detectors (% of Mission-Critical Nodes)", ticks 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50**; y = "Minimum Detection Probability", 0–0.3 (Telstra), 0–0.35 (Sprintlink), 0–0.3 (Verio). **Four series per panel = strategy × mission-critical fraction**: `<Network> - CWS - 10%`, `- ECWS - 10%`, `- CWS - 30%`, `- ECWS - 30%`. Facets = network.
  - **Fig. 5** "Minimum detection probability for different strategies using synthetic topologies" (pdf:44) — single panel, **x = "No. of Detectors", ticks 25, 50, 75, 100, 125, 150, 175, 200, 225, 250** (absolute counts, not percentages, because network size is fixed at 3,000); y = Minimum Detection Probability, 0–0.6. **Seven series**: `Uniform Random Strategy`, plus `Centrality-Weighted Strategy - {10%, 20%, 30%}` and `Expanded Centrality-Weighted Strategy - {10%, 20%, 30%}`. Note the synthetic figure carries the **three-level** mission-critical factor (10/20/30%) while the Rocketfuel figure carries only two (10/30%).
  - **Fig. 6** "Relative increase in entropy for the attacker introduced by different strategies" (pdf:45) — two panels: (a) Sprintlink network, x = "No. of Detectors (% of Mission-Critical Nodes)" 5…50 step 5, y = "**Relative Prior Entropy**" 50–75; (b) Synthetic network, x = "No. of Detectors" 25…250 step 25, y = "Relative Entropy" 70–95. **Six series in each**: CWS and ECWS at 10%, 20%, 30%. The uniform-random strategy does not appear as a series because it *is* the denominator: "we computed the **relative entropy** introduced by the centrality-weighted and the expanded centrality-weighted strategy **w.r.t. the uniform random strategy**" (pdf:44).
  - **Fig. 7** "Running time for computing the minimum detection probability using Algorithm 1." (pdf:45) — x = "Network size", ticks 1000…10000 step 1000; y = "Average Running Time (in mins)", 0–120 (a second 0–80 axis appears in the extraction, likely a secondary axis or the neighbouring Fig. 8). Two series: `Avg. Running Time of Algorithm 1` and `Poly. (Avg. Running Time of Algorithm 1)` — i.e. **the measured curve plus an explicit fitted trendline**, annotated on-chart with `R² = 0.9968`.
  - **Fig. 8** "Running time for different detector placement strategies" (pdf:45) — x = "Network Size", y = "Average Running Time (in mins)"; three series: `Iterative Centrality Strategy`, `Centrality-Weighted Strategy`, `Expanded Centrality-Weighted Strategy`.
  - No error bars are visible in the extracted axis text; none are mentioned.
- Every table: **Table 1** only (md:321–333, topology inventory, 10 rows × 4 columns). There are **no results tables** — every result is a figure or a prose statement.
- How the baseline condition appears — **three baselines, each handled differently, and the most important one is reported in prose only**:
  - *Static iterative centrality* (the prior state of the art) appears in **no** detection or entropy figure. Its result is a flat prose sentence: "In these simulations, we observed that the static iterative centrality strategy **could not detect the exfiltration of the data segments in any of the networks** as there was at least one detector-free path between one of the mission-critical nodes and a C&C location" (pdf:44). A zero on every network, stated once, not plotted. It appears in Fig. 8 only, for running time.
  - *Uniform random* appears as a plotted series in Fig. 5 (synthetic detection) and as the **normalising denominator** in Fig. 6 (entropy). Its entropy role is declared in advance in §6.2's framing: "the static iterative centrality strategy does not introduce any uncertainty whilst the uniform random strategy introduces the **highest** uncertainty" (pdf:44) — i.e. the two baselines are positioned as the two ends of the scale the proposed strategies are then placed between.
  - *Theoretical worst case* `O(|V|⁶)` acts as the baseline for the running-time result (md:211, pdf:45).
- How comparisons are stated in prose: **shape claim → ordering claim → causal mechanism**, in that order, and with the mechanism always attributed to a declared property of the setup:
  - Shape: "the probability of detecting exfiltration attempts **increases linearly** with the number of detectors" (pdf:44).
  - Ordering, with its scope quantified: "**independently of the number of mission-critical nodes and detectors**, the centrality-weighted strategy **outperforms** the expanded centrality-weighted strategy" (pdf:44).
  - Mechanism, tied back to the topology family declared in setup: "This trend can be attributed to the **scale-free nature of the topology** in which most of the paths traverse a small portion of the nodes. As the expanded strategy considers a larger sample space of paths and distributes the placement probability across the nodes on these paths, the nodes with high centrality will be chosen with a lower probability than in the case of the centrality-weighted strategy" (pdf:44).
  - Positioning between baselines: "the centrality-weighted strategy and its expanded version create an uncertainty that **lies in-between the two ends of the entropy spectrum**. In particular, as expanded strategy potentially considers more nodes, the number of combinations of detector locations, and hence the uncertainty introduced by it is **higher** than the centrality-weighted strategy" (pdf:44).
  - The synthesis, stated as a trade-off rather than a winner: "Therefore, for ISP networks, the choice of different centrality-weighted strategies potentially **trades-off entropy for detection probability guarantees**" (pdf:44) — note that CWS wins on detection and ECWS wins on entropy, and the paper declines to rank them.
  - No percentages or magnitudes are quoted anywhere in the results prose; the only numbers are `R² = 0.99` (pdf:45), the `<1%` variation (pdf:44), and the complexity exponents.

## D. Narrative — the claims, in order

1. Detection probability rises linearly in detector count, on both real and synthetic topologies (pdf:44).
2. Synthetic-network variance is negligible (<1%), licensing the reporting of means only (pdf:44).
3. CWS > ECWS on detection probability, universally over the swept factors, with the scale-free mechanism given (pdf:44).
4. **The prior static strategy detects nothing at all**, on every network, because a detector-free path always exists (pdf:44) — the sharpest claim in the paper, and the justification for the whole MTD framing.
5. ECWS > CWS on attacker uncertainty, with the combinatorial mechanism given, and both bracketed between the static (zero entropy) and uniform-random (maximum entropy) ends (pdf:44).
6. The two claims are reconciled as a trade-off, not a ranking (pdf:44).
7. Empirical complexity `O(|V|³)` with `R² = 0.99`, against a theoretical worst case of `O(|V|⁶)` — a *measured* refutation of the pessimistic bound: "Although, in theory, the worst-case running time of Algorithm 1 is _O_(|_V_|⁶), for practical network settings, it can be seen (line marked in orange in Fig. 7) that the execution time grows as _O_(|_V_|³)" (pdf:45). Forward-referenced from the analysis subsection nine pages earlier (md:211).
8. The dynamic strategies are *cheaper* than the static one, with a mechanism: "the dynamic strategy computes the detector locations faster than its static alternative. This is because, the static iterative centrality strategy has to **re-compute the shortest paths multiple times** to determine the location of the detectors" (pdf:45).

Claims about the ATTACKER from the data: yes, and it is one of the two headline metrics — **attacker's uncertainty measured as entropy over detector locations** (md:219–221), reported relative to the uniform-random maximum (pdf:44–45). The interpretive rule is stated when the metric is defined: "by the above definition of entropy, the **higher the entropy, the higher the advantage for the defender** over the attacker" (md:221) and "the higher the entropy, the higher the attacker's effort required to discover the location of detectors" (md:165). This is an attacker-side quantity derived from the defence's configuration, not from observed attacker behaviour — the attacker is not simulated adaptively; it is assumed optimal (md:171), so the reported detection probability is a guaranteed lower bound rather than a measurement of what an attacker achieved.

## E. Sensitivity / parameter analysis

Present: yes — the sweep is the entire results section, and the paper states the *purpose* of the sweep as a resourcing question rather than as a curve-drawing exercise.

- Parameters varied, with ranges and steps:
  - **Number of detectors _k_ — the primary x-axis, swept with two different parameterisations depending on the topology family, and the reason is declared**: "For the ISP networks from the Rocketfuel dataset, we varied the size of the detector set **as a fraction of the number of mission-critical nodes** while, for synthetic topologies, we varied the size of the detector set **as a fraction of the network size**. These simulations were intended to study **the impact on the amount of resources that a network administrator might be willing to invest (either proportional to no. of nodes that need to be protected or size of the network)** to detect exfiltration" (md:340 continuing to pdf:44). Ranges: Rocketfuel — 5% to 50% of mission-critical nodes, step 5 (10 levels; Fig. 4, Fig. 6a axis ticks); synthetic — 25 to 250 detectors, step 25 (10 levels; Fig. 5, Fig. 6b axis ticks). Running-time study — "We varied the number of detectors from **3% to 7% of network size**" (pdf:44).
  - **Fraction of nodes that are mission-critical** — declared as a range in setup, "with mission-critical nodes varying between **10%–30% of the network size**" (md:338), and realised as a **series factor**: three levels (10%, 20%, 30%) in the synthetic figures (Figs. 5, 6b) and the Sprintlink entropy figure (Fig. 6a), but only **two** levels (10%, 30%) in the detection-probability figures for the Rocketfuel networks (Fig. 4). The 20% level is silently absent from Fig. 4; no reason is given.
  - **Network size** — swept only in §8.3, x = 1000 to 10000, step 1000 (Figs. 7, 8), on generated ISP-type topologies.
  - **Topology** — treated as a facet, not a sweep: three Rocketfuel networks plotted (Telstra 2998 routers, Sprintlink 8341, Verio 7102) out of the ten declared in Table 1.
  - **Strategy** — the other series factor: CWS vs ECWS everywhere, plus Uniform Random in Fig. 5 and Iterative Centrality in Fig. 8.
- Parameters **fixed, and stated as fixed** — the paper puts this in one sentence at the head of the results, which is the single most transferable line in it: "**In all our simulations, we set _δ_ = 0.5 for the expanded centrality-weighted strategy** and tested the statistical significance of the presented results using paired _t_-test at 95% confidence interval" (pdf:44). Note the value differs from the illustrative example, where "when _δ_ = 0.25, the nodes shaded with different intensities of brown in Fig. 3(b) will be considered" (md:271) — the example value and the experimental value are different and the paper does not remark on it. `δ` is the one parameter that plainly *could* have been swept (it directly controls the ECWS/CWS difference the results turn on) and was not; no justification is given for 0.5. Also fixed: 500 C&C locations per synthetic network (md:338), 3,000 nodes for the detection/entropy synthetic networks (md:338), 10 networks per setting (md:338, pdf:44).
- Design: **factorial, but reported as multi-series line charts rather than as a grid.** The crossed factors are {detector count: 10 levels} × {mission-critical fraction: 2–3 levels} × {strategy: 2–7 levels} × {topology: 3 real + 1 synthetic family}, with `δ` and the C&C count held fixed. Detector count is always x; the other factors become series or facets. Nothing is one-at-a-time except the running-time study, which sweeps network size alone and reports that the detector-count factor was checked and made no difference (see below).
- Range justification, parameter by parameter:
  - Detector count range: justified *functionally* rather than numerically — the sweep exists to answer the administrator's resourcing question (md:340/pdf:44); the specific 5–50% and 25–250 endpoints carry no stated rationale.
  - Two different parameterisations of the same knob (fraction-of-mission-critical vs fraction-of-network): justified explicitly by the two ways an administrator might budget (md:340/pdf:44).
  - Mission-critical fraction 10–30%: **not justified** — no citation, no rationale.
  - Network sizes 1000–10000: not justified directly, but bracket the real Rocketfuel range (121–10152, Table 1), which is declared.
  - Synthetic topology family: justified by a cited structural property — scale-free / power-law degree distribution at router level [20] — and generated with a cited generator [13] (md:338).
  - C&C set: justified as a worst case for the real networks ("assumed that **all** the external routers are potentially routing traffic to C&C servers", md:336); the 500-random-locations choice for synthetic networks is **not justified**.
  - `δ = 0.5`: **not justified**.
- Reporting: figures only, no sensitivity table; ten x-levels per figure with the tick values legible from the axes; strategy and mission-critical fraction encoded in a compound legend string (`Telstra (1221) - ECWS - 30%`) that names network, strategy, and factor level in one label.
- Where it sits: it *is* §8; there is no separate sensitivity section and no appendix.
- **A swept-then-collapsed reporting convention worth naming**: in §8.3 the detector-count factor is swept, found not to matter, said so, and then dropped from the presentation — "We varied the number of detectors from 3% to 7% of network size and **observed similar trend in the running time. For the sake of presentation, we present the results with 3% of network size** for the total no. of available detectors" (pdf:44). The same move is made for the ten synthetic replicates: swept, variation quantified at <1%, then collapsed to means (pdf:44), and for the ten Rocketfuel topologies: "due to space constraints, we show the results for a **subset** of the topologies from the Rocketfuel dataset" (pdf:44). In all three cases the paper says what it collapsed and why.
- Claim supported: (i) linearity of detection probability in detector count (pdf:44); (ii) the CWS>ECWS ordering *across the whole grid*, which is what licenses the word "independently of the number of mission-critical nodes and detectors" (pdf:44); (iii) the entropy/detection trade-off between the two proposed strategies (pdf:44); (iv) the empirical `O(|V|³)` scaling of the algorithm (pdf:45).
- Distinctive convention: **the swept knob is parameterised in the defender's budgeting units, and re-parameterised per topology family so the x-axis means the same thing to an administrator in both** ("either proportional to no. of nodes that need to be protected or size of the network", md:340/pdf:44) — the sweep is designed around the decision it informs, not around the model's internal variable.

## F. Discussion / limitations

No discussion section and no limitations section. Interpretation is inline, one causal paragraph per result (pdf:44–45). Limitations appear in three places: as declared simplifications at the point of assumption, as presentation-collapse notes in §8, and as a numbered future-work list in §9.

- Detector accuracy, with the fix already scoped: "we ignore the accuracy of detectors and assume that any exfiltration attempt going through a detector node is detected. **Factoring the probability of missed detections in our model is relatively straightforward, and we plan to do so as part of our future work**" (md:108).
- Routing assumption, bounded rather than hidden: "our approach does not rely on the assumption that traffic is routed along the shortest path … However, for the sake of presentation, and **without limiting the generality of our approach**, we do assume that the networks being studied implement a shortest path routing algorithm" (md:61).
- The dual problem deferred for space: "A dual problem that can be defined is that of minimizing the number of detectors needed to satisfy predefined security requirements, but **we do not discuss this problem for reasons of space**" (md:134).
- Presentation collapses, each announced: subset of Rocketfuel topologies shown "due to space constraints" (pdf:44); means only, because inter-network variation was "less than 1%" (pdf:44); 3% detector setting only, because other settings showed "similar trend" (pdf:44).

- No threats-to-validity language, no comparability caveats, and no discussion of the gap between the assumed-optimal attacker and a real one beyond future-work item (ii).

## G. Transferable vs purpose-specific

Transferable to an honours dissertation evaluating existing MTD mechanisms against a new attacker model on a simulator:

- **A threat model as three numbered assumption bullets, each carrying its own justification in the same bullet** (md:126–130) — incident-report citations for the topology-knowledge assumption, a detection-avoidance mechanism for the segmentation assumption, Kerckhoffs for the strategy-knowledge assumption.
- **Declaring the attacker's success criterion inside the threat model** ("if and only if all the _m_ data segments are exfiltrated by time _t_m", md:128) rather than leaving it implicit in the metric.
- **Arguing a fidelity simplification instead of asserting it**: the detector-accuracy paragraph (md:108) reasons that a *prudent attacker's* behaviour makes false-positive/negative rates irrelevant to the question at hand — then still books it as future work.
- **Bounding an assumption by naming the weaker one it stands in for** (md:61: the real requirement is path predictability, shortest-path is only a presentational instance).
- **A one-line "in all our simulations we fixed X, and tested significance by Y" sentence at the head of the results** (pdf:44) — everything held constant, and the statistical treatment, in a single declaration.
- **Significance testing named with its test and confidence level** (paired _t_-test, 95% CI, pdf:44) — rare in this batch.
- **Parameterising the swept knob in the decision-maker's units, and re-parameterising per topology family so the x-axis keeps its meaning** (md:340/pdf:44).
- **Announcing every collapse**: what was swept but not shown, and why (subset of topologies, means only with the variation quantified, one detector level because the others matched) — pdf:44.
- **Two ordering-opposed metrics reported as a trade-off rather than a winner** (CWS wins detection, ECWS wins entropy; pdf:44), with the trade-off sentence stated explicitly.
- **A strategy list ordered as a chain, each entry opening with the named deficiency of the previous one** (md:227–271) — the reader gets the design rationale and the ablation structure at once.
- **Two baselines positioned as the ends of the scale before results appear** ("the static iterative centrality strategy does not introduce any uncertainty whilst the uniform random strategy introduces the highest uncertainty", pdf:44), then the proposed methods reported as landing between them.
- **Reporting a baseline's zero result in prose when plotting it would be pointless** (static strategy detects nothing on any network, pdf:44).
- **Declaring the topology inventory in a table with sizes before any result** (Table 1, md:321), even for networks not subsequently plotted.
- **Justifying a synthetic generator by a cited structural property of the real thing** (scale-free/power-law at router level, BRITE; md:338).
- **Checking a theoretical complexity bound empirically and reporting the fit** (`O(|V|⁶)` theory vs `O(|V|³)` measured, `R² = 0.99`; md:211 forward reference, pdf:45 result) — including a fitted trendline as an explicit second series on the chart.
- **Hardware declared for the timing experiment specifically** (pdf:44) rather than globally or not at all.

Purpose-specific / venue artefacts:

- The minimum-detection-probability metric is a *lower bound against an optimal attacker* computed by a graph algorithm (Algorithm 1, md:191), not a simulated outcome; a simulator that runs an actual attacker produces a distribution instead, and cannot inherit the "minimum" framing.
- Attacker's uncertainty as entropy over detector placements is specific to a randomised-placement defence with a known strategy; it is a property of the defender's distribution, not of an observed adversary.
- Detector placement, mission-betweenness centrality, and the exfiltration-surface formalism are tied to this paper's ISP/router-level setting.
- Fixing `δ = 0.5` with no justification, using a different `δ` in the worked example (0.25, md:271) without comment, and dropping the 20% mission-critical level from Fig. 4 with no explanation, are gaps rather than conventions to copy.
- Not stating the number of replications for the Rocketfuel experiments (only for the synthetic ones) is likewise a gap.
- The horizon `m` (number of exfiltration segments / time points), which is central to the threat model, is never given a value anywhere in the evaluation.
