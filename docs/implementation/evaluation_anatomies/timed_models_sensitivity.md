# Survey: parameter sensitivity and replication conventions in timed / stochastic attack models

Purpose: how the Petri-net / CTMC / SMP / MAL-family / probabilistic attack-model literature reports parameter justification, sensitivity analysis and replication, so the GSPN attacker model's sensitivity section can follow field conventions. All locators are `<file>:<line>` in `docs/sources/tactic_profiles/step_d/0_cross_tactic_timed_models/`. "Not stated" means the md was searched and nothing was found. Garbled maths in several mds (PDF extraction) is noted where it limits what can be read.

---

## 1. Madan et al. 2004 (SMP, SITAR intrusion-tolerant system)

File: `madan2004_intrusion_tolerant_quantification_pe.md`

(a) **Citation.** B. B. Madan, K. Goseva-Popstojanova, K. Vaidyanathan, K. S. Trivedi, "A Method for Modeling and Quantifying the Security Attributes of Intrusion Tolerant Systems" (`:1`, `:3`). Venue not printed in the md; filename suffix `pe` indicates *Performance Evaluation* (2004). Year not printed in the md either (latest reference is 2002, `:242`).

(b) **Purpose.** Model an attacker plus the SITAR system's response as a semi-Markov process and solve it for steady-state availability, mean time to security failure (MTTSF) and the probabilities of absorption into each security-failed state (`:13`, `:60-66`).

(c) **Parameters and justification.** Two parameter sets: mean sojourn time per state and the embedded-DTMC transition probabilities (`:113-117`); nine mean sojourn times and five probabilities are enumerated (`:161-189`). Justification is explicitly *assumed*: "we ignore the issues related to model parameterization and instead used reasonable guessed values for various model parameters" (`:36`); "we assume good 'guestimate' values for various model parameters" (`:213`). Ordinal reasoning is given for the guesses (successful attack "less likely than detection", system "spends more time in the good and vulnerable states than in the active attack state") (`:213`). Parameter estimation from DARPA IA red-team experiments and fault injection is deferred to future work (`:191`, `:230`). Sojourn-time *distributions* are discussed (hypo/hyper-exponential, Weibull, log-logistic) but the steady-state analysis "depends only on the mean sojourn time and is independent of the actual sojourn time distributions" (`:119`).

(d) **Sensitivity analysis.** Motivated directly by the guessed parameters: "In the absence of exact values of model parameters, it will, however be meaningful to evaluate the sensitivity of security related attributes to variations in model parameters. In Section 5 we present some numerical results to evaluate the sensitivity of the MTTSF and the steady-state availability to various model parameters." (`:191`); "The use of 'gusetimate' model parameter values makes it relevant to also carry out sensitivity analysis of different measures to variations in the model parameter values." (`:213`). Parameters varied: two, p_a (probability of successful attack from the vulnerable state) and h_G (mean sojourn time in the good state), varied jointly on a 2-D grid; other parameters held at their baseline. Ranges read off the figure axes: p_a axis labelled "0.025 x pa" with ticks 0-40 (so p_a in 0-1 in steps of 0.025), h_G axis labelled "0.1 x hg" with ticks 0-100 (so h_G in 0-10 in steps of 0.1) (`:217`, `:222`). Design: two-factor surface (factorial over the two), not one-at-a-time. Outputs tracked: MTTSF and steady-state availability. Presentation: two 3-D surface plots, Figure 5 "MTTSF as a function of p_a and h_G" and Figure 6 "Availability as a function of p_a and h_G" (`:217`, `:224`). Reported result is a monotonicity statement: MTTSF "monotonically decreases as p_a is increased... with respect to h_G, MTTSF increases monotonically"; availability "a decreasing function of p_a and an increasing function of h_G" (`:217`). Selection of the two parameters is asserted, not shown: "Sensitivity results ... indicate that both the MTTSF and the steady-state availability are more sensitive to p_a and h_G model parameters" (`:217`), with no table of the screening over the other parameters. Location in structure: inside "5. Numerical Results" (`:213`), following the baseline point solution; no separate sensitivity section.

(e) **Replication.** Analytical (closed-form SMP solution, equations (10), (14), (15)); no simulation, no replication, no CIs (`:200`, `:211`, `:217`).

(f) **Explicit convention statements.** The SMP/DTMC solution method is cited to Trivedi's textbook and Bhat ("using the approach outlined in [23, 6]", `:211`); MTTSF is defined "using the MTTF analogy" from reliability (`:209`). The sensitivity study is framed as the substitute for parameter estimation: "we have sought to address the issue of the absence of exact values of model parameters by studying the sensitivity of different security attributes to small changes in the parameter values" (`:230`). No "as is standard" phrase.

(g) **Transfer.** Establishes the field's baseline move for a guessed-parameter timed model: declare the guesses, say why they are guesses, then sweep the one or two parameters the outputs are most sensitive to over a wide range and plot the output surface; a dwell-time mean (h_G) and a success probability (p_a) are exactly the two parameter kinds the GSPN declares, so a per-tactic dwell mean against a failure-conditioned weight is a direct analogue of Madan's Figure 5/6 pair.

## 2. Orojloo and Abdollahi Azgomi 2018/2019 (SPN → SMP, CPS control loop with IDS)

File: `orojloo2018_cps_stochastic_petri_nets_ietcps.md` — **the md is largely unreadable**: 102 lines, only fragments of §4.2–4.3 plus figure picture-text survive (`:28-75`, `:89-101`); the abstract, §5 and all numbers are missing. The block below is read from a pdfplumber extraction of the sibling PDF, saved as `scratchpad/survey/orojloo_pdf.txt`; locators `pdf:<line>` refer to that file, `md:<line>` to the md.

(a) **Citation.** H. Orojloo, M. Abdollahi Azgomi, "Modelling and evaluation of the security of cyber-physical systems using stochastic Petri nets", *IET Cyber-Physical Systems: Theory & Applications*, 2019, vol. 4 iss. 1, pp. 50-57, doi 10.1049/iet-cps.2018.0008; received Jan 2018, E-first Sept 2018 (`pdf:2-12`, `md:75`).

(b) **Purpose.** An SPN (solved through its underlying semi-Markov model) of a CPS control loop with an IDS, to see how detection interval, false-positive probability and time-to-physical-disruption move availability and MTTF (`pdf:15-21`, `pdf:76-82`).

(c) **Parameters and justification.** Eight declared inputs in Table 3: P_a, P_tp, P_fp, T_a, T_d, T_f, T_RTP, T_RFP (`md:32-43`, `pdf:277-307`); transition rates are derived as ratios (λ_attack = P_a/T_a, λ_fp = P_fp/T_d, etc., `pdf:233-305`). Justification: "The value of these parameters should be determined by security professionals" (expert, `pdf:227-229`); P_tp, P_fp "can be determined by using historical data"; T_x "can be estimated by using the process model" (`md:51`, `pdf:346-354`). In the example, T_f = 154 s and T_RTP = 270 s are *measured* from the two-tank plant simulation (attack initiated at 600 s, level exceeds 100 cm at 719 s; process returns after 270 s: `pdf:727-733`; Table 4 `pdf:773-777`); the remaining defaults (P_fp 0.1, P_a 0.5, P_tp 0.9, T_a 86,400 s, T_d 20 s, T_RFP 30 s, T_r 7200 s) are given without justification (`pdf:762-779`). Table 4 gives each varied parameter a "Confidence interval" column that is in fact the sweep range: P_fp [0.01, 0.2], P_a [0.1, 1], P_tp [0.01, 0.99], T_d [10, 800], T_f [50, 400]; T_a, T_RTP, T_RFP, T_r are marked "—" (not varied) (`pdf:762-779`).

(d) **Sensitivity analysis.** Explicit one-at-a-time protocol: "In each scenario, we set the value of all needed parameters to their default values and change the value of the parameter under investigation to show its effects." (`pdf:744-747`); anticipated in the method section as "we assign values to some parameters to isolate out their effects, while others parameters are changed" (`md:51`). Five numbered "Effect of ..." items in §5.3 Numerical results (`pdf:750-812`): (i) control-input period (1 s vs 0.2 s, plant-level, not the SPN); (ii) valve speed (1/s vs 2/s, plant-level); (iii) attack probability P_a and detection interval T_d → availability and MTTF, Fig. 6a/b, x = P_a, series = T_d (`pdf:809-812`, `pdf:819-820`); (iv) time to physical disruption T_f → availability and MTTF "with varying detection interval T_d", Fig. 7a/b, x = T_f (0-400 s), series = T_d (20 s, 50 s read from picture text `md:98`) (`pdf:771-784`, `pdf:821-822`); (v) false-positive probability P_fp → availability, Fig. 8a/b/c, x = T_d (0-600/300/800 s), series = P_fp ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 0.2}, one panel per (P_a, T_f) pairing: (0.5, 50), (0.9, 50), (0.5, 150) (`pdf:786-812`, `pdf:844-845`, `md:101`). Steps not stated (figures only). Design: OAT on the primary axis with one secondary parameter as the line series; items (iii)-(v) are effectively two-factor line charts. Findings reported as monotone trends plus an optimum ("for each false-positive probability, there is an optimal T_d value under which the system availability is maximised", `pdf:796-797`), with optimal T_d values tabulated in prose (`pdf:798-812`). Location: inside "5 Illustrative example / 5.3 Numerical results"; no separate sensitivity section; bulleted qualitative conclusions follow (`pdf:823-837`).

(e) **Replication.** Analytical (SMP steady-state and absorbing-state solution, eqs (10)-(16), `pdf:462-473`); the plant dynamics are ODE simulation (`pdf:527-538`), deterministic. No replication, no CIs — the "Confidence interval" column is a sweep range, not a statistical interval.

(f) **Explicit convention statements.** The MTTF/availability machinery is cited to Madan et al. 2004 [24] and Trivedi [25] ("can be derived using the following formula [24]", `pdf:462-464`, `pdf:369-370`, references `pdf:889-894`). No "as is standard" phrase.

(g) **Transfer.** Field precedent for (1) a parameter table with a "default value" column and a "range" column that marks the un-swept parameters with "—", and (2) an explicit OAT sentence ("all at default, vary the one under investigation"); for the GSPN, the per-tactic dwell means map onto Orojloo's T_x time parameters and the failure-conditioned weights onto its P_x probabilities, so a Table-4-style declaration (default, range, source) per dwell mean and per weight followed by "effect of ..." line charts with a second parameter as series is the direct analogue.

## 3. Wu et al. 2021 (stochastic evolutionary game Petri net, AD-SEGPN)

File: `wu2021_stochastic_evolutionary_game_petri_net.md`

(a) **Citation.** Z. Wu, L. Tian, Y. Zhang, Y. Wang, Y. Du, "Network Attack and Defense Modeling and System Security Analysis: A Novel Approach Using Stochastic Evolutionary Game Petri Net", *Security and Communication Networks* (Hindawi), vol. 2021, Article ID 4005877, published 13 Nov 2021 (`:1`, `:5-7`, `:17`).

(b) **Purpose.** Combine a stochastic Petri net with evolutionary game theory (bounded-rational attacker/defender) so that transition-selection probabilities come from the evolutionary equilibrium, then compute attack success rate, mean time to successful intrusion, MTTR, reliability and availability for a five-step sniffer-installation attack on a small intranet (`:23`, `:49`, `:162-170`).

(c) **Parameters and justification.** Two parameter columns per transition in Table 3: λ ("behavior ability" / firing rate) and π (selection probability) for five attack transitions and four defence transitions (`:210-222`). Justification is explicit and split: "The exact value of λ can be assumed according to the difficulty in the actual attack process, and the exact value of π is the calculation result of evolutionary equilibrium strategy in Section 3.2." (`:180`) — λ *assumed*, π *derived* from the model's own equilibrium. λ values are integers 5-10 with no units, no source (`:214-222`). Solved with the PIPE tool ("we use the pipe software", `:184`).

(d) **Sensitivity analysis.** Not called a sensitivity analysis anywhere. One parameter is varied: the attack rate λ ∈ {1, 5, 10, 15}, applied uniformly as "rate of attack" (which of the five attack-transition λ's this replaces is not stated) (`:235`, `:248`, `:265`). Outputs tracked against "Network System Time" (x-axis, 0-200 or 0-100, unitless): probability of successful attack (Fig. 4, `:233-235`), mean time for a successful attack (Fig. 5, `:246-248`), MTTR (Fig. 6, `:263-265`); reliability and availability vs time at the base parameters only (Fig. 7, `:282-284`). Presentation: line charts, x = system time, y = metric, series = the four λ values. Design: one-at-a-time on a single parameter, four levels, no step rule stated. Findings are qualitative trend statements ("the higher the attack rate is, the more attack time is needed", `:239`; "the average repair time of the system increases first and then decreases", `:254`) and one steady-state claim "the success rate of the attacker's attack has little to do with the rate of attack behavior" (`:294`, `:224`). Location: inside "5. Network System Security Analysis" §5.1.1-5.1.3, i.e. the results section; no own section, no appendix.

(e) **Replication.** Not stated. Curves are presented as analytic/tool outputs (PIPE); no run counts, no CIs, no error bars mentioned. Whether PIPE's simulation or its steady-state solver was used is not stated.

(f) **Explicit convention statements.** None. The MTTR formula is given as a queueing definition (MTTR = N/λ*, `:250`); reliability and availability definitions cite [28], [29] (`:267`, `:275`). Validation is asserted, not shown: "the results verify the rationality and accuracy of the model" (`:23`), "This result is completely in line with the actual situation" (`:239`).

(g) **Transfer.** A negative exemplar on justification (assumed integer rates, one-parameter sweep at four levels with no rationale for the levels) but a useful precedent for the *form*: a time-axis line chart with the swept rate as series is the field's default when the model is transient; for the GSPN it corresponds to plotting time-to-compromise or compromise probability against simulated time with per-tactic dwell mean as the series, and shows that a uniform scale factor on all rates (Wu's "rate of attack") is an accepted single-knob sweep.

## 4. Zhou, Reniers and Zhang 2019 (timed coloured Petri net, chemical-plant attack time)

File: `zhou2019_petri_attack_time_chemical_cce.md` — **partially lossy**: the md carries the prose but drops §3.3, the §4.2/§4.3 headings and Tables 3, 4, 5 and 8 (the parameter and simulation tables); Fig. 15 survives only as picture-text (`md:214`). Numbers below marked `pdf:` are read from a pdfplumber extraction of the sibling PDF saved as `scratchpad/survey/zhou_pdf.txt`; `md:` locators are the md.

(a) **Citation.** J. Zhou, G. Reniers, L. Zhang, "Petri-net based attack time analysis in the context of chemical process security", *Computers and Chemical Engineering*, vol. 130 (2019), art. 106546, doi 10.1016/j.compchemeng.2019.106546 (`pdf:5-7`; author-version archived at Universiteit Antwerpen, `pdf:2`, `md:1`). Author affiliations `md:12-18`.

(b) **Purpose.** Map an attack tree onto a timed coloured Petri net so that attack *duration* (not just success) can be analysed, then compare simulated attack duration against a surveillance interval to produce a security-protection failure probability, for one, two or three cooperating attackers (`md:20`, `md:45`, `pdf:449`).

(c) **Parameters and justification.** Ten stochastic transitions, each declared by a single mean execution time under a one-parameter exponential distribution; Table 3 "Mean execution times of exponential distributions **assumed** in the Petri-net model": t1_0 1.0, t1_1 4.0, t1_2 10.0, t2_2 1.0, t2_3 5.0, t2_4 8.0, t2_7 10.0, t2_8 10.0, t3_1 4.0, t3_2 2.0 min (`pdf:679-690`). The *distribution family* is justified by citation, the *values* are not: "The durations of the stochastic transitions are determined according to certain probability distribution functions. In Khalil's research (Khalil, 2016), attack events obey one-parameter exponential distributions. Thus, the execution times of the stochastic duration transitions are determined to obey exponential distributions." (`pdf:674-677`; Khalil 2016, *Process Safety and Environmental Protection* 102, 473-484, `pdf:991-992`). No source, measurement or expert elicitation is offered for the ten means; the table's own word is "assumed" (`pdf:679`). The branch choice at the OR gate is likewise declared assumption: "the choice of the two ways is assumed random" (`md:127`). The number of attackers (1, 2, 3) is a structural parameter, not a rate: it changes tokens and arc weights (`md:196`, `pdf:461-465`). Tool: a bespoke Java timed-coloured-hybrid-Petri-net tool from the authors' own prior work (`md:129`).

(d) **Sensitivity analysis.** Not named "sensitivity analysis". Two things are varied and they are varied *factorially*: the surveillance interval (5 to 100 minutes, plotted at 5-minute steps — x-axis ticks 5, 15, 25, ..., 95, `md:214`, `pdf:903-905`) crossed with the number of attackers (1, 2, 3 as the three series). The transition means themselves are never swept. Output tracked: probability of security-protection failure, Pr_fail = Cnt_fail / SimNum (`pdf:480`). Presentation: one line chart, Fig. 15, x = "Time interval of surveillance (minute)" 5-95, y = "Probability of failure" 0-1.2, series = "One attacker" / "Two attackers" / "Three attackers" (`md:213-216`, `pdf:920-932`). Verbatim introduction: "Based on the attack process analysis approach and the failure probability analysis approach described in Section 3.3, failure probabilities of the security protection system under different surveillance intervals (from 5 minutes to 100 minutes) are obtained and shown in Fig. 15, where the number of simulations (SimNum) is 10^4." (`pdf:901-905`; the exponent is a genuine superscript in the PDF, verified at char level, so 10,000 runs). Location in structure: §4.2(ii) "Analysis of failure probability of security protection", inside the illustrative example, after the single-trace walkthroughs of §4.2(i) — no separate sensitivity section and no appendix. Findings are monotone-trend plus a saturation claim: "as the number of attackers increases, the failure probability of security protection increases" (`md:211`), and "the probability of security failure increases sharply from one to two attackers, whereas there is only a marginal increase of failure probability from two to three attackers" (`md:226-228`), explained structurally by the AND/OR/PAND gate analysis (`md:218`).

(e) **Replication.** Monte-Carlo, with the replication convention stated as an algorithm rather than a sentence: Step 3 initialises "the simulation times SimNum, the simulation index, and the failure counter Cnt_fail"; Step 4 samples the transition durations from their distributions and increments Cnt_fail if the attack duration is under the inspection interval; Step 5 "Repeat the trail until the number of trials is greater than the given times SimNum" (`pdf:466-480`, flowchart Fig. 9 `md:167`). Run count 10^4 per point, stated once in the Fig. 15 sentence (`pdf:904-905`). **No confidence intervals, no error bars, no variance reported** — the probability estimate is a bare ratio. Single sample traces are also shown as evidence (Tables 4-6, one attacker; Tables 7-8, two attackers, `md:186-206`, `pdf:718-844`), and the paper calls this verification: "The aforementioned attack process analysis can be used to verify the proposed approach" (`pdf:901`).

(f) **Explicit convention statements.** One, and it is a distribution-family convention borrowed by citation: "In Khalil's research (Khalil, 2016), attack events obey one-parameter exponential distributions. Thus, the execution times ... are determined to obey exponential distributions." (`pdf:675-677`). The TCPN definition itself is cited to the authors' prior TCHPN work (`md:49`) and the semantics of timed PNs to Zuberek 1991 / Reddy 1993 (`md:39`). No "as is standard" phrase; no citation offered for the run count or for the choice of 10^4.

(g) **Transfer.** The closest structural analogue in the set: per-transition *mean* dwell times under an assumed exponential family are exactly the GSPN's first declared input, and Zhou shows the field will accept them declared in one table with the word "assumed" provided the *family* is cited. Two moves transfer directly — (1) sweep a defender-side parameter (surveillance interval) rather than the dwell means, with an attacker-capability level as the series, and (2) state the run count inline in the figure sentence. What Zhou does *not* do is the gap to beat: no CI on a 10^4-run probability estimate and no perturbation of the ten declared means, so a GSPN sweep that perturbs its dwell means and reports an interval already exceeds this paper's standard.

## 5. Liu, Xing and Zhou 2019 (CTMC + SMP, sequential Trojan attack)

File: `Engineering Reports - 2019 - Liu - Probabilistic modeling and analysis of sequential cyber‐attacks.md` — readable; PDF-extraction artefacts are cosmetic (alternate table rows come through as `~~strikethrough~~`, and every figure survives as caption plus picture-text only). One real loss: the §3.2.2 heading and its prose on the recovery-rate sets are missing (the figures for it, `:193-208`, are there).

(a) **Citation.** Q. Liu, L. Xing, C. Zhou, "Probabilistic modeling and analysis of sequential cyber-attacks", *Engineering Reports* (Wiley, open access), 2019;1:e12065, doi 10.1002/eng2.12065; received 31 May 2019, accepted 6 Nov 2019 (`:1`, `:7`, `:11`, `:33`, `:37`). Dept. of Electrical and Computer Engineering, UMass Dartmouth (`:13`).

(b) **Purpose.** Drop the usual independence assumption and model a *sequence-dependent* cyber-attack (attack tree → CTMC for the time-dependent solution, → semi-Markov process for the steady-state solution with arbitrary transition-time distributions), demonstrated on a mobile-banking Trojan (`:19`, `:53`).

(c) **Parameters and justification.** Two declared parameter sets, one per method. **CTMC:** six rates per hour — three recovery rates μd, μq, μc and three attack rates λA, λB, λC — instantiated as five named sets *a*-*e* in Table 1 (e.g. set *a*: μd 0.1, μq 0.2, μc 0.15, λA 0.034, λB 0.024, λC 0.0023; set *c* differs only in λB = 0.47; set *d* has μ = 0.482/0.527/0.39, set *e* μ = 0.0125/0.098/0.0067) (`:120-127`). Justification is one clause: "Table 1 lists five sets of parameters designed based on statistics and data from.[55-58]" (`:107`) — refs 55-56 are industry threat-report *web pages* (Threatpost on Proofpoint's Q4 banking-Trojan data; Kaspersky Securelist "IT threat evolution Q2 2019") and refs 57-58 are computer-virus epidemic-model mathematics papers in *Nonlinear Analysis* (`:569-575`). No per-parameter attribution: which number comes from which source is not stated. **SMP:** seven transition CDFs, each declared as a *family plus two parameters* in Table 7 "Baseline values of model parameters" — F01 Weibull (α=1/0.034, β=0.54), F10 Weibull (1/0.0125, 0.86), F12 Rayleigh (1/0.106, 2), F20 Exponential (1/0.15, 1), F21 Exponential (1/0.2, 1), F23 Weibull (1/0.0023, 0.072), F32 Exponential (1/0.39, 1) (`:263-271`, `:275`). The α's are the reciprocals of the CTMC rates, so the scale parameters carry over; the shape parameters β are new and unsourced. Justification: "Table 7 shows some baseline values of model parameters for the example system based on statistics and data of other works.[62,65-67]" (`:253`) — but those four references are **generic distribution methodology, not cyber data**: Kumar et al. on semi-Markov availability of repairable *mechanical* systems, Alexander on Monte Carlo for *pump* reliability, Bailey and Dell on the Weibull function for *tree diameters*, Thoman et al. on Weibull inference (`:583-597`). The parameters are also given *semantic labels*, which is how the paper defends the sweep rather than the value: λB "reflects a user's awareness and responsibility in protecting the system or device" (`:146`); α12 is glossed "mean time to infection", α10 "mean time to delete malicious files or links", the grouped α01/α12/α23 "mean time from a better state to a worse state" (`:259`).

(d) **Sensitivity analysis.** Never called "sensitivity analysis"; the section headings are "Effects of ..." throughout. It runs on **two tiers**.

*Tier 1 (CTMC, §3.2 "Analysis results").* Not a numeric sweep but **five named scenario sets**, compared three at a time: λB varied over sets *a*/*b*/*c* = 0.024 / 0.106 / 0.47 per hour, glossed as "a cautious user ... a relatively careless user ... an intermediate case between the former two", with the regime named too ("set *a* corresponds to a case where the recovery capability dominates (λB << μq), while set *c* corresponds to a case where the occurrence rate of the malicious event dominates (λB > μq)") (`:146`, `:148`, `:150`); then the three recovery rates μd, μq, μc varied *together* over sets *d*/*b*/*e* (`:146`). Output tracked: the four state probabilities P0-P3 against mission time. Presentation is doubled — Tables 2-4, rows = P0..P3, columns = t at 2k, 4k, 8k, 16k, 20k hours, one table per set (`:128-142`) *and* Figures 4-7, x = t from 0 to 20k hours in 2k steps, y = one state probability per figure, series = the three parameter sets (`:152`, `:163-182`); Figures 8-11 repeat that layout for sets *d*/*b*/*e* (`:193-208`).

*Tier 2 (SMP, §4.2).* An explicit, stated four-case design. Verbatim introduction: "While in Section 3, the system security performance is investigated from the perspective of user's protection awareness and recovery capability, in this section, we study effects of different transition time distribution parameters on the system security. Particularly, we choose to vary the Weibull distribution parameter (α or β) of a single attack transition F12 (case 1), or a single recovery transition F10 (case 2), or all attack transitions (F01, F12, F23) (case 3), or all recovery transitions (F10, F20, F21, F32) (case 4) to illustrate their effects on the system security performance." (`:255`). This is the clearest **single-vs-grouped OAT** statement in the survey: two singleton sweeps and two all-of-a-kind grouped sweeps, attack-side and defence-side matched. Ranges and steps: scale α "from 1 second to 1 month" at six non-uniform, roughly logarithmic levels — 1 second, 1 minute, 15 minutes, 1 hour, 1 day, 1 month (`:259`, `:281`); shape β on irregular grids, {0.1, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 5} for β12, {0.1, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3} for β10, {0.1, 0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.7, 1, 1.5, 5} for the grouped attack β, {0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 5} for the grouped recovery β (`:359`, `:366`, `:377`, `:389`) — the grids are refined where the response turns, not held uniform. Ceteris paribus is stated twice, once per parameter: "All other unchanging parameters remain the same as those in Table 7." (`:259`) and "While we vary β, all other unchanging parameters remain the same as those in Table 7." (`:403`). Outputs tracked: P0, P1, P2, P3 and a derived scalar "Security" = 1 - P3 = P0 + P1 + P2 (`:249`). Presentation is again doubled and is the paper's signature form: **every case gets both a table and a figure** — Tables 8-11 (α) and 12-15 (β), rows = P0..P3 plus Security to nine decimal places, columns = the swept levels (`:279-323`, `:357-395`); Figures 13-16 and 18-21, x = the swept parameter on a categorical axis of the same levels, y = probability 0-1, four series P0/P1/P2/P3 (`:327-344`, `:401-419`), with Figure 18 adding "the zoomed graph for the system security (right figure)" as a second panel because the effect is small (`:405`). Location in structure: §3.2/§3.2.1 and §4.2/§4.2.1 — inside "Analysis results" in both cases, no standalone sensitivity section and no appendix. Findings are stated as shape claims, monotone and non-monotone: "As α12 increases, the attack success probability (P3) reduces or the system security ... increases" (`:261`); "The attack success probability P3 first increases, then reaches the maximum around β12 = 2, and then drops due to interacting effects between β and α" (`:405`); "for the case with changing β01 = β12 = β23, the system security plot is concave, while for the case with changing β10 = β20 = β21 = β32, the system security is a convex curve" (`:427`). The closing sentence is the paper's own sensitivity verdict: "the system steady-state probabilities and particularly, the system security, are highly dependent on values of the transition time distribution parameters. For the Weibull distribution studied, the scale parameter and the shape parameter affect the system security differently (the former's impact is more intuitive, while the latter's impact is more complicated appearing nonmonotonic)." (`:429`).

(e) **Replication.** Analytical, no replication. CTMC solved for the time-dependent state probabilities and SMP by kernel matrix K(t) → embedded Markov chain → sojourn times; "Wolfram mathematics and MATLAB are used to calculate the complex integrals involved" (`:231`), "The set of equations is solved by Wolfram mathematics in this work" (`:239`). No simulation, no run counts, no CIs.

(f) **Explicit convention statements.** Method-by-citation throughout, no "as is standard" phrase: "Assuming the Weibull state transition time, the nonzero elements of K(t) are given in (8) to (14).[62]" (`:231`); "For the SMP steady-state analysis,[64]" — Kulkarni's *Modeling and Analysis of Stochastic Systems* (`:235`, `:587`); "sojourn time Ti used in (6) is evaluated using (16) to (19) according to the work of Kumar et al.[62]" (`:241`). The two parameter-justification clauses (`:107`, `:253`) are themselves the convention: *cite a bundle of sources for the table as a whole rather than defend each number*.

(g) **Transfer.** The most directly usable template in the set. Three moves transfer: (1) the **stated four-case sweep design** — vary one attack parameter, one defence parameter, all attack parameters together, all defence parameters together — maps onto a GSPN sweep of one tactic's dwell mean, one failure-conditioned weight, all dwell means jointly, all weights jointly, and gives a defensible reason to stop at four cases; (2) **semantic labelling of each swept parameter** ("mean time to infection", "a cautious user") is how this literature converts an unsourced number into a defensible one — the GSPN's per-tactic dwell means can be labelled the same way and swept between named attacker profiles rather than arbitrary levels; (3) the **table-plus-figure pair per case**, table for the numbers and figure for the shape, with the sweep grid refined where the response turns. Liu also shows the trap: the "based on statistics and data of other works" citation bundle points at forestry and pump-reliability papers, so a citation bundle without per-parameter attribution buys nothing — attribute each dwell mean to its own source or mark it assumed.

## 6. Ouyang, Liu, Ruan and Jiang 2019 (PSO for reliability-redundancy allocation) — off-topic for the survey, kept for its conventions

File: `1-s2.0-S0951832018304125-main.md` — readable throughout; figures survive as captions plus picture-text, tables intact.

**Identification and a caveat.** This is **not a timed attack model, not a Petri net, and not a security paper**. It is a reliability-redundancy allocation optimisation paper: Z. Ouyang, Y. Liu, S.-J. Ruan, T. Jiang, "An improved particle swarm optimization algorithm for reliability-redundancy allocation problem with mixed redundancy strategy and heterogeneous components", *Reliability Engineering and System Safety*, 181 (2019) 62-74 (`:1`, `:13`, `:20`; University of Electronic Science and Technology of China, `:22-24`). The word "attack" does not appear in the abstract, and the only adversary-adjacent term ("switching reliability") is a hardware failover probability (`:52`, `:77`). It is presumably filed here for its *sensitivity-and-replication conventions* in the reliability-engineering venue that also publishes attack models, and that is all this block records. (a)-(c) below are given briefly; (d)-(g) are what the survey actually wants.

(a) **Citation.** As above (`:1`, `:13`, `:20`).

(b) **Purpose.** Allow *heterogeneous* components inside a mixed active/cold-standby redundancy strategy — previously all components in a subsystem had to be homogeneous — and solve the resulting nonlinear mixed-integer problem with a stochastic-perturbation PSO variant (SPPSO) (`:28`, `:63`).

(c) **Parameters and justification.** Two kinds. *Algorithm* parameters are declared in one table with no justification and no citation: 100 particles, 800 iterations, and Table 1's ω, c1, c2, δ ~ U(-10^-8, 10^-8), C = 0.5 per operator, with operator-assignment probabilities p = {0.7, 0.2, 0.1} (`:326-337`, `:660`). *Problem* parameters are justified purely by **benchmark inheritance**: "the parameter settings for the three modified benchmarks are listed in Tables 2 and 3, and we keep them the same as the reported works in [18,23,24,33,34,60]" (`:660`), and the fixed baseline for the comparison is likewise inherited — "the switching reliability is set to be ρ(t) = 0.99 which is exactly the same as that of Refs. [27,34]" (`:666`). Mission time is fixed at t = 1000 hrs and component lifetimes "are assume[d] to be exponentially distributed" (`:666`). This is the cleanest instance in the survey of the **"same numbers as the prior work, so the comparison is apples-to-apples"** justification: the value's defence is comparability, not evidence.

(d) **Sensitivity analysis.** One dedicated subsection, and unusually it *has its own heading*: "**4.3. Impact of switching reliability**" (`:690`) — the only paper in this survey to give the analysis a section of its own rather than burying it in "Analysis results". Verbatim introduction: "Due to the imperfection of switching systems (could be a hardware and/or software), the switching reliability is less than one. To examine the impact of the switching reliability on the optimal results, we vary the switching reliability of the three modified benchmarks from a higher value (0.99) to a lower value. The maximum system reliability of the following four cases are plotted in Fig. 14:" (`:692`). Parameter varied: one, ρ(t), one-at-a-time, downward from 0.99; the grid is not stated in prose but is legible from Tables 11-13 as **steps of 0.01** (rows run 0.99, 0.98, 0.97-0.96, 0.95-0.88, 0.87, 0.86-0.82, <0.82) (`:597-610`). Crossed against a **four-level categorical factor**, the design cases: "Case 1: The mixed redundancy strategy with heterogeneous components; Case 2: ... with homogeneous components [34]; Case 3: The cold standby strategy with homogeneous components [27]; Case 4: The active redundancy strategy with homogeneous components [18-21]" (`:694-700`) — so a 1-continuous × 4-categorical design, replicated across three benchmark systems. Outputs tracked: maximum system reliability; the *optimal configuration* (n_active, n_cold vectors); and the optimised decision variables themselves (component failure rates). Presentation, three forms at once: (i) Fig. 14, three panels (a)/(b)/(c) one per benchmark, x = switching reliability, y = system reliability, series = the four cases (`:581`); (ii) Tables 11-13, one per benchmark, rows = **switching-reliability intervals collapsed wherever the optimum does not change** (0.95-0.88 is one row), columns = the configuration vectors, heterogeneous beside homogeneous (`:589-610`); (iii) Fig. 15, x = switching reliability, y = the optimised failure rates of active vs cold-standby components (`:684`). Findings are reported as **critical thresholds plus a direction of adaptation**, not as a slope: "The active redundancy strategy becomes the best choice when the switching reliability is lower than 0.82, 0.89, and 0.70, for the three modified benchmarks, respectively, that is to say, the active redundancy strategy is more reliable than other redundancy strategies if the switching reliability is less than a critical value" (`:702`); "it prefers to replace the cold standby components with active redundancy components to alleviate the negative impact from the decease of the switching reliability" (`:702`); "the failure rate of active redundant components should be slightly reduced if the switching reliability decreases, whereas the failure rate of cold standby components can be greatly increased" (`:704`). The intent is also pre-announced in §1 — "This work only deals with the first scenario and will examine how the switching reliability may impact on the optimal system configuration" (`:77`) — and claimed in the abstract as a headline contribution: "Most importantly, we also reveal that the switching reliability has significant influence on the optimal system configuration" (`:28`).

(e) **Replication.** The most explicit replication convention in the survey, and the only paper that reports dispersion. "The SPPSO algorithm was executed **30 times independently** for each benchmark." (`:660`). Reported per benchmark: **best, Mean and Std** over the 30 runs, tabulated beside the same three statistics from every competing published algorithm — Tables 7-9, rows include `Mean` and `Std` (benchmark 1: SPPSO mean 0.928800437, Std 2.625e-003, against Afonso's mean 0.921823 / Std 1.863e-002) (`:483-500`, `:527`, `:549`). The stated reason is robustness: "To examine the performance and robustness of the proposed SPPSO method, we compared our results with the most competitive results reported in the recent publications. To make an apple-to-apple comparison with the reported works in [15,18,21,23,24,60], only the active redundancy strategy is used for the three traditional benchmarks and the best results together with their **mean and standard deviation from 30 independent runs** are presented in Tables 7-9." (`:674`). No confidence intervals and no significance test — the comparison is made informally: "our proposed algorithm still outperforms the algorithm of Afonso [15] **in a statistic sense**, because the mean value of ..." (`:674`). Run-time is also reported as a mean (Table 10, average running time in seconds, SPPSO vs PSO across the three benchmarks, `:554-561`), and the hardware and software are declared (Matlab 2013a, Intel Core 2.50 GHz, 4.0 GB RAM, `:577`, `:660`).

(f) **Explicit convention statements.** "apple-to-apple comparison with the reported works in [...]" (`:674`) and "we keep them the same as the reported works in [...]" (`:660`) are the two convention statements, and both are about *comparability with prior literature* rather than about method. The lower-bound approximation for the intractable equations is taken by citation: "the approximating method reported in [35] can be implemented to get acceptable lower bounds" (`:151`). No "as is standard" phrase.

(g) **Transfer.** Three conventions worth taking, none of them attack-specific: (1) **30 independent runs, reporting best / mean / std side by side with the comparators** — this is the replication standard a stochastic GSPN run should meet, and it is the standard that every attack-model paper in this survey fails; (2) **collapse the sweep grid into intervals where the answer does not change and report the critical threshold** — for the GSPN, the useful sensitivity claim about a failure-conditioned weight is "the ranking of tactics flips below w = x", not a slope; (3) the **"same parameters as the prior work" justification** is available for any GSPN input inherited from the substrate or from Brown 2023, and it defends comparability rather than realism — which is exactly the claim such an input can carry.

## 7. Tripathi, Tripathi, Singh and Chaturvedi 2022 (GSPN, nuclear power plant feedwater control)

File: `1-s2.0-S0306454921007404-main.md` — readable prose; heavy PDF-extraction mangling of the mathematics (λ renders as `k`, ≈/⁻ as `�`), figures survive as picture-text, and **Tables 6, 8 and the Table 9 caption are absent from the extraction** (Table 9's data rows are present as a transposed table at `:570-601`).

(a) **Citation.** D. Tripathi, A. K. Tripathi, L. K. Singh, A. Chaturvedi, "Towards analyzing the impact of intrusion prevention and response on cyber-physical system availability: A case study of NPP", *Annals of Nuclear Energy*, 168 (2022) 108863; received 23 Aug 2021, accepted 23 Nov 2021, online 10 Dec 2021 (`:1`, `:13`, `:17`, `:23`). Dept. of Computer Science and Engineering, IIT (BHU) Varanasi (`:19`).

(b) **Purpose.** Build a **GSPN** of an attacker intruding a nuclear-plant digital feedwater control system through layered preventive defences (perimeter, authentication, access control) and reactive defences (IDRS), and quantify the combined effect on steady-state probability of the undesired state, mean-time-to-disrupt (MTTD) and system availability (`:23`, `:390`, `:642`). **The closest match in this survey to the GSPN attacker model's own shape.**

(c) **Parameters and justification.** Two kinds. *Transition rates* λ0-λ36, split by role: the eleven "malicious" rates that represent the **failure rate of a defence** (λ0, λ2, λ8, λ10, λ13, λ22, λ23, λ26, λ29, λ31, λ34) and the eleven paired rates that represent its **success rate / strength** (λ1, λ3, λ7, λ9, λ12, λ21, λ24, λ27, λ30, λ32, λ35); the remaining rates model attack propagation and are **pinned at 1** — "the firing rates of the transitions that models the attack propagation is kept constant. Hence, for these transitions the corresponding firing rates λ4 = λ5 = λ6 = 1; λ15 = λ16 = λ17 = λ18 = λ20 = λ28 = λ36 = 1" (`:545`). Firing delay is the reciprocal of the rate, stated as a formula: "The firing delay of respective transitions of Figure 6 at each run is calculated as (MST_Ti = 1/λ_Ti)" (`:453`). Transition *probabilities* are derived from the rate pair rather than declared independently: p_T1 = λ1/(λ0+λ1) = 0.96, p_T0 = λ0/(λ0+λ1) = 0.04, and so on for every defence layer (`:547`) — i.e. **a failure-conditioned weight computed from a competing-rates ratio**, which is structurally the GSPN's third declared input. Justification: the paper **declares the parameters unobtainable and makes the sensitivity analysis the justification**, in the survey's single clearest statement of that move: "it is difficult to test cyber-attacks experimentally in NPPs or to simulate them virtually. Hence, the transition rates of stochastic model are difficult to obtain and so is the determining the failure probabilities of preventive and responsive security measures. **The proposed work overcomes this problem by performing a sensitivity analysis to analyze the effect of defense measures strength and to prioritize these measures.**" (`:69`). The levels themselves are explicitly assumed: "It is assumed that the strength of defense measures {L, M, H} is represented with value range {0.1-0.5, 0.51-0.8, 0.81-1.0} respectively in the interval [0.1, 1]. Although, for performing comparative analysis, we have fixed the strength of defense measures {L, M, H} as value {0.5, 0.8, .96} respectively for all cases." (`:545`) — note the two-layer move: a *band* per qualitative level, then one *representative value* per band for the reported runs. The mean sojourn times behind the mean-cycle-time arithmetic (25, 1.04, 0.33 time units, `:549`) are **not sourced anywhere in the readable md**; Tables 6 and 8 are missing from the extraction, so whether the paper declares them elsewhere cannot be read.

(d) **Sensitivity analysis.** Called "sensitivity analysis" by name, twice, and it *is* the paper's evaluation — there is no separate baseline result. Verbatim introductions, both of them: "To evaluate the impact of security measures, we carry out the sensitivity analysis." (`:453`) and "To perform sensitivity analysis for evaluating and comparing steady state probabilities, we vary the strength of preventive measures and responsive measures (at network and host level) and analyze its effect on model evaluation metrics including steady state probability, MTTD and system availability." (`:545`). Design: a **full 3 × 3 × 3 factorial**, 27 combinations — three defence groups (SDf network-level firewalls, SDh host-level authentication and access control, SDr responsive IDRS), each at three levels {L, M, H} = {0.5, 0.8, 0.96}; parameters *within* a group move together (SDh is one knob driving λ7 = λ9 = λ21 = λ24 = λ30 = λ32) (`:545`, `:598-601`). Not one-at-a-time: every combination is evaluated. Outputs tracked: all 25 steady-state probabilities p0-p24, plus availability A and MTTD (`:570-596`). Presentation, doubled: (i) **Table 9**, columns = the 27 defence-strength combinations labelled by their λ assignments, rows = p0...p24, MTTD, A (`:570-601`) — the full numeric grid; (ii) **Fig. 10**, three panels, "(a) ... impact of defense strength SDf, SDh and SDr on steady state probability of being in undesired state (or reactor trip condition) p24, (b) impact of defense strength on availability (A) and (c) impact of defense strength on MTTD" (`:605`), each with **x = the nine host×responsive combinations as a categorical axis (L-L, L-M, L-H, M-L, M-M, M-H, H-L, H-M, H-H), y = the metric, series = the three network-level strengths ("Low Network Strength", "Medium Network Strength", "High Network Strength")** (`:618`, `:620`). That layout — factor-pair on the categorical x, third factor as the series, one panel per output metric — is the most sophisticated presentation in the survey. Location in structure: inside "6.2. Quantitative Evaluation", the case-study section; no standalone sensitivity section (the name is used but not as a heading), no appendix. Findings are ordering and trade-off claims rather than slopes: the extreme corners ("when defense measures strength combination is [L, L, L] ... SSP is highest and when the strength combination is [H,H,H] ... SSP is the lowest", `:605`); a **cross-factor comparison that ranks the factors** ("SSP significantly depends on responsive measures strength. As if the attack is detected and responded with high strength, but the network and host level are low i.e. [L, L, H] as compared to when the responsive level is low but the network level is low and host level is medium i.e. [L, M, L], the SSP is comparatively lesser for [L, L, H]", `:605`); and an **actionable substitution rule** ("if it is not possible to place high strength defense measures at network, host level and responsive level due to any reasons, at least either of the network level defense or host-level defense measures should be strong, as shown in the graph where the availability is comparable for combinations [L, H, M], [L, H, H] and [M, M, L], [M, M, M] and [H, M, L] [H, M, M]", `:605`). The paper also flags the grid's own coarseness as a known limitation: "It is worth noticing that the defense measures strength can be divided into closer ranges for deeper analysis." (`:607`).

(e) **Replication.** Analytical, no replication. The GSPN is reduced to its reachability graph and embedded Markov chain (20 tangible + 5 vanishing markings, a 25 × 25 transition-probability matrix P partitioned into C, D, E, F), then solved for the steady-state vector and the mean cycle time by hand-shown arithmetic (`:453`, `:469`, `:547-551`). No simulation, no run counts, no CIs. No solver tool is named in the readable md.

(f) **Explicit convention statements.** No "as is standard" phrase. Two structural conventions are stated instead: the parameter-difficulty-answered-by-sensitivity claim at `:69` (quoted above), and the **hold-the-attacker-fixed rule** at `:545` — attack-propagation rates pinned at 1 so that only defence strength moves, which is what makes the 27-cell grid interpretable. Validation is by literature comparison rather than data: §6.3 "Comparative Evaluation" is a **qualitative coverage table** (Table 10, rows = four prior works, one column "Vulnerabilities handled", concluding "Proposed approach models and analyzes the impact of preventive and responsive measures both") (`:622-638`) — no numeric benchmark against any of them.

(g) **Transfer.** The single most transferable paper here, because its three declared inputs are near-isomorphic to the GSPN attacker model's: rates → firing delays via MST = 1/λ, and **failure-conditioned weights derived as λ_success/(λ_success + λ_failure) rather than declared independently** (`:453`, `:547`). Four moves transfer: (1) the **justification chain** — say plainly that the rates cannot be measured for this system, then present the sweep as the answer, citing Tripathi as precedent; (2) **group the parameters and sweep the groups**, so a 3-input model gives a 27-cell full factorial instead of an unreadable per-parameter sweep — for the GSPN, group the per-tactic dwell means into tactic families and the weights into attacker-skill levels; (3) the **two-layer level scheme**, a band per qualitative label plus one representative value used in the reported runs, which lets the model speak in {low, medium, high} attacker profiles while remaining numerically reproducible; (4) **Fig. 10's layout** — two factors crossed on a categorical x-axis, the third as the line series, one panel per output metric. Its gaps mark the ceiling: no replication (it is analytical), no source for the sojourn times, and validation only as a qualitative coverage table.

## 8. Almasizadeh and Abdollahi Azgomi 2013 (semi-Markov chain, attacker/defender interaction) — the negative exemplar: no sensitivity analysis at all

File: `1-s2.0-S1389128613000856-main.md` — readable prose; matrices and equations are mangled by extraction (`:603`), figures are captions only, but every input table survives intact.

(a) **Citation.** J. Almasizadeh, M. Abdollahi Azgomi, "A stochastic model of attack process for the evaluation of security metrics", *Computer Networks*, 57 (2013) 2159-2180; available online 8 April 2013 (`:1`, `:13`, `:17`, `:23`). School of Computer Engineering, Iran University of Science and Technology (`:19`). Same second author as paper 2.

(b) **Purpose.** Model the *time-resolved interaction* between attacker and defender across several parallel attack paths as a semi-Markov chain with non-exponential transition times, and solve it analytically for mean time to security failure (MTTSF), steady-state security, and the probability that each attack path is the one that succeeds (`:23`, `:304-316`).

(c) **Parameters and justification.** The declared input per transition is a **mixture of two uniform distributions**: a pair of time intervals with weights, e.g. buffer overflow transition T_{S,BO1} = "[50,150], 0.3 / [200,400], 0.7" (Table 1, `:539-551`); four such tables, one per attack path — buffer overflow (Table 1, `:539`), man-in-the-middle (Table 2, `:695-707`), SQL injection (Table 3, `:711-723`), traffic sniffing (Table 4, `:727-735`) — seven transitions each, with attacker transitions and defender ("detection") transitions declared separately on the same scale. Transition *probabilities* are then **derived, not declared**: "the success probability of the attacker in the attack phase may be expressed as P(X < Y) and the probability of the successful detection ... as P(Y < X)" with P(X < Y) + P(Y < X) = 1 (`:324`) — the failure-conditioned weight falls out of the two competing time distributions. The distribution family is defended at length on four grounds, and this is the paper's substantive methodological argument: "the reasons for the use of the mixture distribution of uniform distributions are as follows. First, it is an increasing failure rate distribution. Second, its parameters can be simply estimated. Third, it describes the stochastic nature of attacker behavior and defender suitably. Finally, based on the selected time intervals, **attacker skill level and the robustness of system can be represented by modeler**." (`:300`) — with the exponential explicitly rejected: "a main problem in some models (e.g., [7,11,12]) is that assigned time distribution to transitions of the models is simply assumed to be exponential ... due to the memoryless property of this distribution, this assumption may not be appropriate in describing many situations of an attack process" (`:685`). The *values*, by contrast, are defended by disclaimer: "In this study, our main purpose is to present a method of modeling and evaluation rather than exact parameterization of the stochastic model. ... Therefore, **only carefully estimated numerical values are used to demonstrate how the proposed model may be used** to obtain quantitative metrics." (`:533`), supported by one ordinal argument tying the numbers to the scenario ("the servers are protected by enforcing strong security controls and such defensive strategies are not taken for the workstations. As a result, the frequency of the occurrence of SQL injection and buffer overflow attacks is likely to be smaller compared with that of traffic sniffing and man-in-the-middle attacks", `:535`). One further parameter is introduced mid-analysis to make the chain irreducible and is declared assumed: "The parameters of the distribution function assigned to the transition are assumed to be: [1,10], 0.8 and [10,25], 0.2." (`:611`).

(d) **Sensitivity analysis.** **None.** The word "sensitivity" appears exactly once in the whole paper and in an unrelated sense — a related-work summary of Hecker and Riguidel on "security assurance" (`:103`); it is never used of this model. No parameter is varied, no range is swept, no second parameter set is evaluated. §6.3 "Quantitative evaluation of security metrics" (`:529`) computes **one point solution** from the four input tables and reports three scalars: MTTSF = 2420 h ("Under the assumptions made, this quantity (2420 h) is an indicator of how much, on average, the time is needed for the attackers to compromise the data confidentiality", `:609`); the steady-state split ("the network stays in the secure state with probability 0.86, interacts with the attackers with probability 0.138 and performs the recovery actions with probability 0.002", `:633`); and the per-path absorption probabilities (`:635`). Presentation: **no results figure and no results table** — every number is inline in the prose or inside a displayed matrix. This is the survey's clean counter-example: a paper that admits its parameters are estimated, and then does *not* sweep them. Note what it does instead — it moves the defence up a level, from the values to the **distribution family** (`:300`), arguing that the family is chosen so that "attacker skill level and the robustness of system can be represented by modeler". The sweep the paper does not run is exactly the one that family invites.

(e) **Replication.** Analytical, no replication, and the paper argues that this is a *virtue*: "the proposed model is a mathematical model which makes the possibility of the analytic computation of the security metrics. **In contrast to simulation models, mathematical models provide a more complete view of system security.**" (`:689`). Solution is by embedded DTMC: transition probability matrix P, mean sojourn times, fundamental matrix M = (I - T)^-1 (`:322`, `:601-603`). No run counts, no CIs, no solver named.

(f) **Explicit convention statements.** The methodological framing is stated as importing dependability practice into security — "The proposed method shows a systematic development of the stochastic modeling techniques and concepts, **used frequently in the area of dependability evaluation**, for attack process modeling" (`:27`); "just as appropriate fault models are critical to dependability evaluation, an appropriate attacker model is critical to quantitative security evaluation" (`:687`, citing [1]). No "as is standard" phrase. Most valuable for the survey is the **explicit, unusually candid validation statement**, which names the field's parameter problem and refuses the usual workaround: "In the context of security analysis, a security model must be validated against real attack data. ... Empirical data are needed as input parameters of security models and our model parameterization is largely dependent on data, usually obtained from actual measurement. ... **In this work, due to the lack of suitable attack data, we have used simple assumptions about the model's parameters. At present, suitable data in order to validate the proposed stochastic model are unavailable.** Obtaining reliable data is a very difficult task." (`:749-751`). It then rejects honeypot data as a source on the grounds that the attackers are not representative — "such experiments are conducted under controlled conditions which are mainly different from real world conditions ... It is clear that such attackers are not good representative of real attackers. Real attackers behave based on their capabilities, goals and motivations. ... we do not recommend conducting such highly misleading experiments in unreal interaction environments" (`:751`) — and proposes instrumented production systems instead. Two other open problems are named as field-level challenges: attack-process modelling coverage (`:747`) and transient (as opposed to steady-state) analysis (`:753`).

(g) **Transfer.** Three things, one of them a warning. (1) The **P(X < Y) derivation** is the cleanest precedent for the GSPN's failure-conditioned transition weights: declare attacker dwell and defender detection times as distributions on the same clock and let the weight fall out, rather than declaring the weight independently — the sweep then runs over the *dwell parameters*, and the weights move with them, which is a defensible way to sweep two of the three declared inputs with one knob. (2) The **defend-the-family-then-the-values order** (`:300`) plus the explicit rejection of the exponential (`:685`) is the argument to reach for if per-tactic dwell times are given a non-exponential family, and the fourth reason given — that the interval endpoints *are* the attacker skill level — is precisely how a per-tactic dwell mean should be argued. (3) The warning: this paper's own parameter defence is a disclaimer ("carefully estimated ... to demonstrate how the proposed model may be used", `:533`) with no sweep behind it, and the candid limitations section (`:749-751`) shows the author knew it. Cite Almasizadeh for the honest statement of the data problem; do not follow it in leaving the parameters unswept.

## 9. Wideł, Hacks, Ekstedt, Johnson and Lagerström 2023 (Meta Attack Language, formal semantics)

File: `1-s2.0-S0167404823001943-main.md` — readable; the LaTeX-heavy formal sections come through as raw math markup but the prose and the code listings are intact. Figures are captions only.

(a) **Citation.** W. Wideł, S. Hacks, M. Ekstedt, P. Johnson, R. Lagerström, "The meta attack language - a formal description", *Computers & Security* (Elsevier, open access), received 27 Jan 2023, accepted 28 April 2023, online 2 May 2023 (`:5`, `:9`, `:11`, `:18`, `:30`). KTH Royal Institute of Technology and Stockholm University (`:13-14`).

(b) **Purpose.** Give the Meta Attack Language — the meta-DSL underlying the whole MAL family (coreLang, powerLang, vehicleLang) — a formal syntax and semantics, so that a MAL model's translation into a probabilistic attack graph and the computation of time-to-compromise are defined rather than tool-dependent (`:28`, `:477`).

(c) **Parameters and justification.** The declared input is a **TTC probability distribution per attack step per asset**, carried in the language itself rather than in the instance model: "stepSpec_L is a function specifying properties of steps in the context of particular assets: their types, their prerequisites and consequences, and the probability distributions of their TTCs" (`:186`), written in square brackets in the concrete syntax, with a **stated default**: "if a distribution is not stated explicitly, then it is assumed that the execution of the step is instantaneous, and so its TTC distribution is Bernoulli(0)" (`:325`). Defences are declared separately as Bernoulli probabilities of being in place — "assigning to each of the defense steps present in the model a Bernoulli probability distribution describing the likelihood of the defense being implemented" (`:388`), e.g. `defenses(Patched_OS, patched) = Bernoulli(1)`, `defenses(NotPatched_OS, patched) = Bernoulli(0)` (`:451-453`). Families named are Bernoulli, exponential and gamma (`:141`). **Justification of the values: none, and by design.** The architectural claim is that the numbers are the *domain expert's*, encoded once in the DSL and inherited by every user — "Ideally, a user will employ a DSL for modeling their infrastructure of interest, with the domain-specific threats and attack logic being **already encoded in the DSL by the domain experts**" (`:26`), whose point is to "Enable non-security expert for analysis" (`:77`). Where the numbers come from is delegated to a cited companion paper — Xiong, Hacks and Lagerström, "A method for assigning probability distributions in attack simulation languages" (`:688`) — and to per-domain TTC estimation work (Rencelj Ling and Ekstedt, "Estimating the time-to-compromise of exploiting industrial control system vulnerabilities", `:675`). The paper's own position on parameter quality is candid and is stated as a *feature of the approach*: MAL "enables language developers to both take on a top-down approach with coarsely defined assets and attack steps that (likely) includes **plenty of probabilistic time to compromise distributions with high uncertainties (as the example in this paper)**, as well as a bottom-up approach with a narrower domain but more determinism" (`:599`).

(d) **Sensitivity analysis.** **None.** No parameter is varied; the single quantitative result is one worked example. The paper says so of itself: "**This article provides no evaluation of the proposed formalisms.**" (`:601`), with validity argued instead by pointing to deployed MAL DSLs for IT, power and vehicles (`:601-605`). What it *does* supply is the presentational form: "Example for time to compromise ... we obtained the optimal attack vector depicted in Fig. 2. ... In Fig. 3, the **empirical cumulative distribution function of the global TTC** for the target is plotted. It has been derived from results of **a hundred runs** of the algorithm presented in Section 7, employing the local TTC distributions specified in the language." (`:587`). So: output = global TTC to one named target attack step; presentation = **an empirical CDF, x = global TTC, y = cumulative probability, single series** (Fig. 3, `:595`), paired with the attack-vector graph (Fig. 2, `:593`). Location in structure: an unnumbered "Example for time to compromise" paragraph at the end of §7, immediately before the conclusions; no results section, no appendix. What the semantics *does* fix — and this is the substantive contribution — is how uncertainty composes: local TTC is sampled per node ("T_loc(v) = 0 if v is an entry point, ← ttc(v) otherwise", `:569`), and global TTC accumulates as **T_glob(v) = T_loc(v) + min over parents** for an OR step and **+ max over parents** for an AND step (`:571-583`), with a footnote conceding that the AND rule is conservative because parent independence cannot be assumed: "Should all the parents of each of the AND attack steps be independent, one could consider taking the sum of their times to compromise instead of the maximal value. In practice, however, such independence cannot be guaranteed, and the obtained result would be a potentially hazardous overestimate" (`:603`). The paper explicitly declines to standardise the *analysis* on top of that: "There are many different ways to calculate the TTC ... we do not want to restrain concrete implementations of MAL to a certain implementation how to calculate or use the generated attack graph." (`:585`).

(e) **Replication.** Simulation with a stated run count and **no other convention**: "a hundred runs of the algorithm" (`:587`). Each run samples the defence Bernoullis first, then the reachable steps' TTC distributions, then propagates — a four-stage Monte-Carlo procedure stated in prose (`:537`). No confidence intervals, no seed, no convergence check, no justification for 100; the entire distributional information is carried by the empirical CDF rather than by a summary statistic with an interval. Tooling is cited by footnote to "the MAL tooling" rather than named in the text (`:535`).

(f) **Explicit convention statements.** No "as is standard" phrase, but two convention statements of the *defaulting* kind, which is this literature's distinctive move: the Bernoulli(0) default for an unspecified TTC (`:325`) and the min/max composition rules with their stated motivation (`:583`). Design requirements are stated as numbered principles inherited from the original MAL paper — "Causality-based security predictions", "Enterprise-wide systems-of-systems analysis", "Enable non-security expert for analysis" (`:74-77`). Related probabilistic-attack-graph work is characterised by its parameter source, notably CVSS-derived probabilities (Frigault et al.; Xie et al., `:93`) — the alternative convention MAL does not adopt.

(g) **Transfer.** Two transfers and one caution. (1) The **empirical-CDF-over-N-runs presentation** is the MAL family's standard way to show a time-to-compromise result under sampled parameters, and it is directly the right figure for a GSPN whose output is a distribution over compromise time rather than a scalar — it shows the whole spread without needing a CI. (2) The **composition semantics** (`:569-583`) is the precedent for saying explicitly how per-tactic dwell times combine along a path, min for alternatives and max for conjunctions, and for *owning the conservatism* in a footnote where independence fails; a GSPN that declares per-tactic means owes the same statement about how they aggregate to a run-level time. (3) The caution: MAL's answer to "where do the numbers come from" is *the domain expert encoded them in the DSL* (`:26`, `:77`) with the sourcing delegated to companion papers — an institutional answer, not an evidential one, and unavailable to a single-model thesis. Note too that even the field's reference formalism ships its worked example on distributions it calls "high uncertainties" (`:599`) with 100 runs and no sweep: a GSPN that declares its dwell means, sweeps them and reports intervals is not behind this literature, it is ahead of it.
