## 1. What §3.3 is

**The job in one line.** §3.3 is the chapter's closing strand and the one the
whole chapter was reordered to end on: it shows that the attacker every MTD
metric is scored over is thin, measures how thin against an instrument the
literature itself supplies, and states the gap once. The reader arrives from
§3.2 having accepted that a metric cannot see past the attacker model it is
computed over (the 3.2.2 closing sentence, `hong2018`); §3.3 then asks what
that attacker model actually is.

**Which sub-question it feeds.** SQ2, *model* (ch1's `sq:model`: how APT
behaviour is made executable as an attacker model). §3.1 answered *capture*
(the behavioural record exists; it encodes order, not tempo) and §3.2
answered *evaluate* (the instruments are mature; every one is scored over an
attacker). §3.3 is the missing hinge between them — the design brief's
"scissors": the field knows how campaigns behave, the field can measure a
defence, and the attacker model in the middle draws on almost none of the
first.

**Rhetorical shape (ruled 2026-09-05).** Instrument → measurement →
verdict, the diagnosis stated once:

| Block | Job | Ledger |
|---|---|---|
| *(interstitial, unnumbered)* | the move: two surveys six years apart name the attacker model as the field's primary limitation; this section builds a scale from the literature they draw on and measures | ½ unit |
| **3.3.1 Attacker model criterion** | the instrument, *derived as the literature's characterisation*: Cho's three under-developed dimensions and four characteristics, Jalowski's corrective, the NIST clauses via §3.1.1 → eight properties numbered in the criterion's order; then the fidelity descriptor (parametric / scripted / procedural / behavioural), earned | 1 unit |
| **3.3.2 Attacker models in recent MTD evaluations** | the measurement: Table 3.2 scores the cross-section on the eight properties plus the descriptor; the prose carries rhetoric-versus-execution; He et al. beside the table as the adjacent contrast | 1½ units |
| **3.3.3 Research gap** | the verdict: "rationality without capability"; the means to close it exist in adjacent literatures (profiling; Bland's agent; Outkin's parameters) and have never been brought to MTD; the consequence and stop (G2) | 1 unit |

Four units, ~1 000 words. The section carries the chapter's most important
paragraph (the close), so 3.3.3 is numbered and appears in the contents (port
plan CONFIRM 4).

**What was cut on the way here, so no session reinstates it.** The

## 2. What it owes the rest of the document

§3.3 is the most-referenced section in the thesis after ch4, and the
references fix what it must carry:

- **The eight properties, numbered 1–8 in the criterion's order, never
  renumbered.** The ch4 preamble says the movement attacker "aims to meet
  the axes from Section~\ref{subsec:attacker-criterion}" (tex l.3346);
  every implementation record and experiment finding cites the axes by
  number; the ch7 fidelity verdict scores this model against them and opens
  with the scoring discipline that was cut from §4.1 (badges; axes fixed from
  the literature before the model was scored). If §3.3.1 does not derive all
  eight, ch4's opening move and ch7's verdict have no antecedent.
- **The fidelity descriptor and its word.** This model places at
  *procedural — demonstrated* (criterion §(e)); the placement only means
  something if §3.3.1 has defined the four rungs and §3.3.2 has shown the
  field at parametric and scripted. *Rung* is the descriptor's word; *ladder*
  is ch4's L0–L4 figure; Cho's four evaluation methods are *categories* or
  *methods*, never rungs (registry, 2026-09-07).
- **Table 3.2 returns in the results with one column added** (the movement
  attacker's) as the fidelity verdict's visual. Its rows, cell vocabulary and
  caption decode are therefore fixed here for two chapters, and the ch3 copy
  carries nothing of this thesis's own.
- **The gap statement ch1 compresses.** The introduction's motivation is a
  landmark-to-landmark hop through this chapter to the gap; the gap is stated
  *once*, at the end of 3.3.3, and ch1 restates it, never the reverse.
- **The "prior MTD work" column of the criterion** (`apt_model_criterion.md`
  §(c)) was written from the review's §IV-B. Once Table 3.2 is ratified it is
  the thesis's reading of the field, and the criterion's column should be
  brought to it (a durable-file edit with `updated` bumped), not the reverse
  — one reading of the field, in one place.

## 3. What it refuses to carry

- **Anything of this thesis's own.** No movement-attacker row or column, no
  finding, no "this thesis builds". The close names what would have to be
  built and stops; building it is ch4 (design brief rule 5; port plan §5).
- **The scoring discipline.** Badge vocabulary, the anti-reverse-fitting
  provenance, rows A/B: ch7's fidelity verdict opener (2026-09-04 ruling).
  §3.3.1 derives the axes as the literature's characterisation and flags the
  selection as synthesis; it does not pose them as a criterion the thesis is
  scored on.
- **The inherited attacker as implemented.** Ch2 §2.2.3 states the inherited
  attacker from the record (ATK-04: no learning; C7). §3.3 scores the
  *published* attacker models, Zhang's time model included. The two must not
  contradict each other on the page — see §5(b) below.
- **The research question and the approach** (ch1, ch4); Cho's defence
  modelling approaches (3.2.1); the evaluation methods (3.2.3); IDS as a
  thread (culled — Tay's sensitivity sweep is a clause about defender
  observability, nothing more).
- **The signature line** ("the defender has grown more sophisticated; the
  attacker against which it is evaluated has not") — spent in the preamble.
- **Forward pointers as closers.** Each unit closes on its own claim.

### 9.1 What the unit is for

One job: turn the surveys' diagnosis ("the attacker model is under-developed")
into a *named, sourced* list of what it is under-developed **in** — the eight
properties — so that 3.3.2 can measure the field against it and ch4/ch7 can
cite it by number. Two source kinds feed it and the unit has to keep them
apart: the two surveys **diagnose** (Cho V-D's three dimensions; Jalowski's
corrective) and the APT literature **describes** (Cho V-A's four
characteristics; NIST's three clauses via Alshamrani, already stated at
§3.1.1). Diagnosis + description = the property list. Everything else in the
current unit is either a duplicate of that move or belongs elsewhere.

Marc's own reading of the unit's intent matches the port plan's row 138 and
the handoff §1 table: *the literature-supplied gap, structured*. The current
text does that, but it reads as if the thesis invented the list — see 9.2.

### 9.2 The heading — Marc's worry is right, and the fix is the refusal list

"Attacker model criterion" names the **repo's** instrument
(`apt_model_criterion.md`, supervisor S6: axes + badges + rows A/B). §3 of
this file records that §3.3 *refuses* to pose that instrument: "it does not
pose them as a criterion the thesis is scored on"; the scoring discipline is
ch7's. So the heading claims exactly the thing the section's contract says it
does not do, and that is why it reads as "we made this up": *criterion* is
the thesis's word for its yardstick, and the content is the literature's
characterisation. The port plan's own heading for this row (2026-08-31) was
"What the literature says a capable attacker is" — the right content, but a
performing heading under the 2026-08-31 sharp-noun-phrase ruling.

Options (sentence case, no acronyms, topic-stating):
(a) **Properties of a sophisticated attacker** — Cho's own adjective (V-A
    "Characteristics of Advanced Attacks"; "sophisticated" throughout V);
    3.3.2's caption then reads "scored on the eight properties of
    Section 3.3.1" unchanged. RECOMMENDED.
(b) Properties of an APT attacker — narrower than the sources (Cho's V-A is
    about sophisticated attackers generally, APT as the typical instance).
(c) Attacker model properties — flat, but ambiguous between "properties an
    attacker model has" and "properties it should have".
Consequence of any of these: ch4 preamble l.3348 "aims to meet the axes from
Section 3.3.1" → *properties*; and the unit's own load-bearing sentence
"eight properties an **attack model** can be scored on" breaches registry
row 67 (*attack model* never) — *attacker model*. Registry row owed: one
word for the list thesis-wide (*property*; *axis* stays repo-side, as *rung*
does for the evaluation methods in row 82).
