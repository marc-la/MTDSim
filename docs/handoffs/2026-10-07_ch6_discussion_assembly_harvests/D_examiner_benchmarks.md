## 1. The UWA rubric, verbatim (fetched today)

Source: CITS4001 *Dissertation Marking Guide*, "Discussion" criterion, page last edited 2019 ([link](http://teaching.csse.uwa.edu.au/units/CITS4001/index.php?fname=DissertationMarkingGuide&project=yes)). Two examiners mark the dissertation. It asks six things:

1. "Has the student made clear the relationship between the results and the hypotheses and possible outcomes that were described in the Introduction?"
2. "Have all of the results been taken into account and evaluated?"
3. "Are the conclusions … justified by the data, or is the case either over-stated or logically unsound?"
4. "Have any shortcomings … been recognised?"
5. "Have appropriate suggestions been made concerning their possible improvement in future studies?"
6. "Does the student suggest any possible further work or highlight any open questions or 'loose ends' … [and] ways in which these might be further investigated or resolved?"

Two other criteria bear on ch6:

- **Results:** results "presented fairly, with no attempt to bias their interpretation".
- **General:** "well-structured", "consistent style, person and tense".

There is no separate Conclusion criterion. Items 5 and 6 are scored under *Discussion*, even though ch7 holds the future-work material.

## 3. Failure modes to avoid

1. **Results replay.** The section restates ch5 numbers without interpreting them (Hess, "overpresentation").
2. **New results.** A number or float first appears in ch6. Section 6.3's return of Table 3.3 is a re-scoring, not new data, and its caption must say so.
3. **Over-claiming.** "Fidelity causes X" where the evidence supports only "the attacker model changes X" (the mark-risk ledger's wording).
4. **Unwarranted speculation and tangents** (Hess). Any speculation must be labelled.
5. **Bully pulpit.** Attacking the baseline or the lineage. Contrast them professionally (Hess, p. 1241; `refusing_the_baseline_race.md`).
6. **No literature comparison, or comparison by numbers across papers.**
7. **Limitations as an afterthought.** A boilerplate list ("laundry list", Verdecchia), generic threats such as "results may not generalise", or mitigation by future work only.
8. **Implications that ignore 6.5.**
9. **Discussion that repeats the conclusion.** Answering the sub-questions belongs to ch7 (board §10.2).
10. **Confusing navigation.** Third-level headings, headings that are claims, terms first used in ch6, and orphan `\ref`s.

## 4. Recommended skeleton of moves per section type

**Results-discussion section (6.1, 6.2).** Open with the section's major finding in one sentence; Hess calls it a "direct, declarative" opening. Then one cycle per finding, major findings first:

- (a) restate with a `\ref` to the float;
- (b) give the mechanism;
- (c) name the alternative explanation;
- (d) compare against the baseline, or against what the lineage expected;
- (e) bound the claim (point to the 6.5 threat);
- (f) deduce, handing on to 6.4.

Use run-in labels, and keep to 3 or 4 cycles.

**Model-assessment section (6.3).**

- First state the discipline: the properties were fixed in §3.3.1 before scoring.
- Then score properties grouped by verdict: shown to change an outcome, implemented with no advantage shown, not met.
- Credit comes first, then the gaps. Cite the evidence for each mark.
- Close on what the capture licenses.
- Mirror an honest self-scoring row, as Torquato's Table VII does, not an all-ticks row.

**Implications section (6.4).**

- Open by scoping what transfers: the dependence and the method, not the magnitudes.
- Then each implication in turn: source finding, then who should change practice, then a conditional recommendation, then its bound.
- Write in the present tense and act with the tools that exist now.
- "More research" belongs to §7.2, not here.

**Threats section (6.5).**

- ¶1: the artefact's limitations as scope, each with its `\ref` to §7.2. Then the four types, each defined once and cited (Wohlin et al.; de França & Travassos 2015).
- ¶2–5: internal, construct, external, conclusion. For each threat give:
  - the threat, specific to this study;
  - the mitigation already carried out, with a `\ref`;
  - the residual, meaning what the claim becomes.
- Consolidate repeated threats that share a root; the action-set ceiling is one example. Order the types by the claim each one threatens. Keep the best-mitigated type short.
- Documented and justified assumptions are not defects in themselves (ACM Simulation standard). Say which assumption could force the outcome; de França & Travassos found that most recurrent in simulation studies.

## 5. Where the sources disagree

- **Where limitations go.**
  - Docherty & Smith (BMJ 1999) put strengths and weaknesses second, straight after the principal findings.
  - Swales & Feak, Jedlitschka et al. 2008 and Runeson & Höst 2009 put them late.
  - Marc ruled late. The ACM anti-pattern is answered by each implication carrying its own bound.
- **Numbers in the discussion.**
  - Hess's opening statement carries no data.
  - Swales & Feak and the lineage (Tay, Alavizadeh) restate the result with a back-reference.
  - Reconciled: words plus a `\ref`, and only the numbers the interpretation depends on.
- **Where future work goes.** UWA and Hess score it within the discussion; Bunton, Soler-Monreal and the field place it in the conclusion. Reconciled by the forward pointers in B11.
- **Typed threats versus "Limitations".**
  - Typed threats are the SE norm (ACM standard, Wohlin et al.).
  - The MTD and security field almost never types them: 0 of the 25 field papers, and 20 % in Schloegel et al. 2024.
  - Marc chose *Threats to validity*, so the B10 discipline applies.
- **Speculation.**
  - Hess allows little speculation, and only labelled.
  - Swales & Feak treat hedged interpretation as the register itself.
  - Both agree that a hypothesis must be labelled as one.
- **"Internal validity" means two things.** Sargent's model-validation technique of that name is not the Cook & Campbell sense. Use Cook & Campbell only.
- **Examples versus no new floats.** French's examples point (B13) can pull toward a new figure. Meet it with a named instance in the prose, or a back-referenced float, not a new results float.
