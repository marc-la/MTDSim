\begin{abstract}
Moving Target Defence (MTD) continuously shifts a system's attack
surface to invalidate an attacker's reconnaissance and raise the cost
of intrusion. Modern networks, however, are themselves dynamic---cloud,
software-defined, and IoT deployments change topology, configuration,
and workload at runtime---and these changes interact with, and can
undermine, the effectiveness of MTD. This dissertation investigates how
the inputs and realism of an AI-driven MTD (AI-MTD) module affect
defensive outcomes in a simulated network. It contributes (i) a more
capable adversarial model grounded in MITRE ATT\&CK campaigns and cyber
threat intelligence, and (ii) an exploration of integrating an
Intrusion Detection System (IDS) as an additional signal for the MTD
agent. \emph{[Replace this abstract with your final results once the
evaluation is complete.]}
\end{abstract}

\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:intro}

\section{Background and Motivation}
Traditional cyber defence is reactive: vulnerabilities are found and
patched after the fact. Moving Target Defence (MTD) inverts this by
continuously reconfiguring the system so that the information an attacker
gathers becomes stale before it can be exploited~\cite{jajodia2011mtd}.
The difficulty addressed in this work is that contemporary networks are
themselves \emph{dynamic}: topology, configuration and workload change
at runtime, and these changes alter how effective a given MTD technique
is.

\section{Problem Statement}
This project studies how the input parameters, system realism, and
attacker sophistication of an AI-driven MTD (AI-MTD) module affect
defensive outcomes in a simulated network environment.

\section{Research Aims and Questions}
The project investigates three aims: (1) evaluate the effectiveness of
MTD techniques as changes are introduced into the network; (2) measure
how effectiveness varies for different types of change; and (3) develop
adaptive MTD configuration that minimises the disruption caused by
dynamic change.

\section{Contributions}
This dissertation contributes: a more capable adversarial module built
from MITRE ATT\&CK campaigns and cyber threat intelligence
(Chapter~\ref{ch:design}); and a feasibility study of integrating an
Intrusion Detection System signal into the AI-MTD agent
(Chapter~\ref{ch:impl}).

\section{Dissertation Structure}
Chapter~\ref{ch:background} reviews MTD, dynamic networks and threat
modelling. Chapter~\ref{ch:design} presents the system and threat model
and the design of the attack and IDS modules. Chapter~\ref{ch:impl}
describes the implementation. Chapter~\ref{ch:eval} reports the
evaluation, Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion} discusses the findings, and
Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion} concludes.

\subsection{The modelled object, and why it is under-served}
\label{sec:tp-object}

The object this thesis models is not an attacker in isolation and not a
defence in isolation, but the \emph{interaction} between the two: how an
advanced, objective-driven adversary makes progress through a network whose
configuration is being deliberately and repeatedly mutated beneath it. Classic
Moving Target Defence (MTD) mutates the \emph{configuration attack surface} ---
addresses, ports, service and operating-system versions, topology --- and the
progress it is designed to spoil is the reconnaissance and exploitation that
precede a foothold: surface intelligence gathered before a shuffle is stale
after it, and a weaponised exploit aimed at a specific address or version misses
a rotated target. In kill-chain terms that reach sits \emph{pre-ingress}
(Reconnaissance, Resource Development, Initial Access). What is comparatively
under-served is everything from the foothold onwards --- the \emph{post-ingress}
campaign of persistence, escalation, credential theft, lateral movement,
collection, command-and-control, exfiltration, and impact --- where the target
is moved under the feet of an adversary who is already inside.\footnote{The
claim that the MTD technique corpus is predominantly pre-ingress in its reach is
a coverage claim about the field and is supported from an MTD taxonomy/survey in
the literature review, not asserted here.} This section defends a model of that
post-ingress interaction, tactic by tactic.

The inherited simulator prices attacker progress purely by reachability and
CVSS-scaled exploitation: its six-phase procedural adversary is
objective-agnostic and taxonomy-free, a smash-and-grab that races toward a
single target \citep{brown2023,zhang2023}. That attacker was the correct
instrument for the original MTD-comparison work and is retained here \emph{unchanged}
as the procedural baseline, because preserving it is what makes the comparison
fair. But it carries a confound that motivates the whole thesis: because the
baseline is a sprint, \emph{an MTD that merely out-paces it wins by default}, so a
verdict that ``mechanism~A beats mechanism~B'' can be an artefact of attacker
triviality rather than defensive quality. A fast, generic attacker cannot
distinguish a genuinely strong MTD from one that is simply faster than a weak
adversary. Closing that confound requires an attacker that is slow, objective-conditioned,
and grounded in observed adversary behaviour --- and that is what the tactic
profiles supply.

\subsection{The abstraction altitude, defended}
\label{sec:tp-altitude}

The behavioural unit of this model is the \emph{MITRE ATT\&CK tactic}, and the
choice of altitude is a deliberate, defensible one rather than a convenience.
Three levels were available. The \emph{technique} level is where genuine
empirical timing exists --- \citet{ling2023} assign time-to-compromise to
ATT\&CK-for-ICS techniques from CVE data --- but that method requires real
vulnerability instances to bind against, and the substrate's vulnerabilities are
synthetic, with no NVD CVE to join a technique to (\S\ref{sec:tp-boundary}); at
corpus scale it is also unexecutable. The \emph{kill-chain phase} level is
coarse enough to be groundable but too coarse to carry the behavioural
distinctions --- credential reuse versus fresh exploitation, low-and-slow dwell
versus fast verb --- on which the MTD interaction turns. The \emph{tactic} level
is the groundable middle: coarse enough that the sparse CTI corpus can populate
transition structure at tactic granularity \citep{rodriguez2024}, fine enough to
separate the behaviours that a mutation treats differently. Critically, this is
open ground. A survey of the timed-attack-model literature finds \emph{no prior
work assigns justified per-ATT\&CK-tactic durations}: the one tactic-level
ATT\&CK Petri-net model is untimed \citep{rodriguez2024}, and every timed model
that carries ATT\&CK labels attaches its timing at technique/CVE level or
declares its rates outright \citep{ling2023,xiong2021,bland2020}. A transparent,
tiered, per-tactic timing-and-reset layer is therefore a methodological artefact
the field does not currently have.

\subsection{The epistemic contract}
\label{sec:tp-epistemic}

Because the object is under-instrumented, the section commits up front to what
each profile is and is not allowed to claim. Four disciplines are load-bearing.

\begin{description}
  \item[Envelope, not actor.] Each objective-conditioned profile is the union of
    many analyst-drawn attack flows and is therefore a behavioural \emph{envelope}
    (a possibility space for an operational objective), not a faithful replay of
    any single named actor. The aggregation over-generates by construction --- a
    token may stitch one campaign's technique onto another's --- and this is
    disclosed, not corrected. ``APT'' throughout names the \emph{source-genre of
    the grounding CTI}, not a claim of full-spectrum (adaptive, detection-evading)
    fidelity.
  \item[Operational validation, tier-badged.] The unobservable per-tactic dwell
    times cannot be input-validated --- ``how long does stealth take'' is a
    micro-parameter of a model, not a measurable property of the world. Following
    the recognised strategy of operational validation / pattern-oriented
    modelling \citep{sargent,grimm}, the free parameters are calibrated so the
    model's \emph{observable output} (the emergent campaign timeline) reproduces
    literature-reported campaign patterns. Every duration is badged as one of
    three tiers: \textbf{Tier~1} input-validated by the substrate (not tuned);
    \textbf{Tier~2} calibrated against a macro observable the breach literature
    reports; \textbf{Tier~3} declared with a written justification and a swept
    range. The tier badge \emph{is} the validity claim.
  \item[Shape, not scale.] The substrate prices actions in simulated seconds; the
    literature observables live in hours-to-months. Matching absolute real-world
    timelines would collapse the timeline into pure stealth-dwell and destroy the
    substrate-comparable time-to-compromise on which the whole comparison rests.
    The literature therefore supplies \emph{relative} structure (orderings and
    ratios); absolute scale is anchored to the substrate's Tier-1 values; and the
    calibrated claim is only that the emergent timeline \emph{shape} reproduces
    reported campaign structure.
  \item[Reset declared, not evidenced.] No public logs ground the MTD-to-attacker
    effect. The substrate fixes the \emph{direction} of each reset
    (\S\ref{sec:tp-reset}); the literature bounds its \emph{width}; but the reset
    \emph{fraction} per tactic is a declared parameter, swept across a
    mechanism-bounded band. This is not a concession: declare-the-rate-then-sweep
    is the dominant practice in timed APT/MTD modelling
    \citep{bland2020,mcqueen2006,xiong2021}, and calibrating the declared values
    to macro observables goes one step further than the norm.
\end{description}

The governing claim the contract protects is therefore ``behavioural fidelity
\emph{changes the answer}'', never ``the attacker model is \emph{true}''.

\subsection{The fidelity boundary --- what is deliberately not modelled}
\label{sec:tp-boundary}

Naming the omissions is what keeps the model defensible. Four things are out of
scope at the substrate level. \emph{Real CVEs}: the substrate's vulnerabilities
are synthetic, so a technique cannot be bound to a specific vulnerability
instance and the attacker must be coded in ATT\&CK terms at the behaviour layer
only. \emph{Redundancy MTD}: despite the conventional Shuffle/Diversity/Redundancy
taxonomy, the substrate implements only Shuffle and Diversity mechanisms, so
claims about redundancy belong to the literature, not to what this substrate can
evaluate. \emph{Detection / IDS}: intrusion detection is culled from this
research thread --- ``caught'' means \emph{a mutation invalidated the attacker's
progress}, never \emph{a sensor observed the attacker} --- which has a sharp
consequence for the stealth-family tactics (\S\ref{sec:tp-stealth}). \emph{Attacker
adaptivity / learning}: the profiled attacker does not recognise a post-shuffle
state collision or learn a mutation schedule; adaptivity is the one genuinely
hard encoding and is deferred. Within these bounds the profiled adversary is a
slow, objective-conditioned, credential-and-foothold-accumulating traversal ---
and that, precisely delimited, is what ``APT'' denotes here.

\subsection{The organising spine --- the per-modality reset taxonomy}
\label{sec:tp-reset}

The substrate already implements a reset model, and that model \emph{is} the
central analytical instrument of this section:

\begin{quote}
  A defensive mutation invalidates the attacker's \emph{position/knowledge} gains
  and imposes a time penalty, but the attacker's \emph{capability/conquest} gains
  --- compromised hosts and harvested credentials --- survive it. The reset is
  partial and layered, never a clean wipe.
\end{quote}

The decisive consequence is that \textbf{the reset verdict is per-modality, never
global}: the same shuffle that erases a scanning attacker's map (its target
positions are invalid) leaves a credential-based lateral move untouched (a stolen
key is not location-bound). A tactic's verdict therefore depends on \emph{what
kind of gain the tactic produces}, and several tactics are split inside
themselves. This per-modality split --- \emph{capability and credential state
survive a mutation; network-position state is invalidated by it} --- is the
strongest, most falsifiable claim the work owns, and it is grounded in what the
substrate actually does, not merely in the MTD literature. It is also the
mechanism beneath the thesis's motivation: the gains a post-ingress campaign
banks are exactly the shuffle-durable ones, which is why surface-shifting MTD
structurally under-defends the post-ingress phase.

Every per-tactic subsection below returns an explicit verdict on a four-value
axis, and the tactics are read through it:

\begin{description}
  \item[\textsc{vulnerable}] the gain is position/knowledge; a position-mutating
    (network-layer) shuffle invalidates it and forces re-work.
  \item[\textsc{survivor}] the gain is capability/conquest; it is not location-bound
    and a shuffle leaves it intact.
  \item[\textsc{partial}] the gain is contested but not cleanly invalidated ---
    a re-planning penalty, an architected-to-survive channel, or a
    rate-dependent contest with no fixed outcome.
  \item[\textsc{null}] the tactic is off-network or otherwise never on the terrain
    the mutation touches; it contributes nothing to the interaction.
\end{description}

The genuine unknown lives in the \emph{magnitude}, not the direction: the
substrate fixes what survives and what does not, the literature bounds how much,
and the declared reset fraction is swept across that band. Where a
literature-argued verdict \emph{diverges} from what the current substrate can
exercise, that divergence is itself recorded as a finding.

\subsection{Reconnaissance \tactic{(TA0043)} --- the vulnerable pole}
\label{sec:tp-recon}

Reconnaissance produces a \emph{map}: the external attack surface of reachable
hosts, open ports, and service/OS fingerprints. Behaviourally the APT literature
places it at the patient, low-and-slow end, but ``passive'' here means
\emph{non-exploitative} rather than inactive --- \citet{alshamrani2019} list port
and service scanning, WHOIS/BGP queries, and fingerprinting among it, and
\citet{jalowski2026} sharpen the adversarial reading: APTs favour passive
reconnaissance to remain in the shadows and \emph{learn mutation patterns over
time}. The substrate meters reconnaissance as an active scan verb, priced by the
inherited \texttt{ATTACK\_DURATION} constant, so it is a Tier-1, not-tuned action
(group \emph{scan-shaped}, $\times 1.0$); the patient, externally-staged
character of real recon is a recorded shape-not-scale divergence in \emph{tempo},
folded into off-network preparation, not a separately-metered dwell.

Read against the reset model, reconnaissance is the canonical \textsc{vulnerable}
tactic --- the opposite pole to credential access. A position-mutating shuffle
re-addresses and re-links the terrain the map described, throwing the accumulated
reconnaissance away and forcing a re-scan; this is
\citeauthor{alshamrani2019}'s ``the rearrangement of network or software
components renders the exploratory knowledge of the attacker useless'' made
concrete. \emph{How hard} the shuffle bites sets the sweep width and is bounded by
the scan-disruption literature: a perfect shuffle caps scan success at
$e^{-1}\approx 0.63$ where vulnerable hosts are sparse, but the effect rides a
mutation-rate\,$\div$\,scan-rate ratio, so the magnitude spans near-total to
modest. \textbf{Verdict: \textsc{vulnerable}; direction firm, sweep wide.} One
structural caveat tempers it: exposed endpoints are never mutated, so
reconnaissance of the \emph{perimeter} (the route in) survives; it is the interior
map that resets. \emph{Not captured}: the schedule-learning recon the literature
attributes to APTs --- the substrate cannot represent an attacker who times its
scans around the mutation cadence (the adaptivity deferral), so the modelled reset
is a worst case for a naive scanner.

\subsection{Resource Development \tactic{(TA0042)} --- the inert null}
\label{sec:tp-resdev}

Resource development --- acquiring or compromising the infrastructure, accounts,
and tooling that back an intrusion --- is the sharpest group boundary in the set
and the section's one \emph{inert} tactic. All nine of its parent techniques are
staged off the victim network before the foothold exists \citep{alshamrani2019},
and \citet{selmanaj2024} note it ``takes place outside of the company's protection
and control''. Whatever weeks or months of tool-building it consumes precede the
simulator's clock: the adversary arrives already equipped. Its in-simulation dwell
is therefore near-zero (group \emph{prep-off-network}, $\times 0$, Tier-3 declared
as an off-clock modelling decision). Its reset verdict is a \textsc{null}: the gain
is built and held off-network, reset-immune, on neither pole of the axis, because
it is never on the terrain the axis describes. The honest move --- per the rubric's
discrimination criterion --- is to record that resource development contributes
\emph{nothing} to the MTD-interaction finding rather than manufacture a verdict for
it. Its whole value to the model is as the documented boundary of \emph{where the
MTD interaction begins}.

\subsection{Initial Access \tactic{(TA0001)} --- the foothold survives, the attempt does not}
\label{sec:tp-initaccess}

Initial access is the hinge from off-network preparation into the estate and the
first place the substrate's exploit model engages. The server-side path (Exploit
Public-Facing Application) is exactly what the complexity-scaled \texttt{exploit\_time}
prices, so the tactic is \emph{exploit-shaped}, Tier-1, $\times 1.0$, not tuned;
the phishing/watering-hole half carries a behaviour the exploit model does not ---
after delivery the attacker ``patiently waits'' for a user to trigger execution
\citep{alshamrani2019} --- recorded as a shape divergence rather than a metered
dwell.

The gain splits by when one looks. The \emph{achieved} foothold is a capability
possession that \textsc{survives}: a compromised ingress host stays owned, and ---
the structural fact that makes initial access unusually reset-robust --- exposed
endpoints are never mutated, so the route in is a permanent fixture the MTD cannot
take away. An \emph{in-progress} entry attempt against a public-facing application,
by contrast, is a surface-dependent action a diversity shuffle can reset,
disrupting a fresh exploit while leaving a valid-accounts entry (a survivor
credential path) untouched \citep{evans2011}. \textbf{Verdict: achieved foothold
\textsc{survivor}; in-progress exploit disruptable by surface diversity only; sweep
narrow-to-moderate.} \emph{Not captured}: the human-triggered delivery-wait, which
sits outside the metered action and is subject to no reset at all.

\subsection{Execution \tactic{(TA0002)} --- a fast verb in a stealth wrapper}
\label{sec:tp-execution}

Execution is the one tactic whose timing group is left open on purpose. The
literature couples it to concealment --- the attacker ``keep[s] low to go
undetected'', and fileless in-memory execution is chosen to evade
\citep{alshamrani2019} --- which reads low-and-slow; yet mechanically ``run code''
is a brief, cross-cutting verb underlying most other tactics. The reconciliation
is that any low-and-slow character is inherited from the \emph{stealth wrapper and
inter-execution spacing}, not from the act, so the central estimate leans to the
fast-verb reading ($\times 0.5$ of the stealth anchor) while the widest sweep among
the enabling tactics ($\times 0.1$--$\times 2$, skewed low) keeps both readings live
until the runner shows which the timeline needs. This is a Tier-3 declared value in
the fullest sense: neither substrate nor corpus pins it.

The reset side reinforces the width. Execution's gain is a fleeting capability act,
and its verdict turns on a classification the profile deliberately leaves open. By
\citeauthor{evans2011}'s per-attack-class taxonomy, dynamic diversity gives
\emph{no} advantage against circumvention/deputy attacks --- which is what
APT-preferred fileless execution is --- and bites only an incremental-probing
(fresh-exploit) execution, and then only at a very high re-randomisation rate
\citep{evans2011}. \textbf{Verdict: \textsc{partial} --- largely immune for the
circumvention reading, disruptable for the probing reading; sweep wide to span the
two.} \emph{Not captured}: the substrate does not distinguish execution modalities,
so execution's reset is effectively inherited from the tactic it serves, and the
memory-layer diversity that would (weakly) bite a probing execution is not among the
implemented mechanisms.

\subsection{Persistence \tactic{(TA0003)} --- the open contest}
\label{sec:tp-persistence}

Persistence is the durability layer beneath the whole post-compromise campaign ---
the mechanism by which a gain survives the ordinary churn of a live environment ---
and the literature confirms its low-and-slow character decisively: multi-host
backdoors and valid credentials that are ``very difficult to push out'', pre-OS
bootkits ``loaded before the operating system'', espionage dwell running to months
\citep{alshamrani2019,cho2020}. It takes the reference stealth multiplier
($\times 1.0$, Tier-3 declared --- no substrate verb prices ``maintain a
foothold'').

Its reset verdict is the section's foremost \emph{open contest}, and it is
presented as one rather than resolved. The naive reading is \textsc{survivor}: a
compromised host stays owned. But \citet{flipit2013} frame foothold survival as a
\emph{rate contest} in which the higher-move-cost player has benefit zero and
whoever moves faster and cheaper controls the resource --- so whether a periodic
mutation evicts an entrenched foothold has no fixed answer; it flips on the
defender-move-rate\,$\div$\,attacker-re-compromise-rate ratio. This is also the
dwell that most directly drives the MTD comparison \citep{cho2020}, so getting its
uncertainty right matters more than for most tactics. \textbf{Verdict: contested
\textsc{survivor} --- foreground as a rate-dependent open question; sweep wide
($\times 0.25$--$\times 4$), driven by the rate contest, not duration alone.} A
load-bearing divergence is recorded here: the substrate implements no
eviction/reimaging operation, so it currently models persistence at the survivor
\emph{extreme} of the contest, and the reset-fraction sweep encodes the contest the
substrate does not yet run.

\subsection{Privilege Escalation \tactic{(TA0004)} --- bimodal, like its siblings}
\label{sec:tp-privesc}

Privilege escalation produces an \emph{elevated-permission state}: a gating
capability that unlocks an admin-only technique subset, formalised by
\citet{xiong2021} as a permission gate a \texttt{userRights} adversary must
``level up'' past. Its canonical instance is vulnerability exploitation (Stuxnet's
two Windows zero-days to gain full control, \citealp{alshamrani2019}), which the
substrate prices via \texttt{exploit\_time} --- so it is \emph{exploit-shaped},
Tier-1, $\times 1.0$, not tuned --- while the token/valid-account variant is a fast
reuse-of-material act noted as a within-group skew.

The reset is bimodal, patterning with its sibling exploit-shaped tactics. The
token/valid-account path is a capability possession that \textsc{survives} a shuffle
(an elevated token is not location-bound); the exploit path is a surface-dependent
attempt a diversity shuffle can reset mid-exploit, bounded by \citeauthor{maleki2016}'s
observation that MTD-defeat probability rises with attacker time and cost
\citep{maleki2016}. \textbf{Verdict: \textsc{partial}/bimodal --- token variant
survivor (narrow), exploit variant disruptable (moderate); overall moderate.}
\emph{Not captured}: the substrate does not model the gating state distinctly, so
privilege escalation's reset in the simulator is really the reset of its underlying
exploit or credential action; its distinctive contribution (the permission gate) is
a modelled precondition the binding carries, not a resettable substrate gain.

\subsection{Stealth \tactic{(TA0005)} --- the tactic the substrate cannot see}
\label{sec:tp-stealth}

Stealth is the anchor of the low-and-slow group and the tactic from which it takes
its name: \citeauthor{alshamrani2019}'s ``low and slow approach'',
\citeauthor{cho2020}'s attackers who ``stay stealthy until the time comes''
\citep{alshamrani2019,cho2020}. Concealment is less an action with a duration than
a \emph{tempo} imposed on every other action, so its modelled duration represents
the spacing it imposes (reference $\times 1.0$, Tier-3, widest reliance on the
shape-not-scale discipline because its absolute dwell is the least observable of
all).

Its reset verdict is dominated by the single largest omission in the model:
detection is culled from the substrate, where ``caught'' means a mutation
invalidated progress, never that a sensor saw the attacker. Because there is no
detector to hide from, stealth's gain has \emph{no direct substrate representation}
--- it is modelled as spacing, not as a state. Where the literature does ground an
MTD effect it is a \textsc{partial}, re-planning reset: a detector reshuffle forces a
stealthy attacker to re-plan detector-free paths, with cost rising with shuffle
frequency and falling with attacker skill. \textbf{Verdict: \textsc{partial}
(re-planning penalty); sweep wide.} This is the section's cleanest examiner
exposure and is stated openly: the interesting MTD-versus-stealth contest lives in
the detection regime the substrate does not model, so what the simulator computes
for this tactic is a proxy (spacing re-work), and the concealment gain, riding an
owned foothold, otherwise survives a shuffle in the substrate's own terms.

\subsection{Defense Impairment \tactic{(TA0112)} --- host-local, and doubly uncertain}
\label{sec:tp-defimpair}

Defense impairment --- the disabling half split from the old Defense Evasion tactic
--- carries the widest sweep in the study, because two uncertainties compound.
First, its \emph{group} is unresolved: the low-and-slow APT the corpus documents
prefers to \emph{avoid} defences rather than \emph{disable} them
\citep{alshamrani2019}, and disabling, when it happens (kill EDR, stop backups), is
a punctuated, decisive act immediately before a noisy payload --- pulling the tactic
toward the fast/objective-adjacent end, so the central multiplier leans fast
($\times 0.5$) and the band spans $\times 0.1$--$\times 4$. Second, its reset
\emph{direction} is clear but not exercised: a disabled control is host-local
software state, so it patterns with the \textsc{survivor} pole --- an IP or topology
shuffle re-addresses the host but leaves the killed control exactly as it was; only a
reimage/reprovision restores it. \textbf{Verdict: \textsc{survivor} under shuffle,
invalidated only by reprovision; sweep widest in the set.} \emph{Not captured}, and
this is the dominant caveat: the substrate represents no defensive-control state on
hosts at all, so the survivor verdict is a conceptual, thesis-level statement of the
direction the interaction \emph{would} take were defences modelled, not something the
simulator computes today; in the data the tactic appears as \emph{prevalence, not
dwell}, which is itself why it stays Tier-3.

\subsection{Credential Access \tactic{(TA0006)} --- the survivor pole, the crown jewel}
\label{sec:tp-credaccess}

Credential access produces the archetypal capability gain and is the profile's
strongest genuinely-owned claim. Behaviourally it is the heart of the post-foothold
pivot --- ``dumping credentials is the most common chosen method for lateral
movement'' \citep{alshamrani2019} --- and its dumping path is on-host work the
substrate can price (\emph{exploit-shaped}, Tier-1, $\times 1.0$), with fast
token/ticket theft as a within-group skew.

Its §3 property is load-bearing. A harvested credential is a \emph{standing
possession}, not a position on the map: a position-mutating shuffle erases the
attacker's map but a stolen credential authenticates against whichever host now
answers, and a surface-mutating shuffle rewrites versions but not the validity of a
captured secret. So the \emph{same shuffle that resets a scanning tactic does nothing
to a credential} --- the clearest \textsc{survivor} in the set, the exact opposite of
reconnaissance and discovery. The only family that bites is credential rotation, which
the substrate holds in reserve rather than in the default roster, and even that is
\emph{leaky}: rotation revokes a captured credential in only a minority of cases
(41\% offline, 17\% online, \citealp{zhangMonroseReiter2010}). \textbf{Verdict:
\textsc{survivor}; direction unusually firm, reset band narrow ($\approx 0$).} The
discrimination payoff (criterion 7) is direct and is why this tactic matters most:
the generic sprint reuses credentials only opportunistically, whereas a
credential-first low-and-slow profile leans on exactly the modality MTD cannot reset
--- the behaviour most likely to separate a genuinely strong MTD from one that merely
out-paces a weak attacker.

\subsection{Discovery \tactic{(TA0007)} --- reconnaissance's interior twin}
\label{sec:tp-discovery}

Discovery is mechanically the same host/service/network enumeration as
reconnaissance, run from inside a foothold, and inherits the same \emph{scan-shaped},
Tier-1, $\times 1.0$ anchor. It produces an internal map, and because that gain is
\emph{purely} positional --- with no survivor sub-modality, unlike lateral movement
--- it is squarely \textsc{vulnerable} and, indeed, more cleanly so than any other
tactic. A position-mutating shuffle invalidates the internal map wholesale and throws
the attacker back to re-enumeration; in a discrete-event contest a mutation interval
short enough drives a scan-first agent's win probability to zero, because it is
``forced to restart his scan'' each interval, and the \emph{thorough} enumerator is
hit hardest (the more complete the map, the more a shuffle destroys). \textbf{Verdict:
\textsc{vulnerable}; sweep wide, magnitude on the mutation-interval\,$\div$\,scan-cadence
ratio.} Surface diversity is largely orthogonal here --- discovery enumerates
reachability and structure, not a specific exploit working set --- so it is the
topology/address family, not OS/service diversity, that resets it. \emph{Not
captured}: the open-ended ``position for future'' watching mode, and the
schedule-aware watcher who re-discovers strategically rather than blindly restarting.

\subsection{Lateral Movement \tactic{(TA0008)} --- the split inside a single tactic}
\label{sec:tp-lateral}

Lateral movement is the showcase for the per-modality reset split --- the crown-jewel
finding located inside one tactic --- and its treatment is built around that. Its
dominant form is a remote-service login or exploitation the substrate prices
(\emph{exploit-shaped}, Tier-1, $\times 1.0$), but its behavioural character is wide
enough (a patient manual pivot at one end, an automatic worm sweep at the other,
\citealp{alshamrani2019}) to justify a wide sweep.

The reset splits on \emph{how} a hop moves. A \emph{scan-based hop} (exploitation of
remote services, or worm-style target discovery) produces a target map and is
\textsc{vulnerable}: a mutated address space leaves a scanner under 1\% valid
addresses. A \emph{credential-based hop} (pass-the-hash/ticket, valid accounts) rides
a survivor capability, so the \emph{same shuffle that kills the scan-worm leaves the
credential-move untouched}. \textbf{Verdict: per-modality --- scan hop
\textsc{vulnerable} (wide sweep), credential hop \textsc{survivor} (narrow sweep).}
And which modality dominates is \emph{objective-conditioned}: a credential-first
campaign is effectively reset-immune in this tactic, a scan/exploit-first one is
highly disruptable. That is the discrimination the whole thesis turns on --- an MTD
that beats the substrate's scan-based baseline hop may lose against a
credential-driven low-and-slow profile that never exposes the resettable modality.
\emph{Not captured}: an attacker that recognises a post-shuffle state collision and
re-routes (adaptivity, deferred).

\subsection{Collection \tactic{(TA0009)} --- modality-split on where the data lives}
\label{sec:tp-collection}

Collection is where the campaign begins to act on its purpose --- gather, stage, and
package data before theft \citep{alshamrani2019} --- so it joins the tuned
\emph{objective-execution} group ($\times 1.0$, Tier-2, because the breach literature
characterises the collect-then-exfiltrate chain even without a per-tactic dwell). It
has a genuine dwell floor unrelated to reset: objective actions ``cannot go any faster,
since they rely on human activity, data throughput, or other fairly rigid time
frames'', which keeps its sweep moderate rather than wide.

Its reset is modality-split, on the same axis as lateral movement, and the split falls
on where the data lives. A \emph{host-local read} produces a capability possession once
staged --- the data sits on an owned host and \textsc{survives} a shuffle, exactly as a
harvested credential does (unsurprising, since collection and credential access share
the same capture instrumentation). A \emph{remote-share read} depends on reachability to
another host, so a topology/address shuffle can invalidate the route mid-collection ---
\textsc{vulnerable}. \textbf{Verdict: per-modality --- local read survivor, remote-share
read vulnerable; overall moderate-to-wide.} \emph{Not captured}: the substrate has no
first-class ``collected data'' object, so the split is proxied through
foothold-durability versus topology-governed reachability, and the modality distinction
is an attacker-side behaviour the binding must carry.

\subsection{Command and Control \tactic{(TA0011)} --- architected to survive}
\label{sec:tp-c2}

Command and control is a persistent, low-signal channel maintained continuously from
shortly after the foothold through to exfiltration --- a ``long-term connection''
beaconing on a regular cadence \citep{alshamrani2019} --- so it takes the stealth anchor
($\times 1.0$, Tier-3; CVE-based timing structurally cannot price it, since using common
ports ``is not exploiting a vulnerability'', \citealp{ling2023}). The MTD-relevant rate
is not the channel lifetime but the \emph{beacon interval} (seconds to hours, tunable),
which is what an MTD move-interval competes against.

C2 is the section's \emph{secondary open contest}, and its distinguishing property is
that the channel is \emph{architected to survive connection loss}: fallback channels,
proxy/CDN-fronting, and domain-generation resolution exist precisely so that losing one
route does not sever control \citep{selmanaj2024}. An IP/topology shuffle therefore
\emph{degrades but may not sever} the channel. \textbf{Verdict: \textsc{partial}
(architected survival); sweep wide, on the switch-interval\,$\div$\,beacon-cadence ratio
traded against switch cost.} The divergence worth recording runs the \emph{opposite} way
to the credential/persistence survivors: the substrate would model a C2 route more simply
than architected reality, so a modelled shuffle \emph{over-resets} C2 relative to the
literature --- here the substrate is harsher on the attacker than reality. \emph{Not
captured}: the fallback/proxy/DGA resilience itself; air-gapped or C2-optional campaigns
route around the channel entirely \citep{chemat2024}, which objective-conditioning can
express as a campaign that simply does not depend on the resettable gain.

\subsection{Exfiltration \tactic{(TA0010)} --- the reset it inherits, the tradecraft that blunts it}
\label{sec:tp-exfil}

Exfiltration is the terminal act of a data-theft campaign, an objective (like impact)
executed low-and-slow (like stealth). It joins the \emph{objective-execution} group
($\times 1.0$, Tier-2) --- access-to-exfiltration is one of the few milestones the breach
literature reports, and it is used here as a \emph{held-out} observable for operational
validation, calibrated against a different pattern than the duration was fitted to. Its
sweep is wide, spanning a fast bulk burst to a slow fragmented trickle, with a throughput
floor at the bottom.

Its reset is inherited from command and control: because exfiltration over the C2 channel
rides the C2 route, a position-mutating shuffle that disturbs that route interrupts an
\emph{in-progress} transfer --- a \textsc{partial} reset, blunted by C2's architected
resilience --- while the already-staged data (a survivor possession) and the off-network
destination are untouched. Crucially, exfiltration's own tradecraft \emph{is} an
adaptation to disruption: the careful attacker ``intelligently splits the data
exfiltration into batches and to servers with different IP addresses''
\citep{alshamrani2019}, so a single shuffle resets at most one batch, not the campaign.
\textbf{Verdict: \textsc{partial} (inherits C2 survival; staged data survives; batching
blunts it); sweep wide.} \emph{Not captured}: the batched, IP-diversified spread that
would blunt the reset in reality is an attacker-side behaviour the binding must encode,
not a substrate primitive.

\subsection{Impact \tactic{(TA0040)} --- blast-radius limiting, not gain invalidation}
\label{sec:tp-impact}

Impact is where a campaign spends its accumulated access, and its dwell is the most
bimodal in the set, split by actor type: a ransomware or wiper actor's impact is a fast
decisive burst (encryption is throughput-bound at minutes to hours), while an espionage
``position for future'' campaign may \emph{never reach impact at all}
\citep{alshamrani2019,alsada2024}. It joins the \emph{objective-execution} group
($\times 1.0$, Tier-2), with the widest sweep in the set and the ``never'' end expressed
\emph{structurally} --- the tactic is simply absent from those objective-nets --- rather
than as a zero duration.

Impact is the one tactic where MTD acts as \emph{blast-radius limiting rather than gain
invalidation}, a distinct reset mechanism worth naming in its own right. Its gain is an
irreversible act: a shuffle mid-campaign cannot ``reset'' an already-encrypted host. What
it can do is limit \emph{how many further hosts} the impact reaches ---
\citeauthor{barach2026}'s runtime-surface MTD cuts encryption reach to 13.2\% by blocking
lateral ransomware spread \citep{barach2026}. So the split maps onto the axis via
\emph{reachability}: the act on a held host \textsc{survives}, but the \emph{spread} to
new hosts is \textsc{vulnerable}, because it needs the same reachability a position-mutating
shuffle invalidates. \textbf{Verdict: act on a held host survives; spread reset-vulnerable
(blast-radius limiting); sweep wide.} \emph{Not captured}: the substrate does not model
data destruction as an irreversible state change, so impact ``reach'' is proxied entirely
by the reachability a topology shuffle governs. Objective-conditioning is sharpest here:
inert for espionage profiles, decisive for ransomware ones --- a genuine discriminator.

\subsection{Synthesis --- the discriminator, and what remains to be demonstrated}
\label{sec:tp-synthesis}

Read across the fifteen tactics, the per-modality reset axis resolves into a single,
mechanism-grounded claim (Table~\ref{tab:tp-verdicts}). The tactics whose gain is
network position --- reconnaissance, discovery, the scan hop of lateral movement, the
remote-share read of collection, the spread of impact --- are \textsc{vulnerable}: a
surface-shifting mutation is precisely what invalidates them. The tactics whose gain is
capability or credential --- credential access above all, the achieved foothold of initial
access, the disabled control of defence impairment, the credential hop of lateral
movement, the local read of collection --- \textsc{survive} that same mutation untouched.
The remainder are \textsc{partial}: contested by rate (persistence), architected to survive
(command-and-control, exfiltration), or a re-planning penalty (stealth, execution). This is
why classic MTD structurally under-defends the post-ingress campaign: the gains a
post-ingress adversary banks are the shuffle-durable ones, so an MTD tuned to spoil
pre-ingress reconnaissance and exploitation is aimed at the wrong half of the kill chain
for an adversary who is already inside.

A distinction must be drawn honestly, because it is the first thing an examiner will
press. Not every verdict in Table~\ref{tab:tp-verdicts} is something the current simulator
\emph{computes}; some are verdicts on the direction the interaction \emph{would} take were
the relevant state modelled. The \textsc{vulnerable} verdicts and the credential/foothold
\textsc{survivor} verdicts are exercised directly: the substrate already resets network
position and already spares compromised hosts and harvested credentials, so those tactics
inherit their direction from what the code does. Four verdicts are instead
\emph{conceptual} at present --- defence impairment (the substrate models no
defensive-control state to disable), the data-object half of collection (no first-class
``collected data'' object), the irreversibility of impact (no destructive state change),
and the eviction extreme of persistence (no reimage operation) --- and each is flagged as
such in its own ``not captured'' sentence rather than smuggled into the table as though the
simulator produced it. The methodological position this takes is deliberate: the substrate
fixes the reset \emph{direction} it can exercise, the profiles argue the direction from
mechanism where it cannot, and the declared-and-swept reset fraction is the single knob
through which both reach the evaluation --- so a conceptual verdict is a stated hypothesis
about a fraction's sign, never a claim the simulator has already demonstrated it.

The section makes one further honest disclosure, because the argument would be
incomplete without it. Everything above \emph{defends} a result; it does not yet
\emph{produce} one. Two objections must be answered by the implementation and evaluation
chapters, not by this prose. First, the profiled attacker's distinction from the generic
one rests on two invented parameter families --- a per-tactic dwell and a per-tactic reset
fraction --- and if those were stripped, nothing would separate the two attackers; the
defence is that declare-and-sweep is the field norm, that the sweep \emph{direction} is
mechanism-bounded rather than free, and that the per-modality split is real, but this is
only realised once the reset fractions are wired into the binding and the sweep is run.
Second, ``fidelity changes the answer'' is mere parameter noise unless the ranking change
it induces \emph{survives its own sweep band} and is \emph{distinct from the generic
attacker's (also stable) ranking}. The single experiment that discharges both is the
discrimination probe followed by the full sensitivity sweep: show that the
profiled-attacker MTD ranking is stable across its uncertainty band and differs from the
generic attacker's. Until that runs, the central finding is, by the project's own
standard, ``declared, not evidenced''. Stating that boundary here is what entitles the
section to its strong claim: the model is built so that, \emph{if} the ranking moves, the
move is attributable to behavioural fidelity rather than to attacker triviality --- which
is the contribution the thesis sets out to make.
