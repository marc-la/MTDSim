# Literature review conventions: what a literature review chapter is for, and how it fails

**Status:** durable. Load this before drafting, scrutinising or cutting chapter 3.
It is the chapter-level yardstick. [`background_conventions.md`](background_conventions.md)
already holds the lean test, the float rules and the ch2/ch3 split; they apply here
unchanged. [`voice.md`](voice.md) §(0) and §(c)9–10 govern the sentences, criteria
before judgement, and circularity. [`connective_prose.md`](connective_prose.md)
governs the opener and the close. [`academic_register.md`](academic_register.md)
§(b)2–§(c) set the person and the reporting tense. [`../implementation/apt_model_criterion.md`](../implementation/apt_model_criterion.md)
§(a) fixed the axes before anything was scored. This file does not repeat them.

## (a) What the chapter is for

- **It argues from the literature that the problem is not yet solved.** The mistake
  is to "merely 'report' or 'describe' previous studies"; instead, "interweave
  various studies to build up the argument that the problem… is not yet solved"
  (Evans, Gruba & Zobel 2014, p. 78). The literature gives "a rationale for the
  research… and criteria against which to judge the contribution of the thesis"
  (Golding, Sharmini & Lazarovitch 2014, §7).
- **Three jobs:** context, "attempts by others to solve similar problems", and their
  methods (EGZ p. 74). In CS this is "a precise analysis of previous work that
  explains… how existing methods differ… and what their respective strengths and
  weaknesses are". That analysis "shapes what the readers expect of your work"
  (Zobel 2004 p. 147): it sets the bar chapters 5–6 are read against.
- **It ends on the gap, and the gap leads to the question.** "The background
  chapters should lead to the gap (or gaps)… the thesis… is aiming to fill"
  (Paltridge & Starfield 2007, p. 100; EGZ p. 82).
- **Honours is marked for analysis, not exhaustive coverage.** Hart's gradient runs
  from a bachelor review that is "essentially descriptive", through a masters review
  that is "analytical and summative, covering methodological issues", to a PhD
  "analytical synthesis" (P&S p. 100, Table 7.1). UWA asks the review to "summarise
  and criticise the existing work to identify research gaps" (CSSE literature review
  marking guidelines, criterion 1).

## (b) What the two readers expect

- **The student** follows the story without knowing the field's papers, so each
  work's vocabulary is translated into the dissertation's terms. A review placed late
  in a paper can present its sources "in a consistent terminology, even when the cited
  papers have differing nomenclature" (Zobel p. 147). A review placed before the
  method has to impose that terminology itself. The top rubric level "discussed and
  resolved ambiguities in definitions" (Boote & Beile 2005, Table 1, criterion E).
- **The examiner** judges four things here:
  - *First impression.* It forms "often by the end of the literature review" (Mullins
    & Kiley 2002, p. 377). A weak review sends the examiner back to check the methods
    (Boote & Beile p. 6). Holbrook et al. call the review a "litmus test" (via Golding
    et al. §7).
  - *Pass versus good.* "A pass requires coverage and a working understanding of the
    literature; but, for a good thesis, a thesis candidate must critically engage"
    (Golding et al. §7, citing Holbrook et al. 2007, p. 353). Edinburgh counts
    "critical evaluation of previous work" among its *additional* criteria, above
    the basic ones (DISS, *Project Assessment*). Examiners want "a literature review
    that tells a story" (Mullins & Kiley p. 379).
  - *Argument.* UWA asks for "any logical flaws… in the arguments leading up to the
    generation of the experimental issues" (CITS4001 dissertation marking guide).
  - *Balance and selection.* Examiners notice major contributions "neglected, or their
    significance downplayed" (EGZ p. 77). "The onus is on… candidates to convince
    their readers that they have thoroughly mined the existing literature and
    purposefully decided what to review" (Boote & Beile p. 7).

## (c) Structure

1. **Themes at the top, a chronology inside each theme, a funnel overall.** No source
   prescribes one arrangement: "There is no single 'right way'" (P&S p. 101). UWA
   lists chronological, classic-studies, thematic and inverted-pyramid arrangements
   (criterion 5). Kwan (2006) found doctoral reviews divided into thematic sections,
   each repeating a move structure like a thesis introduction's. The README's
   "chronological story" is the order *within* a theme: "a historical discussion
   outlining the development of the key ideas" (Zobel p. 143).
2. **By idea, not by paper.** A student stalled on 120 papers wrote her review once
   it was organised around the four ideas that had moved the field on (EGZ p. 44,
   pp. 80–81). "Write about the ideas… rather than… about people or articles"
   (Golding 2017, §7).
3. **Each section closes on its limitation; the chapter closes on the gap.** EGZ put
   the summary of "the main points that are still troublesome" after the engagement
   (p. 78). The close names the need and hands over, without pre-empting chapter 4
   (chapter_scrutiny §7).
4. **Criteria, then scoring, then gap, then hand-over.** Each comes before the step
   that uses it.
5. **Rewritten after the work.** A review written before the work "should not appear
   in the final thesis in that form" (EGZ p. 80). The ledger gives 3 000 words in
   12 units, and no source gives a share of the thesis. UWA's 5 000–6 000 words
   belong to the separate review assessment.

## (d) Failure modes

| Failure | Test | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Catalogue ("brain dump")** | Does a paragraph give one work per sentence, with no comparison between them? | EGZ p. 39 ("a compendium of abstracts… misses the point"); Ernst ("a list of other papers… lifted from its abstract") |
| **He said / she said** | Does the idea carry each field-level claim, with the citation after it? The author is the subject only where that work's own act matters, which is voice.md §(d)'s case | Golding et al. §7 ("a list of who said what"); Golding 2017 §7; P&S pp. 107–108 |
| **Uncritical reporting** | Does each work get a sentence of evaluation? The lowest rubric level is "accepted literature at face value" | Boote & Beile, Table 1, criterion G; P&S p. 100 ("which are the best studies, and why") |
| **Gap asserted, not shown** | Can the gap sentence point at the rows and survey findings that establish it? | Golding et al. §7 ("use the literature as evidence"); Boote & Beile, criterion B |
| **Gap by omission** | Would an unsampled work close the gap? Is the selection rule stated, and the claim scoped to the sample? | Boote & Beile p. 7 (thin searching "led to many erroneous claims about the state of knowledge") |
| **Strawman or imbalance** | Is each work credited with what it achieved before its limitation? Is the limitation judged against this problem, not as a verdict on the work? | EGZ p. 77; Golding 2017 §7 ("not in relation to some universal standard"); Zobel p. 255, exercise 6 |
| **Not connected to the method** | Is each named property taken up later, whether built, disclaimed or measured? Is the chapter 5 baseline shown to be representative? | EGZ p. 78 (the baseline is "the best that is currently available"); Boote & Beile, criteria H–I |
| **Stale** | Is the newest work in each theme current? | P&S p. 99 ("right up to their date of examination"); Zobel p. 209 |
| **Over-quotation** | Is each quotation there because its exact words are the evidence (a stated assumption)? | Cambridge ("unnecessary to quote at length"); Zobel pp. 217–218 |
| **Gap before evidence** | Does the opener say only *where* the gap is drawn? | EGZ p. 78; connective_prose.md |
| **Second-hand citation** | Was each claim read in the cited work, not in a survey that cites it? | Zobel pp. 20–21, p. 164 ("read it first") |
| **Own work leaking in** | Is anything here built or chosen by the dissertation? If so, is it marked as the dissertation's own? | EGZ p. 81; background_conventions §(d) |
| **Borrowed criteria** | Does each criterion carry its source? Is the merge declared as the dissertation's own synthesis? | Zobel pp. 218–219 (describing literature "with respect to the same criteria" as another paper is plagiarism) |

## (e) Floats: the scoring table and the rest

The scoring table carries the chapter's argument, so it answers to more than
background_conventions §(e). The model is Bonneau et al.'s comparative table (2012).

1. **Criteria are sourced, defined and fixed in their own float before any scoring.**
   Bonneau et al. chose criteria "without hidden agenda", and disclosed that they were
   "iteratively refined over the evaluation" (§I). If the criteria moved during
   scoring, say so.
2. **Rows are chosen by a stated rule.** Boote & Beile's criterion A covers this, and
   p. 7 lists Bruce's tests: topicality, comprehensiveness, breadth, exclusion,
   relevance, currency, availability, authority.
3. **Marks are coarse, and the partial mark is defined in the text.** Bonneau's
   "Quasi-" means "almost offers the benefit, but not quite" (§II). Finer scales risk
   being "precisely wrong" (§V-D). No definitions in captions (the 2026-09-22 ruling).
4. **No aggregate.** "We have resisted the temptation to produce an aggregate
   score… (e.g., by counting the number of benefits achieved)" (§V-D). The gap is
   argued from *which* properties are absent and what their absence costs.
5. **Every cell is traceable:** a locator in the scored work and a reason in the
   prose. "Scoring on second-hand data inferred from papers" is a named danger
   (§V-D).
6. **Work the author is tied to gets the same rule.** The lineage this thesis extends
   is scored like the other rows. Bonneau et al., rating schemes they co-authored:
   "We invite readers to verify that we have rated them impartially" (§I, fn. 1).
7. **The table is checked column by column.** Bonneau et al. cross-checked within
   columns to calibrate ratings across schemes (§V-D). A sole author does the same by
   reading each property down every row.
8. **The thesis's own model has no row here.** It returns in §6.3; scoring it in
   chapter 3 would put the dissertation's design into the review (EGZ p. 81).

**Other floats.** A synthesis table (metric families × sources) earns its place only
if a sentence draws a conclusion from it; otherwise it is the catalogue in grid form
(Boote & Beile, criterion G). An exemplar figure shows what the argued-about
representation carries and lacks. A reproduced figure carries its source, since
copying or imitating another's illustration unacknowledged is plagiarism (Zobel
p. 217). A figure the dissertation built is marked as its own.

## (f) The checklist, run per chapter pass

1. The opener says what the chapter argues and where the gap is drawn. It does not
   state the gap.
2. The top-level sections are ideas, not papers. Within each, the order is method,
   its achievement, its limitation, then the next method.
3. Each section closes on the limitation that moves the story toward the gap.
4. Every cited work gets one sentence of evaluation against this problem, crediting
   what it achieved.
5. Field-level claims are made with citations. No paragraph is a run of one-work
   summaries.
6. Each work's terms are translated into the dissertation's terms at first use.
7. Every sampled set states its selection rule. Claims are scoped to the sample, and
   field-level claims rest on the surveys that make them.
8. The criteria are sourced and fixed in their own float before scoring. The merge
   is declared as the dissertation's own synthesis.
9. Every scored cell has a locator and a prose reason. The partial mark is defined,
   and no cell count stands in for the argument.
10. Works the thesis is tied to are scored by the same rule as every other work.
11. The gap states what is missing, the evidence for it, why it matters for MTD
    evaluation, and the scope of the claim.
12. Each property and limitation named here is taken up later, and the chapter 5
    baseline is shown to be representative.
13. Nothing built or chosen by the dissertation is presented as literature.
14. The newest work in each theme is current, and each claim was read in its source.
15. Quotations appear only where their exact words are the evidence. Every float
    carries a conclusion that the prose draws from it.
