# Census D — metrics in the MTD-evaluation papers

Read-only census, 2026-09-23. Sources: `docs/sources/lit_review/<file>.md` (authoritative source markdown) unless stated; tracked extracts under `docs/sources/extractions/` consulted for page anchors only.

**Locator convention.** `md l.N` = line N of the paper's source markdown under `docs/sources/lit_review/` (or `extractions/` for evans2011). Page numbers are given only where the markdown or a tracked extract pins them; otherwise the section + md line is the locator. Equations that the markdown renders as `picture ... intentionally omitted` are marked **[eq. image omitted in md]** — their formula text is not in the source markdown and is not reconstructed here.

**Column key.** paper | metric as written | acronym | definition / formula | perspective | family | unit | locator.

**Coverage and keys.** 14 passes, one paper each, in the order requested. Bib keys differ from file names in two cases: `4_outkin2023defender.md` is cited as **outkin2022** in the thesis bib (bib l.440–441), and `mandiant-apt1-report.md` is **mandiant2013** (bib l.694). he2025 and bland2020 and outkin2023 markdown carry no page numbers; masud2025 carries only running heads without numbers; for those, locators are section + md line and page is "unverified" unless a tracked extract pins it. alavizadeh2022, cremonini2005 and manadhatawing2011tse were read column-by-column from the PDFs (the markdown is an interleaved two-column extraction); sharma2025 and chobenasher2018 formulas are reassembled from interleaved text and flagged where that matters. Scratchpad column extracts: `alav.txt`, `crem2.txt`, `tse.txt`.

**What is not in this census.** Hyperparameters that are not evaluation quantities (e.g. he2025's mutation γ, bland2020's ε) are omitted; attack-model parameters are included only where they bear on tempo, probing, detection or effort, and are marked as parameters.

## (b) Detection, detectability, stealth, evasion, noise, tempo, rate, intensity, dwell, probe/scan counts (priority)

### he2025 — ADR and RI, verbatim

> "_Attack Metrics:_ Within our established threat model, an adversarial attack must possess two key characteristics: evasiveness and maliciousness. Therefore, we evaluate the performance of the attack based on two metrics: Adversarial Detection Rate (ADR) and Relative Intensity (RI)." — §V-B, md l.229

> "ADR quantifies the effectiveness of the _adversarial_ attack in evading detection. It measures the percentage of adversarial attacks that are classified as malicious. A lower ADR indicates a higher level of evasiveness. For instance, an ADR of 0.1 suggests that only 10% of the malicious traffic generated by the adversarial attack is correctly identified as malicious, while the remaining 90% remains undetected." — §V-B, md l.231

> "RI measures the degree of maliciousness of the attack for DoS attacks. It is the ratio of the average packets per second generated by the adversarial attack and the original attack. A lower RI indicates a decrease in the intensity of the adversarial traffic compared to the original attack. For example, an RI of 0.1 indicates that the adversarial traffic exhibits only 10% of the intensity present in the original attack." — §V-B, md l.233

> "The performance of the defense mechanism is evaluated using two key metrics: Adversarial Detection Rate (ADR) and Malicious Detection Rate (MDR). ADR has the same formulation described earlier, except for defense, ADR should be as high as possible." — §V-B, md l.237

Formal definitions are deferred to "Supplementary Material II" (md l.227), which is not in the source markdown. Page: the tracked extract pins §III-A to p.3 and §IV-D to p.6; §V-B lies between them (page not pinned — "unverified").

Further he2025 detection / intensity material:
- Success thresholds and their rationale (§VI-A, md l.261): "A low ADR threshold of 0.001 ensures that the number of packets detected as malicious remains inconspicuous to network operators." RI 0.12 "signifies that the device is flooded for approximately 12% of the attack time" and is analogous to "a 12% packet loss" (md l.263).
- Defence threshold (§VI-B, md l.299): "a successful defense requires an ADR above 0.1 ... an ADR of 0.1 corresponds to at least 100 malicious packets being reported every second, which would undoubtedly raise the alarm for the network operator."
- Evasion–intensity trade (§VI-D, md l.345): "ADR is linearly proportional to RI under a logarithmic scale, indicating that ADR grows polynomially with RI ... _ADR_ = _RI[k]_ ... if the attacker wants to increase the maliciousness of the adaptive attack against FM, the ADR of the attack has to increase polynomially, making the adaptive attack proportionally more detectable." Restated in §VIII (md l.375): "if the attacker intends to reduce the ADR of the adversarial attack, a substantial decrease in flooding intensity against MTD-AD is required."
- Mechanism of the intensity loss (§VI-A, md l.261): LM "introduces periodic delays to individual packets ... the original constant flooding attack is transformed into an intermittent flooding attack"; increasing the maximum delay "reduces the duration of the flooding period, resulting in a decrease in RI" (md l.275).
- RI is analytic, not measured on the wire: "the relative intensity is calculated analytically based on the interarrival times of packets" (§VII, md l.363).
- Tempo knobs: tm ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7} s max time delay, nc = 10 injected packets (md l.125–127); attack intensity "5,000 packets per second" (md l.207).
- MDR (defence reading on unmodified traffic), FPR (benign), anomaly score and threshold (Kitsune alarm), detection deadline "within 20 seconds" (md l.147).

### Other papers

- **outkin2023.** Ready-residence time is the attacker's ability "to remain hidden" (md l.33–41) — fraction of steady-state time in the completed-but-not-yet-acted state. Probability of detection per step from ATT&CK Evaluations = detecting vendors / 12, ceiling over sub-steps and over detection categories IOC / Specific Alert / General Alert (md l.511–525, Table 4). Probability of "succeeding undetected ... about 4%" (md l.485). **Attack intensity and response intensity are named but not defined**: "Markov chain representation allows calculating measures of attack intensity and estimating the response intensity as well" (md l.83; abstract md l.5). Tempo as evasion: "the attacker sends emails at certain intervals ... The emails spread over time to decrease the chance of being noticed by the IDS system of the target organization. We assume that the attacker sends just one email at any given time to avoid detection" (§4.2, md l.377). Detection latency is named as unmodelled: "The time required for information to percolate from logging to analysis can also vary widely between defenders" (md l.612).
- **chobenasher2018.** Pad = probability the attacker detects the deception (0.5); Pfn = IDS false-negative probability (0.05); IDS detection rate T_DET = (mark(AF) + mark(MTD) + 1)(1 − Pfn)/Td, i.e. detection grows with accumulated attack failures and with MTD-prolonged reconnaissance: "If MTD is executed, IDS can have more time to detect attackers based on their suspicious activities during their prolonged reconnaissance period, leading to higher attack detection rates" (p.153, md l.654–657). Pattern-vs-randomness evasion: "the attacker can hide from IDS by performing random attacks, rather than persistent attacks which tend to be easily detected by IDS due to their consistent patterns" (§1, p.148, md l.97–100). Dwell statistic (cited DBIR): "for about 75% of the attacks, it took more than seven days to discover them" (p.147, md l.37–38). No detection metric is reported as an outcome.
- **kim2026.** Scan budget: M = 4096 × Repeat addresses, Repeat = 5, out of N = 65,535 (Table 4; md l.386); attack arrivals ATK_λ = 0.2/min Poisson (md l.382). Evasion is narrated, not measured: outbound connection "attempted in order to evade the detection by a defense system such as a network intrusion detection system (NIDS) or a firewall" (md l.266); "install tools or crafted code in a hidden directory in order to avoid detection" (md l.274); delivery-phase attackers "masquerading as normal users, they should conceal their suspicious activities" (md l.147). ART_C2A (retention time) is the only post-compromise residence metric.
- **evans2011.** Probe counts are the x-axis of the incremental-attack model (L probes, re-randomise every m probes; 1024 probes expected without dynamic diversity; V §2.5.5, pp.40–43). Detectability is named qualitatively: probing "enable[s] an attacker to use smaller, possibly harder to detect, packets to probe the system" (V p.38); NOP-sled variants chosen "To avoid intrusion detection systems that alert on suspected NOP sleds" (V p.36); event-triggered re-randomisation "when an anomaly detector flags a packet as suspicious", with the risk that "a sophisticated attacker may be able to develop a probe attack that does not produce the trigger event" (V p.43).
- **sharma2025.** Only qualitative: expert attackers "employ stealthy or adaptive scanning techniques" (§3.2, p.4, md l.175–176); scanning with Nmap/Nessus (md l.162–163). No detection term enters the TTC model.
- **masud2025.** Claims MTD increases "chances of detection" (abstract, md l.21) and "can aid with the detection of possible attackers" (§3.6, md l.454); IPV read as "renders 40% of reconnaissance attempts ineffective" (md l.572). No detection metric defined.
- **bland2020.** Detection exists only as defender-controlled transitions ("Defender detects spidering", etc., rates notional, md l.549, Tables 2/4/6); no detection rate is reported.
- **alavizadeh2022.** Attack rate "exponential ... average value of 0.2 (i.e., one attack per every five hours)" (p.1778); ARO (events/year, assumed 1). No detection metric; intrusion-detection-triggered deployment is out of scope (p.1786).
- **mandiant2013.** Dwell: average 356 days, longest 1,764 days (pp.3, 21). Tempo: "APT1 was not continuously active on a daily basis" (p.21); backdoor "Sleep ... for a specified amount of time", e.g. "s:54" = sleep 54 minutes/hours (pp.32, 41). Blending: standard backdoors use "the HTTP protocol (to blend in with legitimate web traffic)" (p.32, md l.1139). Activity count: 1,905 observed intruder sessions over two years (p.4).
- **mendonca2023, cremonini2005, manadhatawing2011, manadhatawing2011tse**: no detection, stealth, tempo or probe-count metric. (cremonini names attacker tempo only as a response: an attacker with reduced margin "may be pushed to pay more (i.e. attacking more frequently, with more sophisticated techniques, or looking for different vulnerabilities)", §3 p.2.)

- **cremonini2005** — N intrusions as the proxy for GI/CA (hosts gained per unit attacker cost).
- **masud2025 / alavizadeh2022** — AC (attack cost) summed over attack paths: effort as a static CVSS-derived cost, not attempts.
- **manadhatawing2011** — effort r.ef (access rights to acquire) and der (damage potential per unit effort), per resource, not per compromise.
- **bland2020** — C1/C2 costs incurred per episode (one episode = one attack).
- **outkin2023** — attack campaign restarts: "If a successful attack campaign ... has more steps than the attack graph, it means that one or more of the attacks have failed, and the attacker had to restart" (md l.491) — implicit attempts in the first-passage distribution.
