# Census E: attacker-side detectability / stealth / tempo / effort metrics in the tactic-profile corpus

Read-only census, 2026-09-23. Scope: `docs/sources/tactic_profiles/` (120 md) and the ten listed
`docs/sources/extractions/*.md`. Method: I grepped the tree for detectab|stealth|noise|footprint|probe|scan rate|
intensity|dwell|time to detect|MTTD|deterrence|effort|attack cost|ratio (plus noisy|quiet|low-and-slow|
number of actions), then opened each hit and read the definition in the primary markdown.
Two sources outside the tree were checked only as cross-references because they are already in the bib and
bear on the shortlist: `docs/sources/lit_review/zaffarano2015.md` and `docs/sources/lit_review/3_2_he2025MTD-AD.md`.

Bib column: `yes-<key>` means an active entry in `docs/thesis/references.bib`. `stub-<key>` means a
commented-out (inactive) entry exists. `no` means no entry.
"Verbatim" text is quoted from the source markdown. Page numbers come from page markers in the markdown where
they exist; otherwise the page is marked "p. unverified".

### 1.28 Cross-reference outside the tree (already in the bib; checked because it bears on the shortlist)
- **Zaffarano, Taylor, Hamilton 2015**, *A Quantitative Framework for MTD Effectiveness Evaluation*, MTD'15. File: `docs/sources/lit_review/zaffarano2015.md`. Bib: **yes-zaffarano2015**.
  - **Attack Confidentiality**: "Attack Confidentiality is a measure of how much attacker activity may be visible by detection mechanisms" (Table 4, p. 8). The formal definition is Confidentiality(M,ν) = (1/|T|) Σ_{τ∈T} ν(τ, unexposed) (§4.3, p. 8–9). Its own operationalisation is "whether information is visible in plaintext in network traffic".
  - **Attack Productivity**: "a measure of how quickly an attacker can perform and complete adversarial tasks" (Table 4). Productivity(M,ν) = (1/|T|) Σ ν(τ, duration) (§4.1).
- **He, Kim, Asghar 2025**, *MTD-AD*, IEEE TDSC. File: `docs/sources/lit_review/3_2_he2025MTD-AD.md`, lines 229–233 (the section number is unverified). Bib: **yes-he2025**. ## 2. Ranked shortlist: conventional, citable names for "attacker A is less detectable / stealthier than attacker B"

Constraints: the simulator has no intrusion detector, and the attackers differ in (i) the number of actions on the network and (ii) the time between actions.

**1. Detectability ratio** (no acronym). Jafarian, Al-Shaer & Duan 2015, IEEE TIFS 10(12), §VII, p. 2570.
- **Definition:** C_X / C_ref, the number of scans (actions) the attacker generates relative to a reference.
- **Fidelity: high for (i), none for (ii).** It is the only published MTD metric *named* "detectability" that is computed from action counts with no detector in the loop. The paper's justification is that more actions mean more connection failures, which makes the attack more detectable.
- **Caveat:** Jafarian defines it for one attacker under MTD vs a static network. Using it attacker-vs-baseline-attacker is an adaptation that must be declared, and it is blind to tempo. It is not in the bib yet (no stub either).

**2. Attack confidentiality** (no acronym). Zaffarano, Taylor & Hamilton 2015, MTD'15, Table 4 and §4.3. **In bib as zaffarano2015.**
- **Definition:** "how much attacker activity may be visible by detection mechanisms", computed as the fraction of attacker tasks that are unexposed.
- **Fidelity: high in meaning, medium in mechanics.** It is the MTD-evaluation framework's own name for attacker stealth, and it is defined without a particular detector. The exposure valuation ν(τ, unexposed) must be supplied.
- **Caveat:** Zaffarano's exposure rule is plaintext visibility. To use the metric here, the thesis must declare its own exposure rule, for example a count-in-window rule after Durumeric §3.1 or Snort `count 100, seconds 5` as in Wang & Wu 2016. The result is then a declared proxy, not a measured detection.

**3. Scanning rate** (η or s), generalised to an action rate. Chen, Gao & Kwiat 2003 §II-A Table I ("the average number of machines scanned by an infected machine per unit time"). The tempo-detectability link is Jafarian 2015 §VII ("fast scanning is easy to detect", citing Stafford & Li 2010) and Zhang et al. 2023 ID-HAM §III-A.
- **Fidelity: high for (i)+(ii) combined.** It is the field's standard tempo quantity, and the "low rate = stealthy" convention is stated in two MTD papers.
- **Caveat:** it is a scan-specific, worm-era quantity. A rate averaged over the whole horizon hides bursts (the metrics brief already flags this), so it should be computed per window. Sellke §4 also shows that a rate rule is exactly what a slow attacker evades.

**4. Probability of implant detection** (p, compounded as (1−p)^i over i actions). Anderson, Mitchell & Chen 2016, IFIP NTMS, §III-A Eq. (1) and Table I.
- **Fidelity: medium-high for (i).** It turns an action count into a detection probability with one declared, sweepable per-action parameter. The paper states the mechanism directly: fewer implants give "less opportunities for an attack-ending detection".
- **Caveat:** p is a declared parameter, not measured. The model is blind to tempo (ii) and is not in the bib.

**5. Total number of scans over a containment cycle** (M). Sellke, Shroff & Bagchi 2008, IEEE TDSC, §1 and §4.
- **Fidelity: medium.** It is the cleanest published statement that action count (i) and action rate (ii) are *separate* detection surfaces. A rate limit misses "slow scanning worms [...] and stealth worms", while a count limit M catches every attacker, the slow ones later.
- **Caveat:** M is a containment threshold, not a detectability score. It licenses reporting count and tempo as two axes, not a single stealth number. It is not in the bib.

**Considered and not recommended.**
- **Minimum detection probability** (Venkatesan 2016): it needs detector nodes.
- **Relative intensity** (He 2025): in its source RI is the maliciousness metric, and evasiveness there is ADR, which needs a detector. If RI is kept, it should be introduced as an intensity measure "adapted from" He, with the evasion reading carried by a separate citation (for example Jafarian's or Zhang's "fast scanning is easy to detect") rather than attributed to He.
- **Deterrence ratio** (Jafarian 2015) and **expected number of probes** (Carroll 2014): these measure delay and effort, not detectability.
- **Average move rate α** (FlipIt): in FlipIt, moves are stealthy by construction.
- **Dwell time / MTTD:** defender-side, and they need a detection event. Tripathi's "MTTD" means mean time to *disrupt*.
- **Zhuang:** no primary is held.
