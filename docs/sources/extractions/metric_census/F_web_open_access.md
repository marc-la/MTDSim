# Census F: conventional metric names for stealth and the six outcome quantities (web search)

Date 2026-09-23. Scope: the peer-reviewed literature on security metrics, MTD, graphical security models and HARM, APT, and attack simulation, plus the few industry names that the field quotes. Nothing in the repo was edited.

**Read-status key.** **READ** means I opened the full text (PDF extracted with pypdf) and quote from it. **ABSTRACT** means I read the abstract only, through OpenAlex. **SECONDARY** means I know it only through another paper I did read, and that paper is named. Anything not marked READ is flagged "not read" in the table.

**Why several sources were not read.** ACM DL, IEEE Xplore, ResearchGate, UWA, Deakin, KTH DiVA and UNC Charlotte either returned HTTP 403 or put up a bot wall. Some of those pages are open access to a browser even though I could not fetch them. Section 4 marks which.

**What the census found on stealth.**
- **No source I read defines a detector-free stealth metric under that name.** The field names the *quantity* the thesis computes, events per unit time and the gap between events: attack rate and attack inter-arrival time (Zhan et al. 2013, surveyed by Pendleton et al. 2016). It frames that quantity as *aggressiveness*. Calling it stealth is an interpretation. That interpretation needs the low-and-slow argument (Alshamrani 2019, already in the repo) and He's finding that evasion came from periodic delays.
- **He's RI points the other way.** He reads a *low* RI as a *weaker* (less malicious) attack, and measures evasiveness separately with ADR, which needs a detector. Adopting "intensity" as the stealth metric keeps He's form (a ratio to a reference attack) but reverses his reading. That reversal must be stated.
- **Jafarian 2014 may give the one MTD-native name, "detectability".** The abstract links it to the re-scans the MTD forces, which is exactly "more network actions, more detectable". The definition is paywalled and unconfirmed. It is the first paper to fetch.
- **The Hong lineage has no stealth metric** in any paper I read (see §3). The only detection-related metric there is "probability of attack detection" (Roy, Kim & Trivedi 2012, via Enoch et al.), and it depends on a detector.
- **On measurement versus attribution**, every detector-free option measures activity or exposure, not detection. The metric can license "A takes fewer network actions per unit active time than B". The step to "A is less detectable" rests on the cited rationale, not on the measurement.

## 4. Ranked recommendations

### (a) Stealth / detectability: ranked shortlist
1. **Attack rate** (number of attacker actions per unit time), reported alongside the **attack inter-arrival time**, the mean gap between consecutive actions. Sources: Zhan, Xu & Xu 2013 (TIFS, OA, read) for the definitions; Pendleton et al. 2016 (CSUR) for the survey status and the "aggressiveness" reading. Of the options found, this is the most conventional name for exactly what the thesis computes, and it needs no detector. It still owes one motivating sentence for the stealth reading (low-and-slow, Alshamrani 2019).
2. **Relative intensity** (He et al. 2025), used as the *normalised* form: attack rate divided by the baseline attacker's. It suits the phase-one comparison and is already cited in Table 3.1. The caveat above must be stated in the text: He reads RI as maliciousness and measures evasion with a detector-based ADR. The current handoff name "attack intensity, adapted from He" is defensible on those terms. "Attack rate relative to the baseline" is more conventional and does not reverse He's reading.
3. **Detectability** (Jafarian et al. 2014). This is the MTD-native name and fits the forced-re-scan mechanism. It is conditional on the definition, which I could not read. If the definition is a count of probes or scans, it becomes the strongest option.
4. **Average move rate** (FlipIt, van Dijk et al. 2013). Supporting citation only, for an APT-framed stealthy attacker whose tempo is measured as moves per unit time. It is not a stealth measure itself.
- **Rejected for this use because they need a detector:** dwell time, MTTID/MTTD, detection time, ADR, Venkatesan's detection probability, and probably Zaffarano's attack confidentiality. Dwell time is still citable as the reason stealth matters.

### (b) Conventional names for the other six quantities (best first)
1. **Success rate over runs:** attack success probability, ASP (Cho et al. 2020), estimated as the fraction of runs in which the goal is reached. Second choice: "attack success rate", after Zhuang et al.'s completed attacks out of N runs. Keep it separate from the per-attempt "individual attack success ratio".
2. **Time to first compromise:** time to compromise, TTC (Holm 2014; the MTTC lineage of Leversage & Byres 2008), reported as its mean over runs, MTTC, with "first host" stated. The survey term is "time-to-first-compromise" (Pendleton), which in Holm is tied to a detection alarm. Breakout time is an industry cousin.
3. **Proportion of hosts compromised:** Compromised Host Percentage, CHP (Cheng et al. 2014: realised, "at time t"). Next, Network Compromise Percentage, NCP (Lippmann 2006, used by Hong's group, but computed on an attack graph as potential compromise). Then "fraction of compromised computers" (Pendleton). I found no source for the exact name "network compromise ratio / NCR". Rename to CHP or NCP, or state that the ratio form is your own.
4. **Actions per compromise:** attack effort, after effort-to-security-failure (Dacier and Ortalo via Pendleton; the METF acronym is unverified), or attack cost as the MTD-survey efficiency metric (Cho 2020, e.g. scanning overhead). The count-based form has no established acronym and must be defined in the methodology.
5. **Share of actions that fail:** the complement of the per-attempt success ratio (Zhuang 2012; Ho's per-attempt ASR in the lineage), named as an attack failure rate. Pendleton's **blocking rate** is the defender-side name, but it is a difference of population rates, not a per-action share.
6. **Attacker recovery time:** no conventional attacker-side name exists. Define it in the methodology using the MTTR form ("mean time to recovery", Jaquith 2007 via Enoch et al. 2017; MTIR in CIS 2010), and cite Jafarian 2014/2015 for the mechanism (MTD invalidates reconnaissance and forces re-scanning).
