# The MTD metric catalogue — what the field names, and what this thesis must define

**Why this exists.** Register E3 (22 Sep 2026): every metric the thesis reports is cited, or defined in the methodology with why it is needed. Marc's bar (23 Sep): *"look me in the eye — this exists, or this doesn't and we are inventing it for this reason"*, with the field's names, and its acronyms where it has them. This record is the lookup that answers that for each quantity. The chapter-level dispositions it feeds are in the metrics handoff (`docs/handoffs/2026-09-22_metrics_provenance_and_instrumentation.md`).

**How it was built.** Six census passes, one paper per pass so no metric is attributed across papers, about 1 100 metric rows in all, each with the metric as written, its acronym, definition or formula (verbatim where short), perspective, family, unit and locator. ## (a) The verdicts — each quantity chapter 5 reports

*Exists* means a paper in the census names and defines this quantity (or its faithful abstraction). *Does not exist* means no paper in the census has it, and the thesis defines it in §4.5 with the stated reason.

| Quantity (current float name) | Verdict | Field name (acronym) | Source, with locator | What differs in this thesis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Share of runs that compromise a database host (*target reached*) | **Exists** | attack success probability (**ASP**) | Cho 2020 §VII-A (the dominant effectiveness metric; "the probability that attacks are successfully performed", e.g. a target found); Zaffarano 2015 *Success* (Table 4, §4.2 p.9); estimated over runs as Zhuang 2012 does ("completed attacks out of 1000", F) | an estimate over runs at the targeted objective. **Not "ASR" after Ho**: Ho's ASR (Eq. 11, p.20) is hosts compromised per attempted action, a per-attempt rate (B); Tay names ASR with no definition (B). No paper defines a per-run *rate* under that name |
| Distinct hosts compromised by the end of a run (*hosts reached*) | **Exists** | network compromise ratio (**NCR**); host compromise ratio (**HCR**) | Zhang 2023 §5 p.32 (NCR, used there as the 0.8 stopping checkpoint); Ho 2024 Eq. 10 p.19 (HCR = C_t / T_host, the same ratio); outside the lineage, Cheng 2014 *compromised host percentage* (**CHP**, "percentage of compromised hosts in a network at time t", F) | read at the end of the run rather than used as a stopping rule. | Attacker actions per unit of its active time | **Exists** | attack rate; attack inter-arrival time | Zhan, Xu & Xu 2013 §III (TIFS; "the number of attacks that arrive at unit time … the time intervals between two consecutive attack events", F, read); Pendleton 2016 §5.1 (surveyed as metrics of the *aggressiveness* of attacks, F); Kim 2026 attack rate ATK_λ (an input, D) | an attacker's own actions rather than attacks arriving at a honeypot. | Share of runs leaving the commonest opening (Figure 5.1(b)) | **Does not exist** | — | as above | defined in §4.5; needed because strategic plurality is claimed, and the baseline attacker is one script |

**Near misses, and why each was not taken**

- **Relative intensity (RI)**, He 2025 §V-B: named, acronymed, in an MTD paper, but He's *maliciousness* metric; his evasiveness metric is ADR, which needs a detector. Using RI as a stealth score reverses his reading. (This corrects the 2026-09-23 first pass of the metrics brief, commit f6c4a493.)
- **Detectability ratio**, Jafarian 2015 §VII p.2570 (C_RHM / C_static, the scans the attacker is forced to generate): the only published metric *named* detectability that needs no detector, but it compares one attacker with and without an MTD, not two attackers. Not in `references.bib`. Supporting citation for why action count and rate are a detection proxy.
- **Attack productivity**, Zaffarano 2015 §4.1 (mean attacker-task duration): the field's tempo metric, but duration per task, not the time between tasks; the declared dwell times would make it a restatement of chapter 4's inputs.
- **ADR** (He), **minimum detection probability** (Venkatesan 2016), **probability of attack detection** (Enoch/Hong 2017), **per-step detection probability** (Outkin 2022), **MTTD / dwell time / time-to-first-compromise in Holm's sense**: all need a detector or a detection event. The simulator has none (axis 5 NOT ADDRESSED).
- **Ready-residence time**, Outkin 2022: the attacker's ability "to remain hidden" after success, as a Markov steady-state share; needs the detection model it is built on.
- **ACD**, Hong 2018 §5.1.5 p.40: its rationale runs the *other* way — "the longer the attack takes, the more likely it will be detected" (a time-at-risk detector). The thesis's rate detector must be declared as a choice, and this stated beside it.
- **Attempts required to compromise**, Brown 2023 Fig. 5: exists, but no surviving float reports effort per host.
- **Hong 2018's APV, APN, APE, ACE, ACD**: attacker-side by framing, but computed analytically from a sequence of network states, not from a running attacker.

## (b) What the census shows about the field

1. **Detection is measured from the defender's side, with a detector.** No paper in the census has a detector-free stealth metric computed on the attacker's own behaviour. Zaffarano's attack confidentiality is the one attacker-side detectability metric the field relays (Cho, Sengupta), and it needs an exposure rule.
2. **One name, many quantities.** MTTC (five meanings, B), ASR/ASP (per attempt in Ho, per campaign in Cho, per phase in Kim), return on attack (three formulas: Cremonini, Alavizadeh, Masud, D), AC (attack cost; access complexity in CVSS). The thesis states its definition at every cited name.
3. **Reach is rarely an output.** No paper in D reports the fraction of hosts compromised per run; in the lineage NCR is a stopping rule. Cheng's CHP is the realised-compromise precedent outside it.
4. **No field metric for attacker behavioural variety, disengagement or recovery.** Those the thesis defines, or does not report.
