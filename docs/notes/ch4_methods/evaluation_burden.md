# The evaluation's burden of proof — ranking stability and ranking divergence

## Position in the dissertation

The evaluation chapter's organising requirement: the two results the experiments must produce for the thesis's central claim to stand, and the disposition if they do not. The methodology chapter promises this test; this note specifies it.

## The idea

The thesis's central claim is that behavioural fidelity in the attacker model *changes the answer* an MTD evaluation returns — that the ranking of defence mechanisms produced against a slow, objective-driven, behaviourally-grounded attacker differs from the ranking produced against the fast procedural attacker the simulator inherits. Two objections, each strong enough to fail the claim at examination, follow directly from how the profiled attacker was built, and both are answered by the same pair of experimental results.

**The first objection: the novel behaviour rests on invented parameters.** The profiled attacker differs from the procedural baseline through four families of introduced parameters, not one of them measured. The per-tactic durations set how long each stage of a campaign occupies the attacker. The per-tactic reset fractions set how much of a tactic's progress a single defensive mutation destroys; they are argued tactic by tactic from mechanism, though the coupled runs do not yet exercise them. The third family is the largest and was added after this note was first written: a declared likelihood, for every ordered pair of tactics and each of the two outcomes an action can return, that the second tactic is the attacker's next move. That family is what actually routes the attacker after every action, and at over two hundred pairs it carries by far the most degrees of freedom. The fourth is not numeric but is a choice all the same — which simulator action each tactic dispatches, a mapping with no ground truth to recover, only alternatives to compare. Strip all four and nothing separates the profiled attacker from the generic one. The defence — that declare-and-sweep is the established practice in timed attack modelling, and that the bands are mechanism-bounded rather than free — is genuine but incomplete until the sweep is actually run: a declared parameter is defensible only while the conclusion is shown not to hinge on where in its band the value sits.

Project supervision has since made that requirement concrete for the routing weights in particular. The objection raised was specific and is a fair one: because the values as first authored grade a transition by direction rather than by distance, an attacker can leap the length of the campaign lifecycle almost as readily as it steps to the adjacent phase. The directed remedy is to ground the weighting in the consensus of published attack-lifecycle models — so that near transitions are favoured and distant ones fall to near zero — and then to sweep the result. That remedy and that sweep have now been carried out for this one family, which makes it the pilot for the discipline the rest of this note demands, and the first place where the discipline can be seen paying its way rather than being promised. What it returned is set out below, and it is not uniformly good news.

**The second objection: "fidelity changes the answer" is indistinguishable from parameter noise.** A ranking difference between the two attackers means nothing if the profiled attacker's own ranking wobbles across its uncertainty bands — any observed divergence could then be an artefact of where the declared values were pointed, not of behavioural fidelity. The claim is only evidenced when the divergence is *larger than* the profiled attacker's own sensitivity.

Both objections therefore reduce to one two-part burden on the evaluation:

1. **Stability.** The MTD ranking produced by the profiled attacker must be stable across the declared sweep bands — the same ordering (or an ordering whose changes are characterised and bounded) at the extremes of every swept duration, reset fraction, and routing weight.
2. **Divergence.** That stable ranking must differ from the (also stable) ranking produced by the procedural baseline attacker under identical conditions.

Stability without divergence means behavioural fidelity was not worth modelling — the generic attacker was a sufficient evaluation instrument. Divergence without stability means the result is an artefact of declared parameters. Only the conjunction supports the thesis's claim; the experiments are designed to test the conjunction directly, not either half alone.

**The negative result is a result.** If the conjunction fails, the honest disposition is not to soften the claim but to report the failure as a finding about the limits of CTI-derived behavioural grounding on a corpus of this size: the profiles constitute a defended design whose behavioural distinctions do not survive their own uncertainty, and the contribution retreats to the construction method and the negative demonstration. This disposition is stated in advance — before the experiments run — precisely so the result cannot be quietly reframed after the fact.

Until these experiments run, every statement of the central claim in earlier chapters is a *defended design*, not a demonstrated result, and is worded as such.

## The first instalment, and the two things it settled

The stability half has now been run for one declared family — the routing weights — and it settles two questions and unsettles two others. Four conclusions were nominated, each with its criterion fixed before any parameter was moved, and the weights were then swept across the ranges their derivation declares: singly, and at the corners of the two that proved most influential.

The two that survived are the ones the evaluation's headline actually rests on. No run reached the operational objective at any parameter setting, under either defensive condition, on either of the two action mappings tried — a result stable across every corner of the swept space. Nor did the defence change that verdict anywhere in the space. The second point deserves the emphasis, because it is the shape of finding this half of the burden was designed to produce: the defensive effect that *is* visible under the finer action mapping — a suppression of host compromise by roughly nine parts in ten — is present at every parameter setting, and so cannot be an artefact of where the declared values were pointed. A stable effect is not yet a demonstrated one, and ranking defensive mechanisms remains outside what a single-scheme contrast can support; what has been shown is only that the effect does not move with the weights.

The two that failed are finer, and one fails for a reason the weights do not control. The classification of the single profile that sits *between* the two failure modes turns on where in its range one decay parameter is placed — its blocked-action fraction spans a quarter to nearly two thirds across the swept space, crossing the threshold that assigns it a mode. The profiles at the extremes are assigned robustly; the intermediate case is not, so no finding may be reported about which mode that profile is in. More consequentially, no ranking of profiles by how far they progress survives the sweep on any measure that varies — and the diagnosis is a shortage of runs rather than an excess of parameter freedom. At ten runs per cell, adjacent profiles' confidence intervals overlap almost everywhere, so the orderings that change are changing between quantities the data cannot separate. The measure that ought to have carried the claim, depth reached into the campaign lifecycle, turns out to be saturated: every profile reaches the final stage of the lifecycle in its own campaign structure and then fails against the simulated network, not against its own model of the campaign. That is a genuine finding about where the profiled attacker breaks down, and it is also a measurement gap, since a saturated measure cannot discriminate.

Two consequences for what the evaluation may claim, both binding. First, the evaluation may not report a per-profile ordering by progress until it is run at a sample size that separates the profiles — the stability half is *not* discharged for that claim, and the ordering language must be dropped rather than hedged. Second, one of the three declared parameters in the weighting moved nothing at all, because the analyst-drawn campaigns contain no transition long enough for it to act upon. This reframes the objection that prompted the work: the defect was real in the declared values and was never exercised by the evidence base, so the correction is a defensible-parameter result rather than a behavioural one, and must be reported as the former.

What this instalment does not touch is the rest of the burden. One of four declared families has been swept; the durations, the reset fractions and the choice of action mapping have not. The divergence half — whether the profiled attacker's ranking of defences differs from the procedural attacker's — remains untested, because no ranking of defences has yet been produced by either attacker.

## The second instalment — the durations, and the boundary it found

The durations family has since been swept on the same discipline, and it returns a cleaner verdict than the weights did on every question except one, which it turns out nobody was asking. As before, the conclusions and the criteria that would settle them were fixed and committed before any value moved. The campaign stages were then re-timed across the ranges their own justifications declare — not fifteen independently, which would be unidentifiable, but through the four timing groups the durations are organised into, so that each stage keeps its position relative to its neighbours while the group as a whole speeds up or slows down.

Two conclusions hold across the declared ranges, and they are the two the evaluation's tempo argument rests on. The profiled attacker is slower to its first compromise than the procedural one at every setting tried — never once faster, at any group timing, any defensive tempo, or either distribution shape — and the defence never helps the attacker anywhere in the space, on either attacker. Neither result is close to its boundary, which is worth saying plainly: these are not marginal survivals but findings with a wide margin, and the timing values could be wrong by a factor of four in either direction without disturbing them.

The sweep also produced an identifiability result that was not among the questions asked, and it is the more interesting half. Of the four timing groups, only one moves any outcome at all — the group covering the slow, quiet stages of a campaign, whose durations nobody publishes and which the catalogue accordingly badges as its weakest and gives its widest range. The two groups the simulator itself prices, by contrast, change nothing measurable at either end of their ranges. The model's entire timing sensitivity is therefore concentrated in exactly the values whose provenance is most honestly declared as an estimate, and the values inherited from the simulator carry none of it. That the validity tiers predicted this ordering without being consulted is the closest thing to independent corroboration the tiering has yet received.

The question the design record most wanted answered was whether the *shape* of the timing distribution matters or only its average, and the answer has a boundary in it. The stages in question are paced and deliberate by character, which is the opposite of what the chosen distribution describes — its single most likely outcome is a near-instant one — so the concern was real rather than pedantic. Substituting a distribution with the same average but its mass concentrated around that average changes nothing measurable across the region the evaluation operates in: not compromise breadth, not elapsed time, not success rate. At one corner it does change something — where those slow stages are set to the top of their declared range *and* the defence is mutating — and there the concentrated shape costs the attacker roughly two-thirds of what little breadth it had, consistently across profiles and seeds.

The direction of that effect is the instructive part, and it was predicted before it was found. A long stage is likelier to be cut short by a mutation than a short one, so shape re-enters through interruption even where the arithmetic excludes it. What the corner exposes is *which* feature of the chosen distribution the attacker has been living on: its most probable outcome is a near-instant stage, and that mass of very short stages is precisely what lets the attacker occasionally complete an action in the gap between two mutations. Concentrate the same average and that mass disappears, so nearly every stage runs long enough to be interrupted. The more behaviourally faithful shape is therefore *worse* for the attacker — the least realistic feature of the modelling choice has been quietly working in the attacker's favour. The average is what carries across the operating region, as argued, and the distribution family becomes a live parameter at long stage times under defensive pressure. That is a narrower claim than the sweep set out to test and a better-founded one than assuming either answer.

That scarcity is itself the instalment's most consequential finding, and it is a negative one about the evaluation's design rather than about its parameters. Sweeping the defensive tempo alongside the attacker's revealed that at the mutation interval every run of this project has used, *neither* attacker can complete its objective — not the profiled one, which was already known, but not the procedural baseline either, which was not. The objective only becomes reachable again when mutations are spaced roughly eight times further apart. The evaluation has therefore been operating inside a degenerate region, in the precise sense the analytic literature on this contest names: when the defence moves faster than the attacker completes, success rate is pinned at zero and can no longer distinguish anything, including the things the evaluation exists to distinguish. Compromise breadth and elapsed time remain informative throughout, which is what saves the two conclusions above; success rate does not, and any result reported on it at the current tempo would be reporting the region rather than the attacker. Choosing the operating tempo is a free experimental variable by supervisory ruling, so this is a fixable design fault — but it must be fixed deliberately in the comparative experiment rather than inherited, and no defence ranking taken inside the degenerate region can mean anything.

One conclusion failed, and it failed identically to the way it failed in the first instalment: no ordering of the profiles by how far they progress survives, because at ten runs per cell adjacent profiles cannot be told apart. Two independent sweeps, over two unrelated parameter families, reaching the same verdict by different routes is stronger evidence than either alone, and it converts a suspicion into a settled constraint. The binding consequence stated in the first instalment is unchanged and now doubly grounded: the evaluation may not claim a per-profile ordering until it is run at a sample size that separates the profiles. What the timing sweep adds is the diagnosis's confirmation — the orderings do not move because the parameters are free, they move because the measurements overlap.

One methodological note and one honest boundary. The sweep was run twice, because the rule governing where the attacker's time comes from was revised the day it first ran: supervision ruled that the attacker's behavioural model supplies *every* unit of its time rather than adding to the simulator's own action costs. Repeating the whole sweep against the revised rule reproduced every conclusion above — including the degenerate region, which is a check rather than a coincidence, since the procedural baseline consumes none of these values and could not have moved. It also produced the one finding the first run had missed: the shape effect at the corner. That is worth recording as a lesson about the earlier arrangement rather than a mere correction, because the reason the first run missed it is that a fixed simulator-side cost on every action was damping the declared timing's leverage. Removing the damping did not merely rescale the model; it made the declared values consequential enough for their distribution to matter at the extreme. The boundary: the procedural baseline's own behaviour has moved since the first coupled experiment published its figures, following a round of defect repairs and a deliberate re-baselining, so this instalment's comparisons are against the current simulator rather than against those published numbers.

## Revisit conditions

- When the remaining two declared families are swept — the reset fractions and the choice of action mapping — the instalment sections extend rather than being rewritten, since each is worded as one part of a four-part discharge.
- If the slow-and-quiet stage timings are ever refined toward a more faithful distribution rather than a more tractable one: the corner effect above becomes the governing evidence, and the refinement must be argued at long stage times under defensive pressure, since that is the only region where the choice was shown to matter.
- When the comparative experiment fixes its mutation tempo: if it is set inside the degenerate region the second instalment identifies, success rate must be dropped from its reported measures rather than reported at zero, and the choice of tempo must be justified in the experiment's design rather than inherited from earlier runs.
- When the discrimination experiment runs: this note is rewritten around the observed result (positive or negative), and the "defended design" wording in earlier chapters is upgraded or the negative-result disposition applied.
- When the progress ordering is re-run at a sample size that separates the profiles: the second of the two failures above is re-decided, and the present verdict on it is a statement about power rather than a permanent one.
- If the sweep bands themselves change (a profile's mechanism argument is revised), the stability half of the burden must be re-run against the new bands.
- If the evidence base gains a campaign transition spanning the whole lifecycle, the weighting parameter currently found inert becomes live, and the reframing above no longer holds.
