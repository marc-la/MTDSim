# G3: survey and gap claims that say more than the source

**Applied 2026-10-17 (Marc, items 1-8 as proposed).**

**The rule.** The claim keeps its job in the argument. The words narrow to what the source says, or the citation moves to the sentence the source does support. Where the step is our own reasoning, the citation goes. Chapters 1, 3 and 4 are proposals; Chapter 6 is applied after a ruling. Evidence was re-read in the source texts on 2026-10-17.

**Considered, no change.**

- **"Two surveys" for Cho and Jalowski** (§3.3, §3.4, §6.5). Jalowski calls itself a gap analysis, but its Section 3 reviews the state of the art. "Survey" is fair, and a new term in three places would cost more than it saves.
- **U0016's "on a schedule … even if never detected".** This is true of proactive MTD, the kind the dissertation evaluates.

**Net change:** two citations added, three moved or dropped, and about seven words changed or cut (items 2, 5 and 7).
